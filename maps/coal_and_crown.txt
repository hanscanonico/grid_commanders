# Factories in the north against crown lands in the south: same income, different means.
# grouping 1+2v3+4
# The second board authored for a grouping rather than dealt one, and the first
# 2v2 that is deliberately not level seat by seat. Seats 1 (NW) and 2 (NE) are the
# industry: three bases and two cities each. Seats 3 (SE) and 4 (SW) are the
# territory: one base and four cities each. Six properties a seat either way, so
# the sides open on twelve apiece and the same income -- what differs is what that
# income can be turned into and how fast. That is what the tag claims and what the
# parity lint checks: allied seats match kind for kind, and neither side out-owns
# the other's total (asymmetric-board plan D3). It is a claim, never an
# instruction -- the board plays whatever grouping a launch names, the
# free-for-all included.
# The compensation for the missing factories is three things, and all three are
# board state (D1): the southern half is broken ground -- woods, two mountain
# shoulders per seat -- against the north's open plains, so the territory holds
# what it takes; its cities sit forward while the industry's third base sits in
# the rear; and each southern seat opens with a mech the northern seats do not
# have. Nothing here is a multiplier or a per-seat setting.
# The board mirrors left to right about x 11/12, so seat 1 and seat 2 are exact
# reflections and so are seats 3 and 4 -- which is what makes "allied seats open
# identical" true by construction rather than by counting. There is no half-turn
# and no `# symmetric` tag: that lint is a duel instrument, and the north and
# south halves are not meant to match.
# The middle two rows are the coalfield, and they are the one part of the board
# that is even: four neutral bases and eight neutral cities, laid out the same
# north of the line as south of it. Two lateral roads and two north-south
# highways at x 4 and x 19 are the whole road net, so a side reinforces along its
# own rear and crosses on one of two lanes.
# Land only, like every multi-army board: the computer cannot plan a ferry, so
# every army has to be able to march on every other in any grouping.
# Symbols: . plains  F woods  M mountain  = road  C city  B base  Q HQ

# A wood with two roads through it: under fog, everything not on them is a guess.
# symmetric
# The board fog was drawn for. `data/terrain/woods.tres` conceals, so a unit off
# the road is invisible past an adjacent tile and an ambush on the move is the
# ordinary way a march ends here -- which is what makes the recon's five tiles of
# sight worth its 4000 rather than a luxury, and what makes a tank that drives
# into the trees alone a tank nobody sees again. It plays perfectly well with fog
# off; it is simply a different, quieter board then.
# Two roads and no third: one runs north-west to south-east, the other north-east
# to south-west, and they cross at the four road cells in the middle of row 6.
# Woods cost treads 2 and tires 3 against a road's 1, so the roads are the only
# ground armour can cover a board's width on, and the crossroads is the only
# ground both of them touch. Mirrors on (x, y) -> (17 - x, 12 - y).
# Fourteen properties on 234 cells, deliberately few: two bases a side and six
# neutral cities -- two on the crossroads' own corners, two beside a road, and
# two buried in the trees where nothing on tires gets there quickly. Do not thin
# the woods to make the middle readable: the unreadable middle is the board.
# Land only: a wood is a barrier wings and hulls would simply erase.
# Symbols: . plains  F woods  M mountain  = road  C city  B base  Q HQ

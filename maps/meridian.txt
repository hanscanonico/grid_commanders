# Forty cells long and eight deep: there is no flank on the ground, only the air.
# symmetric
# The corridor. Mountain walls the top and bottom rows for twenty-eight of the
# forty columns, so the fight happens in a six-row channel with a home at each
# end and no way round either -- `data/terrain/mountain.tres` moves foot, boot
# and air only, so a rifleman can work the shoulder and nothing on wheels or
# treads ever will. What decides a corridor is reach and rotation: artillery
# (2-3) and rockets (3-5) fire down the length of it from behind a screen, and
# the screen has to be relieved before it breaks, because there is nowhere to
# withdraw to except backwards. The one flank that exists is overhead, which is
# what the airfield behind each home is for.
# Two parallel highways run the length of the channel at y 2 and y 5 -- one
# rotation of the other -- and the four rows between and around them are the
# ground a unit rotates through. Eight neutral cities are strung along the
# corridor in mirrored pairs, so every one of them is exactly as far from its
# owner's home as its twin is from the other's.
# Mirrors on (x, y) -> (39 - x, 7 - y). 320 cells, a tenth of Bulwark's 1568,
# so the menu's backdrop is unaffected: this board is long, not large.
# Do not open the mountain walls. A gap anywhere along them is a flank, and the
# board's entire question is what an army does when it has not got one.

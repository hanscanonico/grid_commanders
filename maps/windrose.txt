# Four airfields around a fifth: whoever holds the centre owns the sky.
# Seats sit at the corners in reading order -- 1 NW, 2 NE, 3 SE, 4 SW -- so the
# opposite pairs 1&3 and 2&4 duel across the long diagonal. The board is laid out
# under a 90-degree rotation about the centre cell, which is what makes that duel
# fair by construction; no `# symmetric` tag, because that one asserts the
# 180-degree mirror a two-army board is held to.
# Each army opens on an HQ, a base, its own airfield and two cities. Everything
# else is neutral: two flank cities per quadrant, a ring of four around the
# middle, and the fifth airfield on the centre cell -- the prize the board turns
# on, since it is the only pad short of an army's own corner, so anything with
# wings either holds the middle or flies home to refuel.
# A mountain ridge runs down each arm of the cross, leaving every neighbouring
# pair one pass out by the rim: armour meets a neighbour at that chokepoint or
# goes the long way round through the open middle. Nothing walls the sky -- a
# t-copter crosses the diagonal in a handful of turns while a tank spends the
# match on it.
# Land and air only -- no water, because the computer cannot plan a ferry and
# every army here has to be able to walk to every other.
# Symbols: . plains  F woods  M mountain  C city  B base  Q HQ  A airport

# A fen across the whole middle, one causeway over it: the mechs wade, the tanks queue.
# symmetric
# Riverline's two bridges promoted to a front. `data/terrain/river.tres` moves
# `foot` at 2 and `boot` at 1 and nothing else, so the fen is three rows deep
# wall to wall -- five at both flanks, where it bulges into the bank -- and the
# only ground a vehicle crosses is the two-cell causeway at x 10-11. A mech (2
# move, 1 a step) wades the middle in two turns and the flanks in three; infantry
# (3 move, 2 a step) takes three and five. Armour has no answer but the causeway,
# and artillery has no need of one: it ranges 2-3, which is the fen's own depth,
# so a gun parked on either bank covers the far bridgehead without ever crossing.
# Mirrors on (x, y) -> (21 - x, 14 - y).
# The two islets are the fen's only cover -- a plains spur off each bank at (14, 6)
# and (7, 8), and a wooded hummock mid-stream at (5, 7) and (16, 7) that only
# boots and rifles will ever stand on. A lateral road runs the length of each bank
# so reserves shift between the causeway and the flanks faster than an assault
# crosses, which is what stops the board being a single scrum at x 10.
# Six neutral cities, three either side of the fen: the economy is fought for at
# home while the fen is fought over, and neither side can win one and skip the
# other. Do not widen the causeway. Two cells of frontage across 22 is the whole
# design, and a third lane turns the fen into scenery.
# Land only -- there is no sea here, and a river is not one: nothing sails a fen.
# Symbols: . plains  F woods  M mountain  = road  ~ river  + bridge
#          C city  B base  Q HQ

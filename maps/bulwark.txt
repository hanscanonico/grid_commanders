# One dug-in army holds a rampart across the middle against three on the far edge.
# grouping 1+2+3v4
# The tag above is the board's own claim, checked by the parity lint and read
# by nothing else: the alliance against the lone army. Seats 1, 2 and 3 open on
# identical holdings, 12 properties each, which D3's grouped lint checks kind
# for kind. Seat 4 opens on 30 — behind the alliance's 36, and ahead of any one
# of them — so the tally says the lone army is losing on paper. What it has
# instead is concentration and position: 7 bases to their 6 combined, and the
# middle of the board.
# A free-for-all is legal here — nothing forbids a grouping — but it is not
# fair among the three: the terrain and the property ownership mirror exactly
# about column 24, while each seat's four starting units are laid out
# seat-identically — each army is the previous seat's translated by 16, not
# its reflection — and seat 2 sits on the axis facing the bulwark's HQ down the
# middle, so picking a seat picks a role, not a clone. Seat-identical armies
# are what open the three allies on the same match, and seat 2's could not be
# self-mirrored anyway: a centred recon would stand on its own airport at
# (24,2), and no unit may start on a property. The width is odd on purpose:
# an even one cannot centre a single building, and both seat 2's HQ and the
# bulwark's stand on the axis.
# No `# symmetric` tag: that lint is a 180-degree *duel* instrument, and this
# board's terrain and ownership close under a left-right mirror instead.
# Nothing checks that mirror — the grouped parity lint counts property, not
# geometry — so keep it if you edit the board.
# `data/terrain/mountain.tres` moves only `foot`, `boot` and `air` — of the
# three rampart rows at y 16-18, row 17 is mountain wall to wall except the
# passes and is what stops tires and treads; rows 16 and 18 mix woods a
# vehicle can enter, so the middle row is the wall. Infantry and mech climb
# where no vehicle can, anywhere along the wall's 49 cells: mech (`boot` at 1
# a step, 2 move) climbs 2 rows a turn and clears the three in two turns,
# infantry (`foot` at 2 a step, 3 move) climbs 1 row a turn and takes three —
# though through a pass, where road costs 1 for both, infantry crosses all
# three rows in one turn and a mech still takes two. The alliance's armour has
# to come through the four passes at x 7-8, 19-20, 28-29, 40-41 — 8 cells of
# frontage across 49 — while its infantry can cross on a 49-cell front the
# garrison cannot hope to hold everywhere at once. The bulwark's own
# answer is interior lines: its lateral road at y 19 sits one row behind the
# wall, so it shifts reserves between passes on road cost while the alliance
# shifts around the outside on plains — the mechanic that makes one army
# against three a match instead of a grind.
# AB4 pulled this board four ways and every candidate lost to leaving it alone,
# which leaves three warnings for whoever edits it next. Do not narrow the four
# passes: closing one pair handed the alliance 20 of 20, because the garrison
# needs the passes to sortie north as much as the alliance needs them to push
# south, while infantry ignores them and crosses on a 49-cell front. The
# neutral belt is not a dial that helps the bulwark in either direction —
# shallower and richer were both measured and both cost it. And the lone army's
# edge is the interior lines above, never a bigger pile: do not buy the
# asymmetry with more units or more bases. That third one belongs here rather
# than only in the plan, because the garrison's size and placement is the lever
# AB4 left untried, so it is the first one a future retune reaches for and this
# header is where whoever edits the board meets it.
# Land only, like the other multi-army boards: the AI cannot plan a ferry, so
# every army has to be able to march on every other in any grouping this board
# offers, including the free-for-all.
# Symbols: . plains  F woods  M mountain  = road  C city  B base  Q HQ  A airport

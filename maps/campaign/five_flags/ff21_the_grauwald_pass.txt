# Five Flags, Act IV — "The Grauwald Pass". Iris Colt climbs into Iron against Iona Vance.
# The Grauwald road runs straight up a gorge (rows 5-7) to the pass fort on the
# saddle at (16,6). It is the short way and it is the trap: Vance has a battery
# in each of the two height forts, (9,3) and (9,9), cities sunk in the mountain
# walls where no wheel or tread can reach them. From there a gun ranges the
# gorge floor and the southern edge of the northern track alike.
#
# Three ways up, and each costs something different:
#   - the gorge road: fastest, under both batteries;
#   - the northern track (rows 0-2): open ground for armour, longer, and the
#     north battery still reaches its southern edge;
#   - the goat paths (rows 10-12): mountain and pine, foot only. Nobody drives
#     it, which is why the Verdant hill companies arrive there on day 3, behind
#     the south battery at (11,11), (12,11) and (14,10) — deep in the massif,
#     where no army is standing on day 3.
# Foot climbing to a battery silences it; that is the lesson.
#
# Vance has no base. She is a garrison, not a factory: what she fields is what
# stands here plus one reserve, which comes up behind the saddle at (20,5),
# (20,7) and (21,5) when the north battery falls — off the road, so a player
# already at the fort does not swallow the landing. With a base she outbuilt
# the planner every seed measured; without one the pass is a siege she loses
# slowly unless the climb is wasted.

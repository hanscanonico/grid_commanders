# Six buildings, two armies and nowhere to go but forward: the charge is the economy.
# symmetric
# 14x10 and six properties in total -- an HQ and one base a side, two neutral
# cities in the middle and nothing else. Three properties a seat is 3000 a turn,
# so a tank is three days of saving and a Md Tank most of a week: the army that
# opens the match is very nearly the army that finishes it, and what actually
# grows over those days is the Command Power meter, which charges off damage
# rather than off income. That is the board's whole point -- it is where a
# commander's power is the reinforcement, and where banking it is the mistake.
# Seven units a side and eight cells between the two forward tanks, so contact
# lands on day two and the two central cities are contested from the first move.
# Mirrors on (x, y) -> (13 - x, 9 - y). A staircase road runs from each base to
# the middle and both meet at the cities, so the fast lane and the prize are the
# same ground -- take it and you are standing in the open, in range of the gun
# that came the other way.
# Land only, and deliberately no woods worth hiding an army in: the mountain and
# the four copses are cover for one unit each, not a flank.
# Do not add a third property a side. The economy is the constraint the board is
# built on, and a fourth building on each seat turns it into an ordinary duel
# that happens to be small.

# Every army's crown sits in the enemy's corner: garrison it before you attack.
# symmetric
# The exclave is the pocket in the far corner, walled off by mountain except for
# two one-cell doors -- a road gate on the flank and a footpath on the shoulder.
# The shoulder is mountain, so it is a door for boots and nothing else: the road
# gate is the only way armour ever reaches a crown.
# Team 1's home is the north-west (two bases, two cities, the main army), and the
# HQ it cannot lose stands in the *south-east*, behind team 2's production, held
# by a detachment of two mechs, a gun and a rifleman, with a city of its own to
# repair and resupply on. Team 2's board is the same thing rotated: its crown
# sits in the north-west pocket, inside team 1's home.
# Nothing forbids an HQ far from its bases -- it is pure ownership and layout --
# and what it buys is an inverted opening. Every other board asks how fast you can
# reach the enemy; this one asks how much you can spare to hold what you already
# have, because the shortest march to a match-ending capture is the one your own
# infantry make on turn one. Mirrors on (x, y) -> (19 - x, 13 - y).
# The mountain ring is the pocket's whole defence: `data/terrain/mountain.tres`
# moves only foot, boot and air, so armour reaches a crown through the road gate
# and nowhere else, while infantry and mech climb the wall anywhere and take two
# turns doing it. Do not widen the ring's doors -- a pocket with a third way in
# is a pocket that falls before its owner can reinforce it -- and do not pave the
# shoulder: a second gate armour can drive through is the same defect.
# Neither home base sits inside a two-turn march of the crown it faces. That is
# the other half of the hold: a garrison can be worn down, but only by an army
# that walked eight tiles to do it, never by a conveyor from the base next door.
# Keep both bases at least seven tiles from the enemy pocket's gate.
# The eight neutral cities are the ordinary game running underneath: they are what
# pays for the second army each side needs, one to besiege and one to relieve.
# Symbols: . plains  F woods  M mountain  = road  C city  B base  Q HQ

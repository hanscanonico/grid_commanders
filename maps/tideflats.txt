# Two coasts, a tidal apron on each and one causeway between: the guns own the gap.
# symmetric
# The sea runs right around the board and bites into the middle, so the two homes
# are separate coasts joined by a single two-cell causeway at x 9-10 -- road down
# the apron, bridge over the deep water, road up the far apron. Everything a side
# marches has to walk it, and both aprons and the whole crossing sit inside a
# battleship's reach from the open water either side of it. That is the board:
# the ground war is a two-cell frontage and the fleet decides who can stand on it.
# Mirrors on (x, y) -> (19 - x, 15 - y).
# `data/terrain/shoal.tres` costs every land class exactly 1 and carries no
# defence at all, so the aprons are fast ground with nothing to hide behind --
# infantry works the beach in front of its own coast, and anything that parks
# there is a target. `data/terrain/sea.tres` refuses every land class, so the
# apron is a shore rather than a second road: what joins the two coasts is the
# causeway alone, which is why the map lint's landmass count reads the same with
# the beaches and without them.
# The water is deliberately one body -- the frame joins the western and eastern
# channels around both ends -- so a hull built at any of the four docks can sail
# to any other without the transport the computer cannot plan (naval plan R1).
# Two docks, two bases, an airfield and a cruiser apiece: the air is the one
# domain the causeway does not bottleneck, and the eight neutral cities include
# the four standing at the causeway heads, which is the ground both fleets are
# really shooting at.

#          A airport  P port  S sea  _ shoal

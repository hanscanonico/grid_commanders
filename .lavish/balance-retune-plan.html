Everyone fights at fifty.

The Grand Balance Atlas played 20,280 matches — every commander against every commander, on every board, at every tier — and returned three defects: one doctrine beats the entire roster, two doctrines lose to having no commander at all, and the difficulty ladder is inverted at its bottom rung. This plan converts those findings into a tune. The ladder is fixed first, because every commander number is measured on the Normal planner; then the apex comes down and the floor comes up — through .tres numbers, the existing instruments, and the one-variable loop the balance docs already prescribe.

02

Why the numbers are what they are

Every outlier has a mechanical explanation in the code that is already written. None of these requires new telemetry to see — they require the review the balance doc asks for before any number moves, and that review is written here so the tune starts from a diagnosis rather than a leaderboard position.

Apex · gideon_holt.tres

Gideon compounds; everyone else spends.

His entire kit is economy: repairs at 80% price, APCs that supply at range 2, and a 10,000-charge power — the cheapest on the roster — that refills fuel and ammo and heals the whole army at once. In AI-vs-AI attrition wars (mean decision around day 44–51) a sustain edge compounds every single day, and his 1.39× exchange rate says exactly that: he trades up 39% on funds, forever. He is not winning fights; he is winning ledgers.

Floor · cass_orlov.tres

Orlov pays always, collects almost never.

Her passive is −10% defence always on, paid for by +15% attack against enemies at ≤ 5 HP — targets the planner’s kill_bonus already kills without the bonus. The upside rarely changes an outcome; the tax bites every exchange. A negative-sum passive is precisely what “below the No Commander control” looks like.

Floor · sable_wren.tres

Wren’s doctrine is terrain-gated; the planner is terrain-blind.

+1 star in woods, −10% on roads. But the planner scores advances by distance and step cost — it never seeks cover — so her armies pay the road tax while rarely standing in the woods that pay her back. Vanish needs a unit in cover to be anything at all. BL4 verifies woods occupancy from match data before touching a number, because if occupancy is ~0, no magnitude fixes a bonus that never applies.

Low band · rhea_sol.tres · cassian_rook.tres

Rhea and Rook buy upside the AI can’t cash.

Rhea’s +10% indirect attack costs −10% indirect defence and the roster’s most expensive power (14,000). Rook’s light-unit mobility is worth little to a planner that values Recon and APCs low, while his −10% on Tank and Md Tank bites the units it actually fields; his power spends the turn’s damage to reposition — a concept the planner barely exploits.

Ladder · data/ai/default.tres

Normal is the only tier with zero self-preservation.

Normal ships threat_aversion 0.0 / advance_threat_tiles 0.0 — the pre-difficulty AI, bit for bit, pinned by test. Easy (0.3 / 3.0) hangs back; Difficult (0.1 / 2.0) refuses kill zones. Over a 44-day game, the tier that walks into every kill zone feeds the tier that doesn’t — which is why timid Easy outlasts it and why Difficult beats Normal comfortably. The middle rung is weakest because it is the only one with no judgement about danger at all.

Context · mara_voss.tres and the upper band

The 60s may fix themselves.

Win rates are against the field, so the distribution is zero-sum: pull 84 down and two sub-32s up, and everyone in between recentres. Mara (69.5), Alina (64.7) and Orin (63.9) are re-judged on the re-baseline after BL2–BL4 land, and touched only if they still sit above 60. Mara’s counter-doctrine also feeds on planners that attack into her — a stronger, more careful Normal may deflate her on its own.

03

The order of battle

The single most important structural fact: every commander number is measured on the Normal planner. If Normal’s profile moves after the roster is tuned, every roster number is stale the moment it lands. So the ladder is repaired first, Normal is frozen, and only then does the roster move — extremes first, middle re-judged after, because the field is zero-sum.

The wrong order

Tune the roster, then fix the ladder

  • Gideon retuned against today’s Normal planner.
  • BL2 then changes Normal’s judgement weights.
  • Every commander number was measured on a planner that no longer exists.
  • The whole roster pass reruns. Weeks of compute spent twice.

This plan’s order

Instrument → ladder → freeze → apex → floor → prove

  • BL1 builds the long-horizon gate and a fresh baseline.
  • BL2 rights the ladder and freezes Normal.
  • BL3 brings the apex down on the frozen planner.
  • BL4 lifts the floor above the control.
  • BL5 proves the result at scale and records it.

Compute discipline: the full atlas cost 305 core-hours; it is a verdict instrument, not an iteration loop. Tuning iterates on the focused presets the docs already define (make commander-balance BAL="--commanders=… --seeds=…", make difficulty-check on resolving boards) and the atlas-scale driver runs once, at the end, on a targeted subset.

04

Milestones

Five milestones, each independently shippable and each ending with the instruments green. One .tres value moves per probe, COM-120 style: every hypothesis gets a row in the doc, rejected values are restored before the next axis moves, and only the runner writes a standing number.

BL1

Fresh baseline, and a gate that measures who wins

  • Re-run the commander matrix full batch on current rules and record the standing result in docs/commander_balance.md — the doc itself says its N4 numbers predate the charge-meter and planner fixes and must be re-run before quoting. This baseline is the control every later probe is judged against.
  • Give the difficulty check a long-horizon half: same runner, same adjacent-pair grammar, run at a cap the games actually finish under (--days=100) on boards that resolve. scrimmage resolves; ironworks demonstrably does not — so this half adds one committed, 180°-symmetric, resolving mid-size board to the gate set (modelled on Arsenal’s geometry: 99.6% resolved, mean day 47). The 20-day gate stays exactly as documented — it remains the fast smoke and the committed DF4 record.
  • Additive flags only: the existing make difficulty-check and make commander-balance presets stay byte-stable, proven the standing way — fixed-seed runs on both sides of the change, byte-diff of both reports.
  • Run the new half and record the red honestly: long-horizon Normal-over-Easy is expected to fail. That failing number is BL2’s brief, committed to docs/difficulty_check.md as the pre-tune control.
BL2

The ladder rights itself: Easy < Normal < Difficult, at every horizon

  • Retune data/ai/default.tres — judgement weights only, one axis per probe. Leading hypotheses, in order: threat_aversion 0.0 → 0.05 / 0.1 (the §6 probes measured 0.02–0.10 as a mild real gain; ≥ 0.2 is the known cliff), advance_threat_tiles 0.0 → 1.6 / 2.0 (the documented floor for a live value is ~1.6), then kill_bonus / retreat_hp if the ladder still wants more. Every probe row lands in docs/difficulty_check.md.
  • This deliberately supersedes the DF-era pin that Normal plans bit-for-bit like the pre-difficulty AI (test_capability_defaults_plan_exactly_like_the_shipped_profile). The class defaults stay 0.0 and the test keeps proving that 0.0 skips the capability code; what changes is the shipped profile’s values, and the test pin moves with them, recorded as decision D2. The never-cheats assertions do not move.
  • Re-gate both pairings at both horizons. If the stronger Normal pushes Difficult-over-Normal below 70% at the 20-day gate, retune data/ai/hard.tres in the same pass — the ladder ships as a whole, not a rung. easy.tres moves only as a last resort: its timidity is the tier’s designed character, and the defect is Normal’s judgement, not Easy’s strength.
  • Exit: 20-day gate green as documented (≥ 70% both pairings) and the new long-horizon half monotone — Normal ≥ 60% over Easy, Difficult ≥ 60% over Normal, Difficult > 50% over Easy head-to-head. Then Normal freezes for the rest of the plan, and the BL1 commander baseline is re-run once on the frozen planner.
BL3

The apex comes back to the pack

  • Gideon Holt, by the tuning ladder’s own preference order. First lever, power_cost 10,000 → 12,000: the roster’s strongest measured doctrine currently owns its cheapest power, so cadence is the narrowest cut. Second, magnitude: depot_heal_hp 10 → 5 (the refill stays; the free army-wide pip is the part that compounds). Passive last and only with human confirmation: repair_price_pct 80 → 90.
  • Iterate on the focused preset (Gideon vs a 4-commander spread + No Commander, 3 scenarios, reduced seeds); confirm each retained probe on the full batch. Exit: Gideon inside 40–60 against the field at Normal, exchange drifting toward ~1.1, identity intact — he should still be the logistics commander, just not a different game.
  • Mara Voss re-judged on the post-BL3 re-baseline; if she still clears 60, same ladder (cost 13,000 → 14,000 first). Alina and Orin re-judged the same way, expected to recentre on their own.
BL4

The floor rises above the control

  • Verify before tuning, per the balance doc’s own rule: from focused-run CSVs and replays, measure how often each floor doctrine actually fires — Orlov’s finisher bonus on shots that weren’t already lethal, Wren’s woods occupancy and road exposure, Rhea’s indirect share, Rook’s light-unit usage. The number that moves is the one the evidence indicts.
  • Cass Orlov (24.0): cost 12,000 → 10,000 first; then no_escape_attack_pct; passive defense_pct −10 → −5 with human confirmation — the always-on tax is the prime suspect for a below-control result.
  • Sable Wren (30.9): cost 12,000 → 10,000 first; then ambush_attack_pct 40 → 50; passive road_defense_pct −10 → −5 with human confirmation. If verification shows her woods bonus essentially never applies under the planner, numbers cannot lift her — that finding escalates to the doctrine-reshape clause in D4 instead of another magnitude probe.
  • Rhea Sol (34.2): cost 14,000 → 12,000 first (dearest power on a bottom-three commander); then indirect_defense_pct −10 → −5 with human confirmation. Cassian Rook (38.4): cost 11,000 → 10,000 first; then heavy_attack_pct −10 → −5 with human confirmation.
  • Exit: both below-control commanders measure above No Commander at Normal and Difficult, and all four floor commanders reach ≥ 40 against the field at Normal on the full batch.
BL5

Prove it at scale, and write it down

  • One targeted verdict sweep with the resumable grand-sweep driver: all 13 slots at Normal and Difficult on a resolving-board subset (Scrimmage, First Steps, Forge, Riverline, Arsenal, plus Isthmus for a naval read) — a fraction of the 305-hour full grid, aimed exactly at the claims this plan makes.
  • Regenerate the atlas dataset over the new cells, refresh .lavish/grand-balance-atlas.html, and update the Notion record so the readable atlas states the post-tune world.
  • Records: one changelog row per retained probe in docs/commander_balance.md; new standing verdict and tuning table in docs/difficulty_check.md; the CLAUDE.md plan index gains this plan with its invariants.
  • Exit: every commander inside 40–60 at Normal (45–55 preferred), no one below the control at any measured tier, ladder monotone at both horizons, zero rejected commands, zero cap stalls, make verify green. The 24-session human test deck remains the release task it always was — this plan proves the measured half, and does not claim the feel half.

Locked decisions and explicit non-goals

The decisions below are load-bearing; a change to any of them is a change to this plan, not an implementation detail.

IDDecisionWhy it’s locked
D1 Ladder before roster; Normal freezes after BL2. Every commander number is measured on the Normal planner. Tuning the roster on a planner that then changes wastes the entire pass.
D2 DF4 gains a long-horizon half; the 20-day gate survives unchanged; the Normal-identity pin is deliberately superseded. The atlas proved the 20-day gate measures who is ahead early, not who wins. Adding a horizon tightens the gate — nothing is loosened. The pinned test keeps proving 0.0 skips capability code; the shipped profile’s values move with a recorded probe table.
D3 The AI never cheats, at any tier — still absolute. The ladder is repaired with judgement weights on AIProfile only. No income, vision, damage or luck moves, in either direction. This is the difficulty plan’s locked invariant and this plan inherits it whole.
D4 Numbers first, one variable at a time, cost → magnitude → passive; doctrine reshape only for the two below-control commanders, only on evidence numbers can’t fix it, only with human confirmation. The balance doc’s tuning loop is the standing rule. The narrow reshape clause exists because “worse than nothing at every tier” is a defect, not a preference — but it is the last resort, behaviour stays in the CommanderType subclass, and it lands as its own reviewed change.
D5 The telemetry observes; it never instruments the sim. Balance-simulator D2, inherited: nothing under core/ or ai/ gains a hook for any measurement this plan takes. BL4’s doctrine-usage verification reads match CSVs and replays, not new probes in the sim.
D6 Only the runners write standing numbers. Inherited from both balance docs: no number in any record is re-scored by estimate. A claim without a fresh run is not a claim.

Explicit non-goals

  • No core/ rules or damage-chart change — this is a .tres-and-docs plan.
  • No AI ferry planning. The Straits stall is the naval plan’s standing R1, owned there; The Straits stays out of every gate board set.
  • No first-seat rules change (komi, staggered income) inside this plan — see the open decision below.
  • Easy’s unfinished-game character (77.7% resolution, HQ-capture endings) is a tier identity read, not a defect this plan fixes.
  • No Easy-tier heavy-board sweep completion — the archived band already showed the roster shape holds; the 30–40 hours buy nothing this plan needs.
  • The human test deck is not automated away — feel remains a release task outside this plan.
  • The commanders plan’s locked D2 (charge accrual split 100% lost / 50% dealt) does not move.
07

Named risks and mitigations

R1 · The known failure mode

Balancing to the AI leaderboard alone.

The planner underuses terrain, timing and specialised economies — a number that fixes the sweep can hurt human play. Mitigation: the band stays a review trigger, every passive change needs human confirmation, and the human deck keeps the last word on feel.

R2 · Cascade

A moving baseline invalidates the pass.

Changing Normal after roster tuning — or tuning two commanders against each other’s stale numbers — turns the plan circular. Mitigation: D1’s ordering, the freeze, one re-baseline per milestone boundary, extremes before middles.

R3 · Ladder coupling

Fixing Normal breaks Difficult’s gate.

A sharper Normal squeezes Difficult’s 70% from below. Mitigation: BL2 budgets a hard.tres follow-up in the same milestone; the ladder ships whole or not at all.

R4 · Un-liftable floor

Wren or Orlov can’t reach the band on numbers.

A terrain-gated bonus a terrain-blind planner never triggers has no magnitude fix. Mitigation: BL4 verifies doctrine usage before tuning; the reshape clause (D4) is the named escalation, human-confirmed, identity-preserving.

R5 · Compute burn

Iterating at atlas scale.

The full grid costs 305 core-hours; a tuning loop through it is weeks. Mitigation: focused presets for iteration, full batch per retained probe, atlas-scale driver exactly once (BL5), resumable by design.

R6 · Gate debt

The new long-horizon gate rots or flakes.

A gate on a board that stops resolving, or a cap games outgrow, silently degrades to a tiebreak reading again. Mitigation: the gate board is chosen for resolution and asserted symmetric; resolution share is reported with every run and a collapse below the reading rule’s floor fails the run loudly.

08

Open decision: the first seat

The atlas’ mirror cells measured going first at roughly +20 points, stable across all three tiers. Every number in this plan is side-normalised, so the tune is immune to it — but the edge is real in a single human match. Fixing it is a game-rules change (komi-style funds offset, staggered banking, alternating seats), which is out of this plan’s .tres-only scope. What should happen to it?

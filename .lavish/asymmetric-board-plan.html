Bulwark — the board that is not fair, on purpose

Every shipped board deals its seats the same opening, and a lint enforces it. This one does not. Bulwark is 49×32 — twice the largest board that ships — and it seats one entrenched army holding the midline against three allies massed on the far edge. The lone army opens with a dug-in garrison, twice anyone's production, and a rampart across the middle of the map with four passes through it. The three open with nothing but their corners and each other.

The board is the easy half. The hard half is that tests/unit/test_maps.gd::test_every_map_deals_each_team_the_same_properties asserts, over every board in maps/, that no seat opens on more income or more production than another — Bulwark fails it by construction. So the first milestone is not the map. It is teaching that lint to check sides instead of seats, without switching it off.

1

Where this stands — what already exists, and the one thing that blocks it

3v1 is already a shipped capability. GameState.allied is the one hostility authority, --sides=1+2+3v4 is its grammar, and the seat strip ships a 3v1 preset. What has never existed is a board authored for it: the preset is offered on every four-seat map, and on all of them the lone army fights three equal rivals from an equal corner. Four Armies D5 says so in as many words — "3v1 is deliberately asymmetric — a challenge grouping, compensated by commander pick and tier, never by the board." This plan is that clause's supersession, and it is the whole reason the plan exists rather than being another map ticket.

What ships already and is untouched

  • The grouping — sides on the state, GameState.allied, --sides=1+2+3v4, shared sight across a side. No code here.
  • The 3v1 preset — SeatStrip.PRESETS already writes sides: [0,0,0,1], seats: [HUMAN, CPU, CPU, CPU]. Bulwark authors seat 4 as the lone army so that preset fits it byte for byte (D4).
  • Discovery — a board is a .txt under maps/; MapCatalog feeds the menu, the lint and every soak from one scan.
  • Size — the camera derives its limits and minimum zoom from map.size(); test_map_soak.gd scales its command cap by area. 49×32 needs no plumbing.

The one thing that blocks it

test_every_map_deals_each_team_the_same_properties sweeps every board in maps/ and asserts every seat opens on identical holdings, counted by kind. Its own comment says why it is load-bearing:

"two seats that open on different holdings are playing two different matches — and the difference reads back as that commander is stronger rather than that seat is, which is the one attribution no amount of playtesting recovers from."

That reasoning is correct and Bulwark does not refute it. It refutes its scope: the unit of fairness on a board authored for a grouping is the side, not the seat. AB1 moves the lint to that unit. It does not weaken it.

2

The board — Bulwark, 49×32

1,568 cells — twice the largest board that ships (Confluence, 32×24). The width is odd on purpose: 49 gives an exact left–right mirror about column 24, so seats 1 and 3 are true reflections and seat 2, like the bulwark's HQ, sits on the axis. An even width cannot centre a single building.

The lone army's HQ is on the south edge, but its front line is the middle of the map — it opens already holding the ground the alliance has to take.

The rampart is a wall for vehicles and a hill for infantry — and that is the whole board

data/terrain/mountain.tres lists foot, boot and air and nothing else. So the three-row mountain ridge at y 16–18 is absolutely impassable to tyres and treads — including around the board edges, which are mountain too — while infantry and mech climb it anywhere at 2 movement a step. That was not designed in; it was read out of the terrain data, and it is what makes the board a battle rather than a siege of four doorways:

  • The alliance's armour must come through the four two-cell passes at x 7–8, 19–20, 28–29, 40–41 — 8 cells of frontage across 49.
  • The alliance's infantry can cross the ridge on a 49-cell front, three turns of climbing, and arrive anywhere. The bulwark cannot garrison all of it.
  • So the alliance's real opening is not "break a pass". It is: take no man's land with foot, walk foot over the ridge to pin the garrison, and bring armour through a pass the garrison has left.

Read the tally the right way round

The alliance out-owns the bulwark 36 to 30 at kick-off and out-produces it in the air 3 airports to 2. It also gets three turns a day to the bulwark's one. On every number on this page the lone army is behind.

What it has instead is concentration and position. Its 7 bases beat all three rivals' 6 combined, so one army replaces losses at three armies' rate. It opens with 16 units to their 12 — and theirs are three separate armies of four. It stands on the middle of the board behind a wall no vehicle crosses, on a road that answers any of the four passes faster than the alliance can shift around the outside (D6).

The alliance's win condition is no man's land: 30 neutral cities, 5 neutral bases and 2 neutral airports, more than doubling its economy if it takes them fast — and those 5 bases are how the alliance answers the bulwark's production without building a single thing on the far side of the ridge. The bulwark's win condition is to come through a pass and stop that before it compounds. Neither opening decides the match, which is the entire point of authoring the asymmetry instead of leaving the preset to produce it.

Decisions — locked

D1 · The handicap is board state and nothing else

Nothing under core/, ai/ or scenes/ learns this board is special. There is no handicap multiplier, no starting-funds field, no per-seat difficulty, no "boss" flag. The lone army's advantage is entirely [owners], [units] and terrain — the three things a map file already says. Rejected: a starting-funds or income-multiplier field on the map, which would put a second opinion about the economy between the board and GameState.create and would apply to every board that ever set it.

D2 · # grouping 1+2+3v4 is a claim the lint checks, never an instruction the match follows

Read in MapData._read_comment beside SYMMETRIC_TAG, exposed as MapData.grouping, and its only reader is tests/unit/test_maps.gd. Not the menu, not MatchRequest, not a preselected preset. Four-Player Maps D5 rejects map-file metadata for seatings — "no recommended-pairs syntax: the convention lives in the boards' header comments and in the preset" — and a tag the menu obeys is exactly the thing it rejects. What this tag is instead is the # symmetric precedent, unchanged: a fairness claim a board makes about itself that a test verifies. A board that carries it and plays FFA plays FFA; the tag has no runtime.

D3 · Parity moves from the seat to the side. It is not switched off

On a board declaring a grouping, test_every_map_deals_each_team_the_same_properties checks two things instead of one:

  • Within a side, seats stay identical kind for kind. Seats 1, 2 and 3 must open on the same holdings as each other. This is the defect that actually happens — one allied seat authored a city short — and it is exactly the check the shipped lint already performs, applied at the right scope.
  • Across sides, no side may out-own the sum of its opponents. A ceiling rather than a judgement: a lint cannot say whether 30-against-36 is fair, but it can say that a side opening with more property than everyone else combined is a board defect wearing a design label.

That ceiling already earned its place: the first tally drafted for this board gave the bulwark more property than its three rivals held between them, and the ceiling is what caught it. The fix was the design fix — the lone army is behind on paper and ahead on ground — not a looser number.

Rejected: an # asymmetric opt-out tag that skips the lint (the board then has no economy check at all), and a filename exemption list inside the test (the drift MapCatalog exists to prevent).

D4 · Seat 4 is the lone army, so the shipped 3v1 preset fits without a line of UI

SeatStrip.PRESETS already writes sides: [0,0,0,1], seats: [HUMAN, CPU, CPU, CPU] — seats 1+2+3 against seat 4, human on seat 1. Bulwark is authored to that, so opening the board and pressing 3v1 is the match this plan describes. Playing the bulwark yourself is flipping seat 4 to Human in the strip. Rejected: a second "Last Stand" preset — a preset is a table row that exists on every board, and one whose meaning only lands on this one is a menu that lies on twenty-three others.

D5 · The three allied seats are roles, not clones — and the board says so

A rectangle has no three-fold symmetry, so the alliance cannot be laid out interchangeably. Under the left–right mirror, seats 1 and 3 are exact reflections and seat 2 is the centre: 2 faces the bulwark's HQ down the middle, 1 and 3 flank. They open on identical holdings (D3 checks it) and on deliberately different ground. That is a feature in a 3v1 — picking a seat picks a role — but it has one consequence that must be written in the header rather than discovered: a free-for-all on this board is not fair among the three. It stays legal, because nothing forbids a grouping; the header names the grouping the board is for.

D6 · The lone army's edge is interior lines, not a bigger pile

The lateral road at y 16–17 sits behind the rampart, so the bulwark shifts reserves between the four passes on road cost while the alliance shifts around the outside on plains. That is the mechanic that makes one-against-three a match rather than a grind, it needs no code (MovementResolver already reads road cost), and the AI gets it for free. Stated as a decision because the alternative — buying the asymmetry purely with more units and more bases — is what produces an unfair board instead of an asymmetric one, and it is the failure mode this design is most likely to drift back into under a retune (AB4).

D7 · Land only

Naval plan R1: the AI cannot plan a ferry. Every army has to be able to march on every other in any grouping this board offers, including the free-for-all. No water at all — which also keeps the port, shoal and delivery lints trivially green and keeps the board's identity in one idea instead of two.

Milestones — the guardrail lands before the board

Same order the More Maps plan used and for the same reason: the lint ships first, so the board is born validated rather than validated in arrears.

AB1 · The lint learns sides shipped

size S · no new board

MapData.grouping parsed from # grouping 1+2+3v4 (D2), and test_every_map_deals_each_team_the_same_properties split into the two checks of D3. Ships with the house's can-it-fail counter-tests, matching test_the_shared_water_lint_can_tell_two_seas_apart: a grouped fixture with one allied seat a city short must fail, and a grouped fixture whose declared lone side out-owns the rest combined must fail.

Gate: make verify green; every shipped board still linted seat-by-seat, because none carries the tag yet — so this milestone changes no existing board's verdict.

AB2 · Bulwark

size M

maps/bulwark.txt, 49×32, §2's board verbatim, D5's header. Every existing lint green on arrival: one owned HQ per army, a base per army, HQs reachable on foot, no unit starting on a property, a menu description. Plus AB1's two new ones.

Gate: make verify green including test_map_soak.gd (which now plays the largest board in the roster). make commander-balance and make difficulty-check byte-identical — adding a .txt touches no fixed board list, and any movement means something read the roster that should not have.

AB3 · Proof of play, and the measurement

size M

Bulwark joins tests/unit/test_alliance_soak.gd in its own grouping first, the treatment Marchlands gets as the board that exists for a pairing — then in the free-for-all, because that grouping is reachable and must not crash even though it is not fair. On top of that, the fairness instrument the Four Armies plan already names for boards no symmetry tag can hold: the win spread over N seeds, AI on all four seats, neutral commanders, recorded in docs/.

Gate: no rejected command, no stall, both groupings; the win spread written down whatever it says. AB3 measures and does not tune — same split as the Judgement plan's AJ4.

AB4 · The retune, from AB3's number

size S

Read AB3's spread and move the board — the garrison's size, the neutral belt's depth, the rampart's passes — until neither side is a foregone conclusion. The board is the only dial. No AIProfile weight, no tier value, no data/ edit: those are owned by the balance retune and the difficulty plan, and a board that needs the planner changed to be playable is a board that is wrong.

Gate: the retune and its before/after spread recorded; both balance reports still byte-identical.

5

Risks

R1 · Tempo, not income, is the alliance's real advantage

Three armies act three times a day to the bulwark's once. Every property tally on this page understates that, and no lint can see it. Do not assert the balance — measure it (AB3). If the alliance runs away with it, the dial is the garrison and the belt, not the tally.

R2 · The AI has no "hold the line" mode

_advance_goal pushes forward; a bulwark that marches out of its rampart on day two throws the board's premise away. The only things pulling the other way are AJ1's defend_weight and _besieged_home_hqs, and neither was priced for this. Mitigation is AB3's soak; if it walks out, the answer is the board — a deeper rampart, fewer passes — never new planner code. Precedent: Four Armies looked at hq_capture_multiplier after elimination changed what it bought, and left it alone.

R3 · Suite wall clock

test_map_soak.gd plays every board for 10 days at a cap of w×h×4 — 6,272 commands here, the largest in the roster by far — and test_alliance_soak.gd adds more, twice, on a board with 21 armies' worth of production. Watch make test's runtime at AB2 and AB3. The dial if it bites is the seed count in AB3's measurement, not the board's size.

R4 · The balance instruments cannot measure this board

BalanceMatchEngine plays two sides. make commander-balance and make difficulty-check must not be pointed at Bulwark, and both reports staying byte-identical is AB2's and AB4's merge bar precisely because this board is invisible to them. The fairness number comes from AB3's GUT soak and nowhere else.

R5 · The tag must not become a general opt-out

# grouping is one board's declaration, not a way for the twenty-fourth map to duck the parity lint. Guard, cheap and self-limiting: a board carrying the tag whose sides open equal fails — if the opening is fair seat by seat, the tag is unnecessary and belongs deleted.

R6 · A board this size has never been played here

49×32 is 1,568 cells against Confluence's 768 — this board is twice the biggest thing the engine has been asked to play, and it carries 66 owned properties and 28 starting units on top. The pipeline is size-agnostic by design (the More Maps plan measured that), and ironworks — the one board reaching the arena's command cap — does it at 24×16, so the dominant risk is economy density rather than area. But Bulwark is large and dense: 101 properties all told. This is now the plan's most likely source of an unpleasant surprise, and AB2's gate is a measured make test runtime, not a green tick.

6

What this supersedes, in one place

Four Armies D5 — "3v1 is deliberately asymmetric — a challenge grouping, compensated by commander pick and tier, never by the board." True of every board that shipped before this one, and it stays the rule for all of them. Bulwark is the named exception: a board may compensate a grouping when it is authored for that grouping and declares it.

Four-Player Maps D5 — "boards are authored full and reduced fairness is a convention, not a parser feature." Unchanged and deliberately respected: the # grouping tag is not a parser feature in the sense D5 forbids, because nothing at runtime reads it (D2). It is a lint claim, the # symmetric shape.

The map lint's per-seat parity — its reasoning survives verbatim; its scope becomes the side on a board that declares one (D3). Every board without the tag is checked exactly as it is today.

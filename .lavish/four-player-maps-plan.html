Four-Player Maps & Open Seats — a 4-seat board plays 2, 3, or 4

The Four Armies plan (.lavish/four-players-plan.html, rev 2 — COM-44→49, unimplemented) seats up to four armies and lets the match group them; its FP6 ships the first two multi-army boards, Trident (3) and Compass (4). This plan finishes the thought in two moves. First, a roster: eight more four-seat boards in three tiers — a small pair for the lunch-break brawl, a standard trio (one for the land war, one for the air, one for the sea), and a grand trio half again their size, built for the long game — so four armies is a shelf of maps, not a demo. Second, a capability the Four Armies design deliberately does not have: a seat may stay empty. Its D1 makes the map the roster authority — the board says four, the match plays four. Here that authority bends downward only: the map still owns how many seats exist and where they sit; the match chooses which of them are filled, any two or more. A 4-seat board becomes a duel or a three-way without touching the file; a vacant seat's start dissolves to neutral ground worth fighting over.

Where this stands — a layer on Four Armies, not a rival

Everything here assumes the Four Armies milestones FP1–FP6 land first, and touches their mechanisms rather than duplicating them. The dependency is real and one-way:

What Four Armies already provides

  • The roster as data — FP1 derives GameState.teams from the map's [owners] + [units], bounds teams to 1–4 contiguous in the file, and gives MapData a player_count. This plan reads that roster; it never re-parses.
  • One hostility authority — FP2's state.allied(a, b) and the sides grouping. Open seats compose with any grouping: sides simply may not name an empty seat.
  • Elimination — FP3 models a fallen army: units removed, properties to neutral, survivors play on. An empty seat is not an elimination (§3 D1), but the "properties revert to neutral, stay capturable" precedent is exactly the semantics a vacant start borrows.
  • The seat strip — FP5's per-seat Human/CPU rows with side badges and presets. The Empty state is one more value on a control that already exists per seat.
  • Retargeted lints — FP1/FP6 point tests/unit/test_maps.gd at each map's own roster (one HQ and base per army). The three new boards are authored full and lint as 4-army maps; emptiness is match state, never file state.

What Four Armies deliberately does not do

  • Fewer armies than the board offers — its D1: "the number of armies is a property of the board, read from the map file — never a menu setting." True as a ceiling; this plan adds the floor. The rejection D1 argued against (growing a global constant, seeding four funds entries into every duel) stays rejected — nothing here widens a 2-seat board.
  • A second HQ's meaning — FP3 keys elimination to "the HQ's owner", which is exact while every HQ has a living owner. A vacant seat's HQ has none, and a survivor who captures a fallen army's HQ suddenly owns two. §3 D3 sharpens this once, for both cases.
  • More boards — FP6 ships two proof boards and stops. The shipped duel-roster instruments (the menu, the map lint, the per-map soak) already discover maps through MapCatalog.paths() (core/map_catalog.gd:27), so shipping a board is still dropping a .txt in maps/ — the content cost is authorship and proof, not plumbing.
# The seam, end to end — one new fact flows left to right map file (4 seats, authored full, lints as 4-army)   → menu seat strip Human / CPU / Empty per seat (≥2 filled, ≥2 sides)   → MatchRequest.seats (empty array = every seat, today's behaviour)   → GameState.create(map, …, seats) roster = map roster ∩ seats; vacant starts dissolve to neutral   → save v4 records the roster it already records — resume, rematch, watch mode all inherit for free

The design — the map offers seats, the match fills them

A seat, not a mode
A board's seats are its authored starting positions — team ids 1..N in the file, each with its HQ, base and livery. The match fills each seat with a human, a CPU, or nobody. Filled seats play exactly as Four Armies specifies; an empty seat simply never enters the state. No funds entry, no commander slot, no turn, no banner — the sim cannot tell a Compass duel from a board that was authored with two seats.
The vacant start dissolves
An empty seat's [units] rows are skipped and its [owners] rows fall to neutral — its HQ and base become high-ground income properties anyone may capture, the same "fallen empires are contested ground" rule FP3 applies to the eliminated. The map file is never edited and no terrain converts — this is match-time state, so the map-retrofit plan's conversion rules are not even in play.
Seats keep their numbers
Closing seat 2 on a 4-seat board leaves roster [1, 3, 4] — never a renumber, because [owners], [units], liveries and commander picks are all keyed by the seat's team id. Turn order stays roster order over the filled seats; the day advances on wrap to the first of them. FP1's "contiguous from 1" stays a file rule; the match roster may be any subset of it.

The generalisation is deliberate: nothing below is four-specific. Any board with N seats plays 2..N — Compass plays 2, 3 or 4; Trident plays 2 or 3; a duel board offers no seat to close and its setup screen is pixel-identical to today. "Play a 4-player map with 2 or 3" is the headline case of one rule, not three features.

# Compass with seat 3 empty — a three-way on a four-seat board seats filled {1, 2, 4} · sides empty ⇒ free-for-all of three seat 3's HQ, base → neutral · its infantry never spawn · no funds[3], no turn 3 day wraps 4 → 1 · elimination and victory exactly as FP3 wrote them for three armies save mid-match → envelope roster [1, 2, 4] → resume rebuilds the same three-army match

Decisions

D1 · An empty seat never enters the state — it is not an elimination

proposed

Two ways to model a vacant seat: an army eliminated on day 0, or an army that never existed. Never existed wins. Day-0 elimination would drag a ghost through the whole presentation layer — a name in the standings, a livery the info sheet lays out, an entry the elimination banner has to be taught to skip, an eliminated record in every save — and every one of those is a place the ghost can leak. Instead GameState.create gains one defaulted parameter, p_seats (empty = every map team, today's behaviour verbatim), intersects it with the map roster, and builds only the filled seats. FP1's invariant strengthens rather than bends: a reduced match on a big board produces exactly the state a small board would have produced — which is also why the Balance Lab, the codec and every downstream consumer need nothing.

Rejected: a menu-side map preprocessor that hands the sim a doctored MapData. It would put a second opinion about the roster between the file and the state — the exact drift D1 of Four Armies exists to prevent — and watch mode, rematch and the CLI would each need their own copy of the doctoring.

D2 · The seats are the match's fourth setup fact, carried by MatchRequest

proposed

Board, sides, commanders — and now seats. scenes/common/match_request.gd gains seats: Array[int] (empty = all), flowing through all three adapters: from_menu reads the seat strip; from_match copies the live state.teams, so a rematch of a reduced match is the same reduced match; apply_cmdline reads the one new flag, --seats=1,3,4 — has()-gated like every override, and a --seats= that names fewer than two of the board's seats fails the build with a pushed error, it does not guess. Sides validation tightens by one clause: a side may only name a filled seat. --co= stays positional over the filled roster in seat order — commanders belong to armies that play.

D3 · Elimination keys to the home HQ — one rule covers vacant and conquered HQs

proposed · sharpens Four Armies FP3/D3

FP3 says "HQ capture eliminates the HQ's owner". Open seats force the question that rule leaves open: what is an HQ nobody owns? And its sibling was already lurking in FP3 itself: a survivor who captures a fallen army's HQ owns two HQs — and "eliminate the HQ's owner" would then let a rival behead them through the captured outpost. One rule closes both: GameState.create records each filled seat's starting HQ cell as its home_hq, and only capturing a team's home HQ eliminates it. Every other HQ — a vacant seat's from day 1, a fallen army's after FP3's reversion — is a high-value income property with HQ terrain stars, captured like a city. The save envelope carries home_hq per team (it cannot be re-derived once the map's HQs have changed hands). In a duel on a duel board the home HQ is the only HQ a team could ever lose, so today's behaviour is pinned unchanged.

Rejected: downgrading a vacant HQ's terrain to a city at match start — it keeps "every HQ has an owner" true but mutates terrain per match, splits the board from its file, and does nothing for the two-HQ edge FP3 already has.

D4 · Empty is the seat strip's third state; presets stay one tap

proposed

FP5's seat row cycles Human → CPU → Empty — Empty offered only while closing that seat leaves at least two filled (so a duel board never shows it, and the last two seats of any board cannot close). Setup validation becomes "≥2 seats filled, ≥2 sides among the filled". The presets row gains the reduced counts on multi-seat boards — Free-for-all, 2v2, 3v1 joined by Duel (fills the board's opposite-seat pair, §4's authoring convention, human in seat 1) and Three-way on 4-seat boards — so "I just want a duel on Windrose" is one tap, not four cycles. The map list's player count (FP5) becomes a range where seats can close: "2–4 players". Commander select's slot walk (FP5/D6) walks the filled seats only. Defaults are untouched: picking a 4-seat map still opens all four seats, seat 1 human, the rest CPU, free-for-all.

D5 · Boards are authored full; reduced fairness is a convention, not a parser feature

proposed

Every new board lints as a full 4-army map — one HQ per seat, production and property counts equal across seats, funds-fair by review per FP6/R5. Reduced play then leans on geometry: the authoring convention is that opposite seats (1&3, 2&4) make the fair duel — the Duel preset encodes it, and on a 90°-rotational board it is fair by construction. Three-on-four is honest about being casual: closing any seat of a rotational board leaves one army with a quieter flank and more neutral ground to race for, which is a texture, not a defect — a player who wants a fair three-way has Trident. No new map-file metadata, no recommended- seating section in the format: the convention lives in the boards' header comments and the preset, and a future N-way symmetry lint (already named future work in FP6/R5) remains the structural answer.

4

The boards — eight four-seat maps, three tiers

Compass (FP6) proves four armies on land. These eight give the shelf a spread, in three tiers. The small pair is the quick game — four armies in contact by day 2. The standard trio sits near the shipped boards' footprint (the largest today is Crossfire at 20×15) — each a distinct movement-domain character. The grand trio is half again bigger — boards where a 2v2 has room for real fronts, production runs two bases per seat, and a match is won over a campaign, not a skirmish. All eight are 90°-rotationally laid out so the Duel preset is fair by construction, seats at the corners in reading order (1 NW, 2 NE, 3 SE, 4 SW — opposite pairs 1&3, 2&4). Schematics are the character of each board; authorship happens in the milestones, under the retargeted lints.

The small pair

Foursquare · the four-way forge
The smallest four-seat board, ~12×12, and a build-first one: no [units] section, per the production plan's D1 — four HQs, four bases, a handful of cities, and day 1 is four build menus opening at once. The four-way answer to Forge: the whole match is the escalation curve, and a full FFA fits in a sitting. Land only, per the production boards' D3.
Pinwheel · first contact, day 2
~14×14 with a standard infantry-screen start and the roads laid as a rotational spiral — each seat's fast lane runs clockwise into its neighbour's flank, so the opening question is whether to race the wheel or cut across the wooded hub. The chaotic little brawl of the shelf; a duel on opposite seats plays like a compact classic. Land only.

A deliberate menu consequence: MapCatalog.ordered() sorts the beginner board first, then by area — so Foursquare lands right behind First Steps, and the second board a new player scrolls past reads "2–4 players". The shelf's widest capability advertises itself without a single menu change.

The standard trio

Windrose · land & air
The first multi-army air board: an airfield per seat, a contested neutral airfield dead centre, mountain passes between neighbours. T-copters make the diagonal — the duel pairing fights across the long axis while the flanks stay neutral income. ~18×18, [units] opening with the standard infantry screen.
Atoll · the sea between
One connected central sea, one port per seat, a neutral island of cities in the middle. The naval plan's standing R1 is satisfied by construction — every fleet reaches every other across open water, no ferry required — and the island is why a navy is worth building: it is capturable only by what a lander or t-copter delivers. ~20×20.
Marchlands · the 2v2 board
Built for the alliance groupings: a wooded ridge splits north from south, roads run the lanes, and the seat pairing across the ridge (1&2 north, 3&4 south — allied via --sides=1+2v3+4) gives each pair a shared front and separate purses. Land only, per the production boards' precedent. The largest of the three, ~22×16.

The grand trio

Heartland · the great plain
The big open land war, ~28×20: a dense lattice of neutral cities, two bases per seat, roads on the diagonals, woods for cover instead of chokepoints. Where Marchlands channels a 2v2 into lanes, Heartland lets fronts form anywhere and the capture race decide where — the board the income curve was always pointing at. Land only.
Causeway · islands in chains
~30×22: four home islands and a neutral mid-sea chain, every island linked by shoal causeways and bridges — so land armies march everywhere on their own feet and naval R1 is satisfied twice over, while navies fight for the crossings that make the marches short. One port and two bases per seat; the causeways are the whole game.
Confluence · everything the game has
The flagship, ~32×24: rivers run from each quadrant into a central sea, and every seat fields all three domains — two bases, an airfield and a port apiece, a harbour island where the rivers meet. The board that shows the whole roster at once; the one a 3v1 challenge match wants, with the lone seat picking the domain the three neglect.

Naming keeps FP6's family feel (Trident, Compass — instruments and geography, no faction words). All eight take the standard header: one-line pitch, no # symmetric tag (it stays a 180° duel instrument per FP6/R5), seat convention noted in a comment. The grand tier deliberately scales economy with area — two bases per seat and a denser city lattice — because a big board on a duel-board income is a slow board, not a grand one; the map lints hold one HQ per seat everywhere, and property parity per seat at whatever count the board sets.

Risks

R1 · The foundation is still on paper

FP1–FP6 are Backlog issues, not merged code. Every mechanism this plan cites from them (the derived roster, allied(), the seat strip) may land differently than written. Mitigation: this plan binds to mechanisms and decisions, never line numbers, and OS1 must not start before FP3 merges — the chain is COM-44→49, then OS1→OS4. If Four Armies is descoped, only §4's boards survive as-is (authored full, played full).

R2 · The Lab's byte-stability bar, again

OS1 touches GameState.create and the capture command — the Lab's substrate. Same standing procedure: a defaulted parameter and a rule (home HQ) that is definitionally today's on a duel board; both gates byte-diffed against main before merge, per docs/balance_sim.md.

R3 · Sparse boards bend the AI's opening

A Compass duel has twice the neutral property of a duel board — the opening becomes a capture race the profiles were not tuned on, and a vacant seat's neutral HQ/base are unusually rich prizes. The soak observes; if the AI misplays the race, the fix is an AIProfile weight, never code — the difficulty plan's standing rule.

R4 · Atoll is the first four-front naval board

Naval R1 (the AI never builds transports) is satisfied by the connected sea, but four navies on one water body is unmeasured. If the soak shows stalls or a dead domain, the fallback is authored, not engineered: shrink the sea's weight in the economy (fewer ports, richer land) rather than touching ai/. Shipping Atoll later than the other two is an acceptable split.

R5 · The grand tier stretches the AI's clock and the game's pacing

Four armies × two bases × a 32×24 board is more units than the planner has ever carried in one turn, and more days than a watch soak has ever waited out. The threat-map cache is per-turn and the planner is O(units × reachable cells), so the cost is real but linear-ish — OS4 measures it before anyone tunes anything. If a grand board drags, the levers are authored in order: economy density (fewer properties), then board size — never a special case in ai/, and never a change to the shared planner for one board's sake. Pacing for humans is what the game-speed setting already owns; nothing here touches it.

R6 · Save compatibility

Low. The envelope already records the roster; the one addition is home_hq, carried under the codec's versioning (READABLE_VERSIONS). A pre-open-seats save decodes with every seat filled and each team's home HQ inferable as the only HQ it owns — exact for every save such a version could have written.

7

Explicitly out of scope

  • Seat swapping and handicaps — choosing to play seat 3 while seat 1 sits empty is already possible (close 1, fill 3); moving a livery or start between seats, or compensating a reduced seating with funds or income, is not. Fairness stays board-authored.
  • Map-file metadata for seatings — no [meta] section, no recommended-pairs syntax. The opposite-seat convention plus the Duel preset carry it; a format extension needs its own case.
  • An N-way symmetry lint — named future work in FP6/R5 and still is.
  • The Balance Lab on multi-seat boards — the Lab stays a duel instrument (Four Armies D8). Open seats make "a duel on Compass" expressible, but the Lab's seat swap and schema stay two-seat; reduced-board balance evidence comes from the OS3 soaks.
  • Retrofitting shipped duel boards to more seats — the thirteen stay duels; widening one is the deliberate, separate decision the map-retrofit plan requires.
  • AI coordination, diplomacy, network play — exactly where Four Armies left them: elsewhere, or nowhere.

Grid Commanders · design of record · proposed · MC1–MC5 · 2026-08-04

More Commanders — four generals, one new seam

Eighteen generals ship today and every one of them is a specialist: armour, artillery, infantry, the coast, the air, the fog, the treasury. Nobody on the roster is simply faster, nobody is simply steadier, and nothing on the board has ever been destroyed by anything other than a shot. This plan adds four generals who fill exactly those gaps — and the last of them brings the first aimed Command Power in the game, a power the player points at a square of ground.

01

The four generals

One per faction, which takes the roster from 5/5/4/4 to 6/6/5/5 and keeps the picker's four columns even. Two of them are deliberately the simplest generals in the game — the roster has no easy pick left in it — and two of them are the most violent.

Aurora Compact

Sera Lark — the open road

Doctrine. Every unit she fields has +1 movement. Infantry, tanks, ships, aircraft — everything, always. That is the whole of it: no combat modifier, no terrain rule, no economy, and no downside anywhere.

Forced March · 10 000 · owner's turn
+1 movement again, so her army moves at +2 for a turn.

She is the roster's one-line general and the first one a new player can hold in their head. Nia Rowan is what happens when the terrain rule is the doctrine; Lark is what happens when there is no rule at all, only distance. Her power is deliberately the smallest on the roster — the passive is what you picked her for.

Hooks: move_bonus, wants_power. Nothing else.

Meridian Coalition

Iona Vance — the even hand

Doctrine. Every unit is +3% attack and +3% defence. Every unit, every domain, every fight. Nothing is specialised and nothing is paid for.

Tip the Scales · 17 000 · one-shot
Her army gains one pip of health. Every hostile army loses one. Nothing dies: a unit already on its last pip keeps it.

A pip is small and the swing is the entire board at once: on a full line of eight that is eight pips gained and eight taken, in one command, before a shot is fired. She never takes a unit off the board — she makes every exchange for the rest of the turn one she wins, which is the even hand doing at scale what her passive does per shot.

Hooks: attack_bonus, defense_bonus, on_power_activated, wants_power.

Verdant League

Ivar Thorne — wounded and dangerous

Doctrine. His units gain +8% attack for every pip of health they are missing — on top of the health scaling every unit in the game already has. That second half is the whole design and the card says it rather than burying it: a unit at 5 of 10 pips already deals half its damage, so Thorne's +40% there does not make it strong — it makes it 0.70 instead of 0.50. He never profits from being hurt. He profits from not going home. (The full table is in §5.)

Cut Both Ways · 18 000 · one-shot
Every unit on the board loses two pips — his, his ally's, every enemy's. Nothing dies: a unit on two pips or less is left standing on one.

Cass Orlov is paid to finish their wounded; Thorne is paid to keep fighting with his. It is the only power in the game a player fires knowing it will hurt their own army, and that is the point of him: everyone else's line is worth less after it, and his is worth more.

Hooks: attack_bonus, on_power_activated, wants_power.

Iron Dominion

Radek Morn — the hammer

Doctrine. Every unit is +5% attack and +5% defence. The same shape as Vance's and simply larger — he is what she is, turned up.

Hammerfall · 24 000 · aimed · one-shot
The player names a cell. The 3×3 around it is annihilated: every unit standing in it is destroyed outright — full health or not, his or theirs, land, sea or air, cargo with its transport.

The most expensive power in the game by 2 000, and it should be: nine tiles of board, cleared, with no roll and no counter. It is also the only power in the game that can fail — aim it a tile off and it lands on empty ground, and the meter is gone.

Hooks: attack_bonus, on_power_activated, wants_power, plus the three new ones D2 adds.

What Hammerfall looks like

[X] [X] [X] . y=6 . . . . . nine tiles, Chebyshev radius 1 a transport in the square goes down with everything it carries an army that loses its last unit here falls, through the rout path that already exists neither meter moves — a death outside a fight banks no charge (D4) units only: an HQ, factory or city in the square is untouched and keeps its owner

The footprint is Chebyshev radius 1 — the eight neighbours and the centre — and it is clipped to the board at the edges, so aiming into a corner is a legal shot that simply catches fewer tiles. That clipping is the one thing that makes a corner aim a real decision.

02

What the seams already give us

The commanders plan's D1 — a general is a subclass in core/commanders/ with its numbers @export on a .tres — has held for eighteen generals. It holds for three of these four with no change to any shared file at all. Writing that down first is what keeps the fourth one's cost visible.

General Needs Exists today? Where
Sera Lark Movement points added to every unit, and again while the power runs Yes CommanderType.move_bonus — Alina Ward, Nia Rowan, Cass Orlov and Halden Marr all use it. MovementResolver.move_budget is the one reader.
Iona Vance A flat attack and defence bonus; a one-shot health swing across the board Yes attack_bonus / defense_bonus, and on_power_activated — Gideon Holt already heals his army in it and Orin Flux already reaches across the table in it.
Ivar Thorne An attack bonus that reads the attacker's own health; a one-shot swing Yes Engagement.attacker_hp is already the effective displayed HP the formula uses — Cass Orlov reads the defender's half of the same object.
Radek Morn A power the player aims at a cell No PowerCommand carries no target, the replay line for a power names nothing, AIController has no cell to choose, and battle.gd has no targeting state for a power. This is D2 and it is the whole of the new machinery.

And one thing that exists that nobody expects to

One of these four powers destroys units without a shot — Hammerfall, and only Hammerfall; the two health powers are both floored at a pip. And the rules already know how to do it. GameState.remove_unit takes the unit's cargo down with it and calls _check_rout, so an army that loses its last unit to a meteor falls exactly as one that loses it to a tank. And no charge moves, because every charge the economy ever banks comes out of three calls inside CombatResolver — TurnRules._lost_to_empty_tank already relies on that when a plane runs its tank dry. So D4 below is a restatement, not new code.

03

Locked decisions

What the milestones may not re-litigate. Each names the alternative it rejects, because a decision with no rejected alternative is a description.

D1 — A general is still a subclass and a .tres, and three of these four prove it

Behaviour in a typed class under core/commanders/, every number @export on data/commanders/. Lark, Vance and Thorne are written entirely against hooks that shipped with the commanders plan, and MC1–MC3 touch no shared file — no new hook on CommanderType, no resolver change, no ai/ change, no save or replay change.

Rejected: widening a hook to make a doctrine read more naturally. The hook tree is eighteen — soon twenty-two — overrides wide, and every widening is paid for twenty-two times. The one new capability this plan adds is D2's, and it is new because there is genuinely no way to point at a square of ground today.

D2 — A power may name a target cell, and PowerCommand is where it is named

One field — PowerCommand.target: Vector2i — and three hooks on CommanderType:

  • aims_power() -> bool — false for every general but Morn. It is what the battle scene asks before entering a targeting state, and what the command validates against.
  • power_blast_cells(state, team, target) -> Array[Vector2i] — the footprint, clipped to the board. Empty by default.
  • power_target(state, team) -> Vector2i — the computer's own aim, asked only by AIController. See D5.

on_power_activated gains a defaulted target parameter rather than growing a sibling hook: an aimed power and an unaimed one are the same concept — what fires the instant the meter is spent — and two hooks for one concept is the seam drifting. The two existing overrides (Orin Flux, Gideon Holt) take the parameter and ignore it.

Rejected: a separate AimedPowerCommand. A power is deliberately just another command — that is what puts it in the log, the save, the replay and the AI with no special case anywhere — and a second class would need a second case in every one of those five places.

D3 — The blast is one function, and the preview is that same function

power_blast_cells is the single authority for which tiles a meteor takes. The overlay paints exactly what it returns, the AI scores exactly what it returns, and on_power_activated destroys exactly what it returns. Nothing anywhere spells "3×3" a second time.

This is the range-preview plan's D1 applied to a power: the fire ring is the single authority's geometry, never a second opinion. The bug that rule exists to prevent is precisely the one available here — a preview that shows nine tiles and a power that clears sixteen.

Any cell on the board is a legal aim point, fog or no fog. The sim stays permissive and fog is enforced in presentation, so the player may drop a meteor into the dark and hope; what fog costs them is knowing what was there. validate refuses a target off the board and nothing else.

D4 — Only Hammerfall kills outside a fight; it banks no charge, and it can end a match

The two health powers are floored at one pip and take nothing off the board. That floor is the rule, not a softening of one: a power that removes units is a different kind of thing from a power that moves numbers, and exactly one general on this roster gets to be that kind of thing. It keeps the meteor singular, and it keeps two of the three new powers out of elimination, rout and charge banking entirely.

Hammerfall alone kills, through GameState.remove_unit. That path banks nothing — charge is minted in three calls inside CombatResolver and nowhere else — so neither the victim's meter nor the firer's moves. The rule already exists for a plane that starves: running yourself dry is not an exchange, and paying the other side charge for it would make starving your own air force a tactic. Being nuked is the same shape.

The consequence is stated rather than hidden: a match can now end without a shot. An army whose last unit is in the blast falls through _check_rout, the path that already fells one whose last unit is shot. Nothing new is needed for that, but two readers have to be checked rather than assumed — BalanceMatchEngine.termination, which is told how a match ended and must not read a power rout as an HQ capture, and the fallen-army banner, which the pipeline reports on BattleCommandReceipt.fallen.

Rejected: banking charge to the victim "in compensation". A meteor that clears five units would hand the opponent a nearly-full meter, so the answer to the most expensive power in the game would be their power — which turns a decisive moment into a trade.

D5 — The computer aims through the doctrine, never through the planner

power_target lives on Morn's subclass beside wants_power, and they are measured with the same function: _best_blast(state, team) returns the cell whose blast is worth the most, in funds, counting hostile units destroyed minus his own and his ally's. wants_power is "that number clears hammer_want_value"; power_target is "that cell". They cannot disagree, which is the failure worth designing out: a commander that wants to fire and then aims somewhere it did not want.

It obeys the doctrine-AI plan's D4 verbatim: it reads only what the planner already reads (enemies through Vision.is_hidden_from), it is pure, integer and RNG-free, it never reads the damage chart — a kill is a kill, there is nothing to forecast — and it is scan-ordered over cells so a replan on one board always agrees with itself. ai/ gains one line: _plan_power setting command.target when the commander aims.

Rejected: scoring the blast in AIUnitActionPlanner. The planner scores moves for units; a meteor is not a unit, has no movement and belongs to no seat on the board.

D6 — A power heals its own army and harms every hostile one — and leaves the ally alone on both counts

Vance's two halves take different questions to different authorities, and the split is the four-players plan's D2 read straight off: allies share sight and purpose, never infrastructure.

  • The heal is state.units_of(team) — his own army. Health is infrastructure: repair, resupply and paid repair all stay owner == unit.team, and an ally's workshop does not mend your tanks.
  • The harm is every unit where not state.allied(unit.team, team) — every hostile army, exactly as Orin Flux's jam reaches them, so on a four-seat board it lands on three opponents at once.

Thorne is the deliberate exception and the exception is the doctrine: Cut Both Ways reaches literally every unit on the board, allies included, because "everyone pays" is what the power is — non-lethally, floored at one pip, which is what lets it be that indiscriminate. Hammerfall likewise takes whatever is standing in the square, and is not floored at all.

D7 — Thorne reads the Engagement, never the Unit

His bonus is computed from fight.attacker_hp — the effective displayed HP the formula is actually using — and never from fight.attacker.hp. The two differ in the case that matters most: a forecast's counter is priced at the defender's projected post-attack health, so reading the live unit would make the damage preview promise a counter-attack the resolver then fires at a different number.

That is the whole reason Engagement carries effective values instead of two Units, and Thorne is the first doctrine to read the attacker's half of it.

D8 — Lark's and Vance's passives are unpaid on purpose, and the retune lever is the number

Every strong passive on the roster is paid for somewhere: Draeg's armour costs him his infantry, Rhea Sol's guns are soft, Mara Voss is bad at starting fights, Tomas Reed's vehicles are weak. Lark's +1 movement and Vance's +3/+3 cost nothing anywhere — and neither does Morn's +5/+5 — and that is what they are for — the roster has no general a new player can pick without also picking a weakness. Perrin Ash is the precedent: a doctrine with no downside, priced by being narrow instead.

So they are the two most likely to land outside the win band, and when they do the lever is the number on the .tres — 5 becomes 4, or Forced March gets dearer. Rejected: bolting on a penalty to bring them into line. A penalty would make them the specialists they exist not to be, and there are eighteen specialists already.

04

Milestones

Ordered cheapest-first, which is also least-risky-first: three generals ship against seams that already exist before anything shared is touched. Every milestone ends with a playable general and a green make verify.

MC1

Sera Lark — the roster grows to nineteen

no shared file touched

The smallest general it is possible to write, shipped first precisely because it proves the seam needs nothing. move_bonus returns her passive plus her power's, and wants_power follows Nia Rowan's shape — the offensive read or a property her infantry can reach with the march's own movement, because a movement power measured only in fights banks its meter all match on an empty map.

  • core/commanders/sera_lark.gd + data/commanders/sera_lark.tres
  • Portrait via make portraits; three power quotes under the 60-character cap
  • tests/unit/test_sera_lark.gd: the budget moves for every domain, the power stacks to +2, fuel is spent for the steps actually taken

Gate. make verify green; make commander-balance BAL="--commanders=sera_lark,..." focused run reads sane.

MC2

Iona Vance — the board-wide swing

no shared file touched

The first power that reaches every unit at once, and the first to take two different questions to two different authorities: the heal asks units_of(team) and the harm asks state.allied, which is D6. Both halves are clamped rather than unbounded — full health at the top, one pip at the bottom — so nothing enters or leaves the board and remove_unit is never called.

  • wants_power: the pips the swing would actually move — an army already at full health and an opponent already at one pip are both worth nothing. Enemies read through Vision.is_hidden_from, like every other doctrine gate
  • tests/unit/test_iona_vance.gd: the pip swing on both sides; the cap at full health; a hostile unit on one pip still standing on one pip; an ally untouched by both halves; and a four-army board where the harm lands on all three opponents (R5)

Gate. make verify green; a headless four-army soak in which Vance fires at least once and the match resolves.

MC3

Ivar Thorne — the wounded doctrine

reuses MC2's rules

attack_bonus off fight.attacker_hp per D7, and Cut Both Ways as a board-wide, non-lethal version of MC2's swing — the same clamp, two pips deep, and reaching allies as well as enemies (D6). That floor is what makes his wants_power the simplest of the four: there are no losses to weigh, so it asks only whether the two sides are in contact and whether there is an enemy army out there worth bleeding. A gate any more clever than that would bank the meter all match, which is the failure the whole toolkit exists to avoid.

  • tests/unit/test_ivar_thorne.gd: the per-pip bonus at 10, 5 and 1 pips; the forecast and the resolved shot agreeing, counter included (D7); the power reaching his own army, his ally's and every enemy's; and a unit on one pip still standing on one pip afterwards, which is the floor's whole bar

Gate. make verify green; the damage preview and the resolved exchange identical for a wounded attacker, which is the D7 bar.

MC4

Radek Morn — and the first aimed power

the machinery

The machinery and its one user ship together, because targeting with nobody to target is code no one can play. This is also the only milestone that removes a unit without a shot, so it owns D4 outright and the two implementation rules that come with it: collect the doomed, then remove them — state.units is being iterated and remove_unit mutates it — and nothing is banked to either meter. Five layers move, and no more than five:

core/PowerCommand.target; validate refuses an off-board aim; on_power_activated gains the defaulted parameter; three hooks on CommanderType (D2)
replayREQUIRED_KEYS["power"] = ["target"] and FORMAT 1 → 2. Older replays are refused, which is the replay plan's D3 — a replay is disposable, so a stale one is deleted rather than migrated
ai/one line in _plan_power: set the target from power_target when the commander aims (D5)
scenes/a power-targeting state in battle.gd; the 3×3 preview painted by BattleOverlays (field-overlays D3 — anything laid over the board is the overlays'); cursor movement through DirectionalInput, confirm/cancel through the existing convention, cancel landing on rest_state()
save/nothing. The blast is one-shot and power_active is already saved
  • tests/unit/test_radek_morn.gd: the footprint clipped at a corner; cargo dying with its transport; an ally's unit dying too; no charge banked on either side; an army losing its last unit in the blast falling through _check_rout; and the computer's aim being the highest-value net square, matching its own wants_power
  • Check BalanceMatchEngine.termination reads a power rout correctly rather than as an HQ capture (D4, R4)
  • tests/unit/test_replay_fidelity.gd extended: a recorded match containing an aimed power replays to an identical final board — the merge bar for the format bump
  • A power_targeting smoke scenario capturing the preview

Gate. make verify green; replay fidelity with an aimed power; make smoke green with the new frame; every other power's line still decodes and applies unchanged.

MC5

The balance gate at twenty-two

adds no code

The full matrix: 22 × 22 ordered pairs × 5 scenarios × 4 seeds = 9 680 matches, up from 6 480 — a release task, not a merge gate, and deliberately out of make verify. The committed artifacts are the tuned .tres files and docs/commander_balance.md, never the generated report.

The only numbers this milestone may move are the four new generals' own. It may not touch data/ai/ — that is the balance retune's BL2, and the difficulty ladder is already failing knowingly against it.

Gate. All four inside the preferred win band, or a written reason in docs/commander_balance.md why a flagged one ships anyway — the AI is rule-based and a commander outside the band is a review trigger, not an automatic nerf. Plus the human test deck note for Hammerfall specifically, which no matrix can judge: whether aiming it is a decision or a formality.

The two flat doctrines are the same shape, one turned up

Vance and Morn deliberately read on one scale, so the ordering the brief asked for is visible in the numbers rather than asserted in the prose. Both terms count: the defence half is worth as much per point as the attack half, because the formula's (200 - def) / 100 shape makes +3 defence a x0.97 on damage taken exactly as +3 attack is a x1.03 on damage dealt.

Vance deals x1.03 takes x0.97 → ~6 points of swing per exchange Morn deals x1.05 takes x0.95 → ~10 points of swing per exchange

So Morn's army is worth roughly two-thirds again what Vance's is in a straight exchange, before either power is counted — and then his power is the most expensive in the game and hers is a pip. That is the whole of what separates them, which is the point: two generals with no specialism at all, one of whom is simply better at fighting and pays for it in how long his meter takes.

Neither number is load-bearing on anything but feel, so both are MC5's to move — and moving one without the other is what would put them back out of order.

Why Thorne's bonus is +8% per pip and not +25%

The damage formula already multiplies by attacker_displayed_hp / 10, so a wounded unit hits weaker before any doctrine applies. Thorne does not reverse that — he softens it.

HP plain unit Thorne at +8%/pip 10 1.00 1.00 x1.00 7 0.70 0.70 x 1.24 = 0.87 5 0.50 0.50 x 1.40 = 0.70 2 0.20 0.20 x 1.64 = 0.33 1 0.10 0.10 x 1.72 = 0.17

So a half-dead unit of his does 70% of a full one's damage instead of 50%. He never profits from being hurt in absolute terms — he profits from not going home, which is the doctrine in one line: everyone else rotates a wounded tank back to a workshop, and his stays in the line and is still worth something there.

The number to not reach for is the one that cancels the scaling. Making a 5-pip unit hit like a full one takes +100% at 5 pips — twenty points per pip, and at that setting the column above flattens to 1.00 everywhere: health stops meaning anything for his army, and deliberately shooting your own tanks becomes the optimal opening. +8% keeps the curve sloping the right way and only makes it shallower, which is the difference between a doctrine and a broken rule.

And why Cut Both Ways is still worth firing

Two pips off his own army costs him output — but at +8% per pip it also buys him +16%, so a full-health unit of his ends the exchange at 0.8 × 1.16 = 0.93 of its damage while every enemy unit is flatly at 0.8. He gives up 7% and takes 20%.

And because it never kills, that is the entire transaction — there is no downside case where he hands the opponent a unit. What he is buying is a board where every enemy is permanently weaker and every unit of his is permanently better than it looks, which is why it is dearer than Vance's despite taking nothing off the board. It is the power you fire when you are winning the attrition and want it decided.

06

Risk register

R1 — Hammerfall lands in the same build as the AI's cohesion term

The AI Judgement plan's AJ3 shipped cohesion_tiles live on every tier, and its own measurement found tight beat loose — the computer now advances in a column on purpose. This plan ships the one power that deletes a column. AJ3's R1 (the clump being artillery and Command Power bait) was explicitly left unobserved rather than refuted, because both sides were the same planner and neither punished concentration. Morn is the instrument that punishes it.
Watch: Morn's rows in the MC5 matrix, and the funds destroyed per firing. The lever if it is too good is Morn's .tres, not the AI dials — those belong to BL2.

R2 — Lark's +1 movement is the strongest unpaid passive on the roster

It touches every unit, every domain and every turn of the match, and D8 forbids paying for it with a penalty. Movement also compounds with the things this repo has already measured as decisive — the property race, capture claims, reaching a workshop.
Watch: her win rate and, in the Balance Lab, her property count rather than her army value. If she has to come down, the honest lever is Forced March getting dearer or the power being removed entirely — a general whose only trick is the passive does not need a second one.

R3 — The gate grows by half

6 480 → 9 680 matches, roughly +49% wall clock on a run that is already a release task. Focused runs (BAL="--commanders=...") are the iteration loop for MC1–MC4 and the full matrix is MC5's alone.

R4 — Hammerfall can end a match, and two readers have to be told

Covered by D4 and by _check_rout, but BalanceMatchEngine.termination is told how a match ended rather than inferring it, and the fallen-army banner rides on BattleCommandReceipt.fallen. Both are checked in MC4 rather than assumed, because a power rout misread as an HQ capture silently corrupts every balance report afterwards. The risk shrank when the two health powers were floored — it is now one power on one milestone rather than three across three.

R5 — Board-wide powers scale with the number of hostile armies

Vance's harm and Thorne's bleed reach every hostile army, so on a four-seat board they are worth up to three times what they are in a duel, while their charge cost is the same. Orin Flux's Signal Jam already has this shape and the roster lives with it. Neither is lethal, which is what keeps this a scaling problem rather than a snowball.
Watch: the alliance soak, not the matrix — the matrix is duels.

R6 — Thorne makes every forecast vary with the attacker's health

The bonus is an integer read on an object the resolver already builds, so it costs nothing to evaluate. What is untested is the planner: _attack_score and the retreat threshold were tuned against doctrines where a wounded unit is strictly worse, and Thorne's is not. A planner that keeps sending wounded units in is correct for him and looks like a bug.

R7 — The picker and the info sheet grow to 22

The gallery is a fixed 4-column grid, so 22 cards is six rows rather than a layout change, and the select panel walks CommanderDB.all() unbounded. But the commander_info and gallery smoke frames both move, and CommanderInfoSheet.layout_error exists precisely because a collapsed sheet still writes a healthy PNG.

R8 — Aimed powers are a new interaction with no convention yet

Confirm, cancel, and what the cursor does are all already decided for attack targeting; MC4 reuses that vocabulary rather than inventing one. The open question no test answers is whether a player who has never seen it understands that the meter is spent whether or not the square had anything in it — a first-contact question for the UX recovery plan's register, not for the matrix.

07

Out of scope

  • Any value in data/ai/. The difficulty ladder is failing knowingly and closing it is the balance retune's BL2. This plan inherits that boundary from the AI Judgement and AI Economy plans verbatim: it owns four commanders' numbers and nothing else.
  • A second aimed power. D2's machinery is general, but one user is what proves it; a second doctrine wanting a different footprint is the moment to find out whether power_blast_cells is the right shape.
  • New unit types, terrain or boards. Every one of these four plays on the shipped roster.
  • A campaign appearance. The Ember Line treatment is closed and casting is its own decision.
  • Retuning the eighteen. MC5 may move only the four new .tres files. If a new general exposes an old one as mispriced, that is a finding for the next balance pass, recorded in docs/commander_balance.md and not fixed here.
  • Battle cut-in art for the powers. Hammerfall gets the existing activation banner and the ordinary destruction path. A meteor animation is a presentation slice of its own, and the battle-animations plan's D1 — the cut-in replays a snapshot and computes nothing — is what it would be built against.

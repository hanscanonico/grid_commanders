Grid Commanders · campaign mode · design of record · rev 1 · 2026-08-15 · proposed — nothing shipped

Standard Missions

Beating the other side, as the default — not the exception.

Every one of the 108 missions is already winnable by routing the enemy army or taking their home headquarters — MissionRuntime answers tactical victory at step 4, above the objective list. But only 15 of 108 actually ask for it. The rest ask for a named city, a property count, or a survival clock, and 85 of 108 carry a hard day deadline that makes the ordinary win unreachable in practice. The campaign reads as 108 errands rather than as a war.

This plan makes the genre-standard mission the default. It converts 53 missions to an enemy-HQ primary, demotes 61 deadlines from failure to pressure, and leaves 38 deliberately special — the defenses, the escorts and the escapes that earn their verb. It is content only: no file under core/ or ai/ changes, and no rule, objective class or verdict order moves.

Section 1

The finding

The campaign-depth plan diagnosed "86 of 108 primaries are CaptureCell" and fixed it by widening the vocabulary. It worked — eleven objective types are in use where two were. But the pass over-corrected in one specific way: it treated the ordinary beat-the-other-side mission as one flavour among eleven, when in this genre it is the baseline the other ten are measured against. Advance Wars is essentially 100% rout-or-HQ, and its variety lives in the boards. Ours lives in the objective cards, and the boards underneath them all get played the same cautious way, because a deadline is counting.

Zero missions ask for a pure rout. The one DefeatTeamObjective in the tree is a bonus, never a primary.

The deadline is the real blocker

85 of 108 missions carry a DayDeadlineObjective in failures. The median deadline sits 3 days past par_day — and all 108 missions already set par_day, which MissionRuntime already turns into a speed star.

So the clock is stated twice: once as a star the player chases, once as a loss three days behind it. On a capture-a-city mission with a day-10 deadline, "just beat them" is legal and practically impossible — which is why re-labelling objectives without touching deadlines would change nothing about how the missions play.

Deadline days in use: 4 (×2), 5, 6 (×3), 7 (×4), 8 (×16), 9 (×18), 10 (×27), 11 (×8), 12 (×5), 14.

Section 2

What "standard" already means, and why this is content only

MissionRuntime's precedence puts tactical victory at step 4, above the objective list, with a comment that already states this plan's whole premise: 

## **Tactical victory outranks the objective list, which is what an author has to
## know**: an objective whose own completion ends the match — the last hostile
## army's home headquarters, or that army itself — is answered at step 4, before
## a single objective is read, so every objective standing beside it is
## decorative. A mission about the enemy's headquarters has exactly one primary
## and states the rest as bonuses.

Two consequences settle the shape of the work. First, nothing under core/ needs to change: routing the enemy already wins every mission, so this plan edits what a mission asks for, never what it allows. Second, an enemy-HQ primary must stand alone — MissionDefinition.board_error already refuses the other shape — so every converted mission's old primary becomes a bonus_objective, which is also where its star goes.

That second point is a quiet win: 51 of 108 missions currently offer zero bonus objectives, so the conversion hands most of them their first one for free, and the star count per mission rises without a single new authored idea.

Section 3

The five classes

Every mission falls into exactly one class, decided by two facts read off its own board: does it have a two-sided production economy, and is its primary time-shaped?

A0 Already standard — 15 missions

Primary is already the enemy HQ, stated alone. Untouched by SM1–SM3; they only lose their deadline in SM2.

A Convert, no board edit — 33 missions

Primary is capture-a-city or own-N-properties, and the board already gives both sides a base. The enemy HQ is reachable and the economy can sustain a real fight, so the conversion is a .tres edit and nothing else: the old primary moves down into bonus_objectives and a CaptureCellObjective naming the enemy HQ takes its place.

B Convert, board needs a base pair — 20 missions

Same primary shape, but the board gives fewer than two sides a base — 23 boards have no base at all. A rout mission with no production is a one-punch skirmish, so these need a symmetric base pair added before conversion. Per the map-retrofit rule, that is a conversion of existing land cells, never a carve: a plains or road cell becomes a base, no cell becomes sea, no coastline moves, and land stays passable to every land class — which is what keeps saves that name these boards loadable.

17 of the 20 currently ask for a city; 3 ask for a property count.

C Standard-adjacent, left alone — 2 missions

Primary is DestroyUnitObjective — kill the named marshal. This is the genre's "defeat boss", which reads as beating the other side and counts toward the headline number without an edit. Slag Row and The Column in the Pines.

S Stays special — 38 missions

Primary is time-shaped or positional: survive to day N (15 alone), hold a cell, protect a unit, keep an ally alive, reach a cell. These are the missions the campaign-depth vocabulary was built for and the ones a reviewer would miss if they went. They keep their deadlines — on a defense the clock is the mission.

Result

70 of 108 (65%) become standard missions, 38 stay special. The genre ratio without deleting the variety that was paid for.

Section 4

Locked decisions

D1
This plan changes what a mission asks, never what it allows.

Tactical victory is already available on all 108 boards and already outranks the objective list. No objective class, no verdict order, no rule and no file under core/ or ai/ is touched. The rejected alternative is a new RoutEnemyObjective: it would be a second authority for a verdict MissionRuntime step 4 already reaches, and every one of its instances would be decorative by that class's own comment.

D2
A converted mission states the enemy HQ alone; the old primary becomes a bonus.

Forced by MissionDefinition.board_error, which already refuses an HQ primary standing beside another. The demotion is not a consolation — it is where the mission's star goes, and it is why 51 missions gain their first bonus objective. Deleting the old primary outright is the rejected alternative: the authored line is the mission's flavour and its star, and both survive the move.

D3
par_day is the pressure; day_deadline is a failure, and a failure needs a reason.

All 108 missions already set par_day, and MissionRuntime already pays a star for beating it. A deadline therefore stays only where a time-shaped primary makes the clock the mission's own subject — a defense, a hold, an escort. Everywhere else it is a second statement of the same pressure with a loss attached, and it goes. 61 drop, 24 stay, 23 never had one. Where a converted mission's authored briefing names the clock, the line moves to the bonus objective's text rather than being cut.

D4
A board retrofit converts cells; it never carves.

The map-retrofit plan's standing rule, applied to class B: a base is added by converting an existing plains or road cell, symmetrically for both sides, at a distance from each HQ that is equal within one tile. No cell becomes sea, no coastline is redrawn, land stays passable to every land class — because a save stores its board by map_path and reloads the edited file from res://. The rejected alternative is authoring new boards for these 20 missions: their terrain is the mission's identity and the authored dialogue names it.

D5
Difficulty is not retuned to compensate.

A mission that becomes a full fight is a longer mission, not a harder one — the tier stays whatever it shipped (14 easy / 46 normal / 48 hard). If SM5's play-through finds a converted mission unwinnable, the answer is its par_day or its board's starting army, never its AIProfile tier: a campaign that ships its own AI numbers is balancing the planner through the back door, which the campaign-mode entry already forbids.

D6
The Long Front is exempt by design.

Nine of its eighteen missions are survive-shaped and eight of its boards carry no production at all — it is the campaign that already reads as a fighting retreat, and it is the one whose objective spread the other five should be measured against rather than converted toward. It contributes 9 standard missions of 18 after SM3 and that is its ceiling.

Section 5

Milestones

SM1 Convert the 33 class-A missions — one PR per campaign

For each: add a CaptureCellObjective naming the enemy home HQ as the sole primary, move the old primary into bonus_objectives with its authored text intact, leave failures alone for now. Six PRs, split by campaign, because the campaign is the unit a reviewer can read end to end. Gate: make campaigns plus tests/unit/test_campaign_content.gd.

Six Marshals 11 · The Collection 12 · Hollow Crown 4 · Furnace Winter 3 · Quiet War 3 · Long Front 0.

SM2 Demote 61 deadlines from failure to pressure

Delete the DayDeadlineObjective from failures on every mission whose primary is not time-shaped. Where the deadline day carried authored meaning, restate it as a DayDeadlineObjective in bonus_objectives — the same class, judged as a star rather than a loss. This milestone is independent of SM1 and could ship first; the order below runs it second only because a converted mission is the clearest place to see the change.

Deliverable beyond the edits: a note in docs/campaign_authoring.md stating D3, so the next author does not reach for a deadline by reflex.

SM3 Retrofit the 20 class-B boards with a base pair, then convert them

Per D4: convert one plains or road cell near each side's HQ into a base, equal within a tile, then run SM1's edit on the mission. The board's header comment gains a line recording that the pair was added and why. Gate: make campaigns, tests/unit/test_maps.gd, and the mission played to a verdict in test_campaign_soak.gd.

Long Front 8 · Quiet War 7 · Furnace Winter 4 · Six Marshals 1. Highest-risk milestone: it is the only one that touches a board.

SM4 Hollow Crown's verb spread

Before this plan, all 18 of its missions carried own_properties + a deadline and 16 also carried capture_cell — every mission the same shape. After SM1–SM3 it reads 13 standard / 5 special, which fixes the monotony in one direction. This milestone spends the freed bonus slots on variety instead: the property counts and city captures demoted in SM1 become the war's bonus texture, and two of its five specials are re-pointed at verbs the war never uses.

SM5 Play it and record the reading

The soak asserts legality, never winnability — so the acceptance for this plan is a human play-through of one converted mission per campaign plus every class-B board, recorded in a new docs/campaign_missions.md alongside the final per-class counts. The one number that decides the plan succeeded: a converted mission should take longer than its old deadline and still feel finishable.

Section 7

Risks

R1 · A converted mission is longer, and 54 boards are under ~170 cells.

Half the campaign boards are small. A rout mission on a 15×9 board is over in six days, which is fine; the risk runs the other way, on boards where removing the deadline lets a cautious player grind a stalemate. Mitigation: par_day stays and still pays a star, and SM5 measures the actual day counts rather than predicting them.

R2 · Authored dialogue names the old objective.

273 authored lines, and a briefing that says "take the crossing post" is wrong if the crossing post is now a bonus. Mitigation: the demoted objective keeps its own text, so the card still names it — but SM1's per-campaign PR must read every briefing line in that campaign, which is why the split is by campaign rather than by class.

R3 · Class B's base pair changes a balanced board.

Adding production to a board authored without it is the largest change in the plan, and 23 of these boards were deliberately production-free — the production-maps plan's D3 shows that is sometimes a decision rather than an omission. Mitigation: SM3 is last of the three edit milestones and separable; if a board's comment says the absence is deliberate, that mission drops to class S rather than getting a base.

R4 · Deadlines were load-bearing for a scripted beat.

416 events, 84 of them on a DayReached trigger. Removing a day-10 failure from a mission whose day-9 reinforcement wave assumed the player was nearly out of time changes what that wave means. Mitigation: SM2 checks every dropped deadline against that mission's own DayReached triggers and flags any beat scheduled within two days of it for a read.

R5 · The headline number is the goal, and goals get gamed.

70/108 is a measurement, not a target — a class-S mission converted to make the ratio look better is exactly the variety this plan claims to preserve being spent on its own scoreboard. Mitigation: the class is decided by the two board facts in section 3, and D6 names the campaign that is exempt.

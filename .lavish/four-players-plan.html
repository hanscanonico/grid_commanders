Grid Commanders · New capability · Shipped · rev 2

Four Armies — Free-for-All, 2v2, or 3v1

Every match today is a duel: core/game_state.gd:7 pins TEAMS to [1, 2] and everything downstream — the win condition, the save file, the menu, the colour fallbacks — leans on there being exactly one opponent. This plan seats up to four armies, with the map deciding how many play and the match deciding how they group: every army for itself, two against two, or three against one. Allies share sight, pass through each other's lines, and win together; everything else — funds, production, repair — stays each army's own. The survey's headline is good news: turn order is already modulo-N, the map format already parses any team, the AI already fights "everyone who isn't me" — the duel is an assumption of the data, far more than of the code.

What the survey found — closer than it looks

A four-layer sweep (sim, data, presentation, AI/tools) found that most of the engine already loops a team list rather than assuming a pair. The genuine two-player knots are a short, enumerable set:

Already N-army, verbatim

  • Turn order — game_state.gd:238 cycles TEAMS[(find + 1) % size]; the day advances on wrap to the first team (end_turn_command.gd:16). Modulo-N today.
  • Economy, commanders, vision — funds, CO slots, capture progress and fog are all per-team dictionaries; core/rules/vision.gd answers one explicit viewer team at a time.
  • The map format — [owners] / [units] rows carry a free team int; the parser (map_data.gd:168) floors it at 1 and bounds nothing. A 4-army map parses today.
  • MatchConfig — ai_teams is already an Array[int], commanders already a per-team dictionary.
  • The AI — ai_controller.gd:143 defines the enemy as "every unit not mine", never 1 − team. One planner per team already exists (battle.gd:181), and watch mode already sets every seat to AI (battle_setup.gd:96).
  • The art — four tinted faction rows ship in every atlas (FACTION_ROWS = 4, side_identity.gd:32), and the ordinal army names already read "First" through "Fourth". Twelve commanders, three per faction.
  • Cut-ins and EventBus — combat and capture cut-ins are role-based (attacker/defender), resolved through SideIdentity; every bus signal carries a plain team int.

The genuine two-player knots

  • The constant itself — const TEAMS := [1, 2] (game_state.gd:7). Every correct loop widens the moment this grows; nothing can grow it per match.
  • The win condition is a duel — rout hands victory to the first other team in the list (game_state.gd:243), and capturing any HQ ends the whole match (capture_command.gd:70). With three armies, side 2 dying would crown side 1 while side 3 stands. Elimination is not a modelled state at all.
  • Hostility is re-derived everywhere — "enemy" means team != mine, independently, in the attack and move commands, the movement blocker, vision's jamming loop, the threat map and the target sweep. Right answer for a duel and for a free-for-all; there is no seam an ally could enter through.
  • One hardcoded save line — the funds encoder writes a literal 2-key dictionary (save_codec.gd:120); the decoder beside it already loops. Teams 3–4 would be silently dropped on write.
  • _opponent_of — "the first team that is not this one" (commander_type.gd:222), gating Orin Flux, Mara Voss and Sable Wren's powers against one arbitrary rival.
  • Colour fallback runs dry — _GENERIC_ORDER and _MIRROR_ORDER hold two classics each (side_identity.gd:41,46); a third collision resolves to neutral grey, violating the file's own "no two sides share a colour" invariant.
  • The menu is a two-slot machine — "1 Player"/"2 Player" hardcode the AI roster (main_menu.gd:100), commander select is an enum Side { RED, BLUE } ping-pong (commander_select_panel.gd:23), and the info sheet opens exactly two cards (commander_info_sheet.gd:36).
  • Mixed-seat fog leaks — the viewer during an AI turn is "the first human team" (battle.gd:768), and no handoff fires across an intervening AI turn — with two humans and two CPUs, player B watches through player A's fog.
  • The Balance Lab is two-seat by design — swap_team is a literal 1↔2, tiebreak compares properties_of(1) to (2) (match_engine.gd:166–183), and the report schema is red_*/blue_* columns.

The test blast radius, measured

Of 58 unit-test files, ~42 build inline 2-team boards — but they build them through MapData.parse, so under a per-map roster they remain valid 2-army scenarios and keep passing. The files that actually break are the ones that assert the pair: test_maps.gd:55 (exactly TEAMS.size() HQs, one per team — growing a global constant to 4 would red-build every shipped map at once, which is the single sharpest argument for the roster being per-map), the 2-slot test_side_identity.gd, the tiebreak and recorder-census tests in the Lab, and the doctrine tests that pin _opponent_of. Roughly 8–10 files need rework; the rest need 3/4-army additions, not repair.

The design — the map seats the armies, the match takes sides

The roster becomes match state

GameState.teams: Array[int], derived by GameState.create from the map's own [owners] and [units] sections — data the parser already reads and throws away. The parser gains the bounds it never had (1 ≤ team ≤ 4, contiguous from 1) and MapData exposes player_count, which the menu prints beside the size and uses to shape the seat strip. A two-army map produces exactly today's state — which is what keeps every existing map, test fixture and balance report honest.

Sides are the match's choice

How the armies group is picked at setup, not baked into the board: the same 4-army map hosts a free-for-all, a 2v2, or a 3v1. One new sim fact — state.allied(a, b) — becomes the single hostility authority, and every scattered team != mine asks it instead. Allies share vision, pass through each other, and cannot target or capture each other. Funds, production, repair and transports stay strictly per-army: allies share sight and purpose, never infrastructure. Free-for-all is simply the grouping where every army is its own side — the authority answers exactly what the old expression answered, which is the regression guarantee.

Elimination, then victory

A new modelled state: GameState.eliminated. An army falls when its last unit dies or its HQ is captured — the HQ falling now removes that army's units and returns its properties to neutral instead of ending the match. next_team() skips the fallen, income never accrues to them, and the day advances when the hand reaches the first surviving army. The match ends when every surviving army stands on one side — in a duel, the first elimination is the victory, and both paths collapse into today's behaviour exactly.

# Compass, 2v2 — sides {1+3} vs {2+4} Day 12  →  1 Meridian ▸ 2 Aurora ▸ 3 Iron ▸ 4 Verdant 1 and 3 share fog; 2 and 4 share fog; funds stay separate 4's HQ captured by 3  ⇒  4 eliminated:   units removed · properties → neutral · banner plays survivors {1, 3} vs {2}  ⇒  two sides remain, match continues 2 routed  ⇒  survivors {1, 3}, one side  ⇒  Meridian & Iron win

What the AI knows about sides

The AI's enemy set comes from the same authority — it will never fire on an ally, never fear one in its threat map, and never path around one as a blocker. What it will not do is coordinate: an allied AI counts its partner's guns toward nothing, plans no joint focus fire, and picks its own front. That is accepted and named (§5 R3) — the FFA and the alliance modes ship with an honest army that fights its own war beside yours, and "fight together" is future work with its own plan.

3

Decisions

D1 · The map is the roster authority; the match is the sides authority proposed

The number of armies is a property of the board, read from the map file — never a menu setting. Rejected: growing the constant to [1, 2, 3, 4] globally, which would seed four funds entries and four CO slots into every duel, fail the one-HQ-per-team lint on all thirteen shipped duel boards at once, and make a 2-player save claim teams that never played. The grouping is the opposite: pure match setup, chosen at the seat strip, carried as MatchConfig's one new field — sides: Dictionary (team → side id), empty meaning free-for-all. The same board hosts every mode; a board cannot demand an alliance. ai_teams and commanders stay as they are — already per-team collections.

D2 · One hostility authority: state.allied(a, b) proposed

The vision-authority rule applied to allegiance: ask it, never re-derive. Every site that today writes team != mine routes through the authority — attack_command.gd target validation, capture_command.gd (an ally's property is not capturable), move_command.gd / movement_resolver.gd (allies pass through like your own units, and an ally is never an ambush), game_state.gd:213's blocker check, the AI's _visible_enemies and threat_map.gd, and the doctrine hooks via _opponents_of() → Array[int] — "every team hostile to mine", one element in a duel so every shipped doctrine behaviour is pinned unchanged. Shared sight: core/rules/vision.gd computes a side's visible set as the union over its member armies — the one place fog composes, so BattlePerspective and the AI's fog-limited pathing inherit it for free. Deliberately not shared: funds, build menus, repair/resupply (services stay owner == unit.team), join, and transport loading. Allies also still block each other from stopping on an occupied cell, exactly like your own units.

D3 · Elimination is a modelled state; victory belongs to a side proposed

Rout and HQ capture both mark an army eliminated: its units leave the board, its properties revert to neutral (re-capturable — fallen empires are contested ground, and handing them wholesale to the conqueror would snowball an already-winning side), and an elimination banner plays for everyone — public information, fog or no fog. The match ends when every surviving army shares one side; winner keeps its scalar meaning (the winning side's lead army) so every command's winner != 0 gate and watch mode's diffable watch: team %d wins on day %d line survive untouched, and a new winners() lists the whole side for presentation. Consequence for the AI: hq_capture_multiplier's ×3 was priced when an HQ ended the match; it now buys an elimination, still the best purchase on the map, so the weight stands until the FP6 soak says otherwise — an AIProfile number, tunable in data.

D4 · Turn order is seat order, whatever the sides proposed

Armies play in roster order — 1, 2, 3, 4 — regardless of grouping, exactly the modulo-N rotation that exists today. Alternation is therefore a setup choice: a 2v2 grouped 1+3 vs 2+4 alternates sides every turn; grouped 1+2 vs 3+4 it plays in blocks. The seat strip makes the grouping visible before the match starts, and the genre precedent (allies play in seat order) says this is a feature, not a gap. Rejected: re-sorting the rotation so allies always alternate — it would silently reorder the map's authored seat positions and break "a 2-army map produces exactly today's state".

D5 · Four liveries on screen; the art budget is exactly four proposed

Each army keeps its own faction livery — an alliance is a relationship, not a paint job, so a 2v2 shows four colours and the UI says who stands with whom. _GENERIC_ORDER and _MIRROR_ORDER extend to all four theme keys — existing first two entries untouched, so every 2-player resolution (mirrors, commander-less sides, the borrow rules the tests pin) stays byte-identical, and iron/verdant join as third and fourth choices. With four keys and at most four armies, "no two sides share a colour" becomes provable, and the neutral-grey escape hatch in _fallback becomes genuinely unreachable. FACTION_ROWS = 4 is exactly the budget and this plan spends all of it. Everything downstream — cut-ins, thumbnails, fogged-owner memory, HUD chips — already asks SideIdentity and inherits the guarantee for free; the bottom bar's unit card gains one word of allegiance ("Ally"/"Enemy") relative to the viewer.

D6 · Seats replace modes: a seat strip with side badges proposed

Picking a map shows one row per seat — Human or CPU, plus a cycling side badge (A/B/C/D) that expresses the grouping; one-tap presets cover the named modes (Free-for-all, 2v2, 3v1) on 4-seat maps, and setup validates at least two sides. Defaults keep the one-click paths: seat 1 human, the rest CPU, everyone their own side. The difficulty dropdown stays match-wide and applies to every CPU seat; per-seat tiers remain the Balance Lab's CLI grammar, whose plumbing (BuiltMatch.per_team_difficulty, one planner per team) already works per seat. Commander select sheds enum Side {RED, BLUE} for a slot walk over the map's roster — same page, N confirm steps, back rewinding one seat — and confirmed emits a per-team dictionary that main_menu.gd passes through instead of the literal {1: red, 2: blue}. CLI: --co=a,b,c,d already maps positionally over the roster; --sides=1+3v2+4 is the one new flag (groups joined by +, separated by v), and --red/--blue stay exactly what the faction plan's D5 says they are — developer vocabulary for seats 1 and 2.

D7 · The viewer is the last human in the chain; handoff fires human-to-human proposed

Two policy fixes close the mixed-seat fog leak. Handoff: the blackout fires whenever a human turn begins and the previous human seat was a different player — regardless of how many AI turns ran in between — replacing "more than one human and the very next team is human". Viewer: during AI turns the board renders through the fog of the human who just played (information they already had), not TEAMS[0]'s. The rule stays uniform across sides — allied humans share a fog union anyway, so their blackout is cheap theatre, and hostile humans are exactly who it protects. One human at the table keeps today's behaviour exactly: their fog all match, no blackouts.

D8 · The Balance Lab stays a duel instrument proposed

The Lab's schema (red_*/blue_* columns), seat swap, tiebreak and side-bias math are two-seat to the bone, and two committed documents rest on its byte-stable output. It keeps measuring duels — on 2-army maps its state, seed stream and reports are bit-for-bit today's, and the merge bar for FP1–FP3 is the fixed-seed byte-diff of both shipped gates, as the balance plan's D1 demands. What N-army balance evidence needs is watch-mode soaks (--watch already seats every army with an AI, and --sides= gives it the groupings), not a four-way report schema — a 4-seat Lab axis is out of scope, additive later if wanted. One Lab-internal fix does land: the recorder's kill attribution stops crediting "the other team" and records the victim's team on the event, which the census check demands the moment a third army exists.

Milestones — each one shippable

FP1 · The roster becomes data size M shipped

Teams flow from the map into a per-match roster; nothing a player can reach changes. The whole milestone is provably inert on 2-army maps.

  • core/map_data.gd — validate 1 ≤ team ≤ 4 and contiguity, derive player_count / teams() from [owners] + [units]; a bad team fails at parse, not at test time.
  • core/game_state.gd — var teams seeded by create() from the map; current_team = teams[0]; the ~40 GameState.TEAMS call sites move to the instance (mechanical; the constant remains as the legal maximum).
  • core/save_codec.gd — funds encoder loops the roster (the one crashing line), the envelope records teams, version 3→4; a v3 save decodes as [1, 2] and validation tolerates only roster teams.
  • tests/unit/test_maps.gd — HQ/base lints retarget to each map's own roster; new parser tests for the bounds; a 4-army fixture map under maps/fixtures/ exercising roster derivation, funds seeding and turn rotation.
  • Proof: make verify green; both Lab gates byte-diff clean against main (the reports/ regeneration procedure), make screenshot byte-identical.

FP2 · Hostility gets one authority size M shipped

The allegiance seam of D2, wired while still unreachable: with no UI writing sides, every match is a free-for-all and every routed site answers exactly what team != mine answered.

  • core/game_state.gd — sides from MatchConfig (empty = FFA), allied(a, b), enemies_of(team); save v4 records the grouping.
  • Route the sites: attack/capture validation, movement pass-through and ambush (move_command.gd, movement_resolver.gd, game_state.gd:213), the AI's enemy set and threat map, and vision's jamming loop. Join, load and services deliberately keep owner == unit.team — noted in code where an ally might be expected.
  • core/rules/vision.gd — a side's visible set is the union over member armies; BattlePerspective asks per viewer as today and inherits the union.
  • core/commander_type.gd — _opponents_of() (every hostile team); Orin Flux, Mara Voss, Sable Wren aggregate over it; duel behaviour pinned unchanged by the existing doctrine tests.
  • Alliance unit tests set sides directly on fixture states: ally can't be targeted or captured, ally pass-through, no ally ambush, fog union, doctrine gating against two rivals.
  • Proof: make verify green; Lab gates byte-diff clean; make screenshot byte-identical (FFA default proves the routing inert).

FP3 · An army can fall; a side can win size M shipped

The elimination model of D3, sim-side. In a duel the first elimination still ends the match on the same day with the same winner — pinned by the existing rout and HQ tests.

  • core/game_state.gd — eliminated state + active_teams(); _check_rout marks the fallen; the match resolves when all survivors share one side (winner = the side's lead army, winners() for presentation); next_team() skips the fallen.
  • core/commands/capture_command.gd — HQ capture eliminates the HQ's owner (units removed, properties → neutral, progress cleared) instead of setting winner.
  • core/commands/end_turn_command.gd — the day advances on wrap to the first surviving army; income (turn_rules.gd) never runs for the fallen.
  • Save v4 carries eliminated; the codec round-trip test covers a mid-elimination save.
  • Edge tests: an army routed by a counter-attack on its own turn; three armies falling in sequence; an allied pair winning together; an eliminated army's properties re-captured by two different survivors.
  • Proof: make verify green; Lab gates byte-diff clean again (the duel path re-proven after the win-condition surgery — the milestone most likely to disturb it clears the bar alone).

FP4 · Four liveries on one board size S shipped

Presentation learns to seat four armies and say who stands with whom; every 2-player frame stays pixel-identical.

  • scenes/common/side_identity.gd — both fallback orders grow to four keys (D5); "no two sides share a colour" holds for any roster ≤ 4; test_side_identity.gd gains 3- and 4-slot cases including triple mirrors and all-commander-less boards.
  • Elimination banner — the fallen army's name and livery through the existing turn-banner mechanism, gated like every banner (Instant clamps it, capture suppresses it).
  • scenes/battle/battle_outcome.gd — the victory line reads the winning side ("Meridian & Iron win!"), the fallen listed in elimination order; watch mode's diffable line unchanged.
  • scenes/ui/commander_info_sheet.gd — open() takes the match roster and lays out N cards (2×2 at four), grouped by side, replacing the (red_co, blue_co) pair. The bottom bar's unit card gains the Ally/Enemy word of D5.
  • Viewer policy + handoff per D7 in battle.gd — the last-human viewer and human-to-human blackout; hot-seat pairs behave exactly as today.
  • Proof: make screenshot byte-identical; a posed 4-army scenario frame added to the gallery; hot-seat 2-human match indistinguishable from today.

FP5 · Seats and sides at the table size M shipped

The menu flow of D6 — the first moment a player can reach a 3–4 army match, in any grouping.

  • scenes/menu/main_menu.gd — the seat strip (Human/CPU + side badge per roster seat, the three presets) replaces the two mode buttons; map labels gain the player count; the footer identity chips build per seat; setup validates at least two sides.
  • scenes/menu/commander_select_panel.gd — the slot walk of D6: enum Side and _red_id/_blue_id become a per-seat array, N chips, N confirm steps, seat-by-seat back; confirmed(picks: Dictionary).
  • scenes/battle/battle_setup.gd — the --sides=1+3v2+4 flag; --co= positional list over the roster (already loops); --watch unchanged; rematch's remember() carries the grouping.
  • Dev capture flags (--co-select=blue) become seat-indexed for the gallery.
  • Proof: every menu path playable — 1 human + 3 CPUs FFA, 4 humans hot-seat, 2v2 with a human ally, 3v1 with the human alone, mixed 2+2 with fog (exercising D7), full-AI watch in each grouping; a duel map still sets up in the same number of clicks.

FP6 · The boards, and the proof of play size M shipped

The capability is only real when shipped maps use it and the AI demonstrably fights on them.

  • Two new shipped maps: Trident (3 armies) and Compass (4 armies) — land-only per the naval plan's standing R1, one HQ and one base per army, funds-fair by design review (the 180° symmetric tag stays a duel instrument; these ship without it). Compass laid out for clean 2v2 and 3v1 groupings: opposite corners allied, or three corners against a fortified fourth.
  • Map lints already retargeted in FP1 hold them to one HQ/base per roster army; the menu offers them with their player counts.
  • AI-vs-AI soak: seeded --watch runs on both boards at all three tiers, in FFA, 2v2 and 3v1 groupings — no stalls, no phantom winners, eliminations orderly, allies never fire on each other, matches conclude. The hook-layer lesson applies: the soak is the integration test the unit suite cannot be.
  • Threat-cap sanity check from the survey (incoming damage saturating against three rivals): observe in the soak; if the AI visibly cowers, the fix is an AIProfile number, not code.
  • README + CLAUDE.md: the roster rule, the sides rule, the elimination rule, the new maps and flags.
  • Proof: make verify green; the soak's winner/day lines recorded in the PR; a 4-army 2v2 save/load round-trip mid-match.
5

Risks

R1 · The Balance Lab's byte-stability bar

FP1–FP3 all cut through match_engine.gd's substrate (GameState internals, the hostility test, the win condition), and two committed documents rest on its numbers. Mitigation is structural — on a 2-army FFA the roster, the authority and the elimination collapse are definitionally today's — and procedural: both gates byte-diffed at each of the three sim milestones, per the standing merge-bar procedure (generate both sides; mean_ms always differs).

R2 · Elimination and side-victory semantics hide edge cases

The win condition is the one genuine redesign. Sharp corners: an army routed by a counter on its own turn (whose turn is it now?), the day-wrap rule once seat 1 is dead, capture progress on a dying army's cells, a doctrine power firing during the elimination cascade, and the match-over check now being "one side", not "one army". Mitigation: FP3's edge-test list is written before the code, the duel path is pinned by existing tests, and the FP6 soak brute-forces the sequencing at three tiers, three groupings, two boards.

R3 · The AI fights beside you, not with you

It respects the alliance perfectly — never a shot at an ally, never a wasted fear of one — but it coordinates nothing: no joint focus fire, no shared front, and in FFA it pools all rivals into one threat blob (the incoming-damage cap saturates on contested cells), averages three armies' rosters into one counter-build answer, and will happily kingmake. Accepted for this plan: the soak checks it fights rather than stalls, and "which rival do I press" / "fight together" is named future work, not smuggled in.

R4 · Hot-seat fog with mixed seats

The current viewer policy leaks vision the moment humans and CPUs interleave, and shared ally sight adds a second fog composition to get right. D7's two rules close the known leak and D2 puts the union in one place (vision.gd), but fog correctness is exactly the kind of split-across-layers rule the hook-layer lesson warns about. Mitigation: the FP5 proof matrix plays the 2-human + 2-CPU fog match and the 2v2 human-ally match at the desk, and the blackout rule lives in one place (_needs_handoff), not re-derived per caller.

R5 · Multi-army maps are hard to make fair — and 3v1 isn't meant to be

The 180° symmetry tag cannot speak for 3–4 armies, so Trident and Compass are fairness-by-review, not fairness-by-lint: rotational layouts (Compass can be 90°-rotationally fair by construction even if no lint asserts it), equal property counts per army checked by the retargeted lints, and the seeded soak's win spread across seats as the smoke test. 3v1 is deliberately asymmetric — a challenge grouping the player chooses, compensated by commander pick and tier, never by the board. A proper N-way symmetry lint is future work.

R6 · Save compatibility

Low: the codec already versions (READABLE_VERSIONS), the decoder already loops teams, and v3 saves carry exactly the [1, 2] roster and empty grouping FP1–FP2 assume for them. The round-trip, mid-elimination and 2v2 cases are in the milestone test lists.

6

Explicitly out of scope

Shared infrastructure between allies

Pooled funds, repairing on an ally's city, riding an ally's transport, joining into an ally's unit. D2 draws the line at sight and hostility; logistics co-op is a second axis on the economy with real balance weight, and it layers cleanly on the authority later.

AI coordination and FFA strategy

Joint focus fire, shared fronts, aggro selection, kingmaking avoidance, per-rival counter-builds. Named in R3 as the known gap; it deserves its own plan against soak evidence, not guesses.

A four-seat Balance Lab axis

Per D8 the Lab stays a duel instrument; N-way evidence comes from watch-mode soaks. A long-format row schema (one row per seat) is the additive path if four-way telemetry is ever wanted. Mid-match diplomacy — breaking or forming alliances in play — is likewise out: the grouping is fixed at setup.

A fifth army, and retrofitting shipped maps

Four is the faction art budget (D5) and the cap the parser enforces. The thirteen shipped boards stay duels — the map-retrofit plan's conversion rules make widening one a deliberate, separate decision. Network play stays where it has always been: nowhere in any plan.

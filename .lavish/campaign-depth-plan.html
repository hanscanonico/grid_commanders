Grid Commanders · campaign mode · design of record · rev 1 · 2026-08-07

Campaign Depth

Six wars, not 108 battles.

The six campaigns ship 108 authored missions with real dialogue and a real story treatment behind them — and 86 of their 108 primary objectives are CaptureCell, which is the skirmish win condition with a label on it. Nothing on the board knows a mission is being played, nothing can happen during a fight that the player did not do, and nothing at all carries from one mission to the next. This plan is the engine work that turns an ordered list of duels into a war: a wider objective vocabulary, scripted mid-battle events, a consequence ledger, an army that carries its wounds, interludes and optional missions — then a retrofit pass over all 108.

89 of 108 missions have exactly one objective, and in the overwhelming majority that objective is “take the enemy HQ” — which is what GameState already ends a match on. The mission layer is, for most of the campaign, a caption.

Three structural gaps, in the order they hurt

  1. A mission cannot ask for anything a skirmish cannot. The vocabulary is four objective Resources and one failure. Every beat the story treatments wanted — evacuate, escort, keep this general alive, destroy that column, hold this ridge — was ruled out at authoring time and rewritten as “capture ground by day N”. .lavish/campaign-scenarios-plan.html §5 says so explicitly and calls it “the trap every campaign treatment falls into”. The campaign that shipped is precisely the campaign that vocabulary can express.
  2. Nothing can happen during a battle that the player did not do. There are no triggers, no reinforcements, no defections, no dialogue inside a fight. A mission is fully determined at deploy time, so its shape is knowable from the briefing card and never surprises anyone.
  3. The board does not know it is in a campaign, and the campaign does not remember the board. battle.gd touches CampaignSession at exactly one line — decide(game). The objective, the deadline and the bonus star are printed once on the hub's briefing card and never appear again. And CampaignState carries unlocked ids, stars and a best day, so mission 18 opens in a state indistinguishable from mission 1.

What is not wrong. The story itself is good and it is already data: MissionLine with a commander id as speaker, briefing and victory dialogue on every one of the 108, a narrator's defeat line, six premises with real shape and a full treatment behind them. The campaign layer's architecture is also sound — pure, Node-free, observing at one seam, saving through its own codec. Nothing in this plan replaces any of that. The words are fine; they have nowhere to land inside a fight, and no consequences behind them.

02

What ships

Six capabilities, then a retrofit that uses all of them on all 108 missions.

A · Objective vocabulary

Six new MissionObjective subclasses so a mission can ask for destruction, protection, arrival, endurance and restraint — not only conquest. Plus the board's first ability to name a unit, without which half of them are unsayable.

B · Scripted events

A MissionEvent is a set of triggers and a set of effects. Reinforcements land, a garrison defects, a bridge is dropped, a marshal speaks mid-fight, a hidden objective is revealed. Every effect is applied as a Command, so a scripted beat is in the log, the save and the replay with no special case.

C · The consequence ledger

Named facts written by one mission and read by the next: which town was saved, whether the courier lived, how many marshals are still standing. They change later briefings, later starting boards and which missions open.

D · The carried army

Survivors roll forward and losses hurt — but a carried unit replaces a map-authored one in an authored slot, so every board still fields exactly the army it was balanced for. What carries is condition and identity, never force size.

E · Interludes & branches

A narrative page between blocks that shows what the last three missions cost and what the enemy did about it, and optional missions that open on ledger facts — so two players' routes through the eighteen differ.

F · Mission presence on the board

An objective panel that stays up during the fight — what wins, what loses, the deadline counting down, bonus progress — and the speech overlay an event talks through. An event nobody is told about is indistinguishable from a bug.

The shape of one mission, after

Sketch of the authored data, not final field names.

# lf08_after_hammerfall.tres
objectives    = [ DestroyUnit { tag: "draeg_gun" } ]
failures      = [ ProtectUnit { tag: "carrow_relay" },
                  DayDeadline { last_day: 12 } ]
bonus         = [ LossLimit { max_losses: 2 } ]

events = [
  MissionEvent {
    id: "iron_relief"
    triggers: [ DayReached { day: 5 } ]
    lines:    [ viktor_draeg: "You bought five days. I brought a division." ]
    effects:  [ SpawnUnits { 2, [ md@16,3  md@16,6  r@17,4 ] } ]
  }
  MissionEvent {
    id: "the_wardens_turn"
    triggers: [ UnitDestroyed { tag: "draeg_gun" },
                Flag { flag: "greenwater_held", at_least: 1 } ]
    lines:    [ ivar_thorne: "The wardens remember Greenwater. They are yours." ]
    effects:  [ Defect { from: 2, to: 1, tags: ["warden_column"] },
                SetFlag { flag: "wardens_joined", value: 1 } ]
  }
]

Everything above is data under data/campaigns/ and maps/campaign/. The second event is the plan in one block: it fires on a board fact and a fact from an earlier mission, it speaks, it changes who an army belongs to, and it writes a fact the rest of the campaign can read.

03

Locked decisions

The rationale that must survive any change in this area. Read the one that owns your file before editing it.

D1 · An event effect is a Command

A scripted beat mutates the board through a single new MissionEventCommand under core/commands/, issued at the one live broker (Battle.execute_command → BattleCommandPipeline) — never by the campaign layer reaching into GameState between commands. This is the more-commanders plan's D2 applied verbatim: a power is deliberately just another command, and that is what puts it in the log, the save, the replay and the AI's world with no special case.

Rejected alternative: a campaign-layer mutator that writes the board directly. It is invisible to ReplayRecorder, so every campaign replay fails its own 48-bit digest at the first event; and a mid-mission save would resume onto a board its own command log cannot account for. ReplayCodec.FORMAT goes to 3 and older recordings are refused outright, which is the replay plan's D3.

D2 · A trigger is a pure read of the committed board and the mission's own tally

MissionTrigger.is_met(state, team, progress) takes MissionObjective.is_met's shape and inherits its whole contract: it reads only authorities that already exist, it never mutates, and it re-derives nothing the sim owns. No timers, no callbacks, no EventBus subscription — so an event can only fire at a command boundary, which is exactly where MissionRuntime is already asked, and there is one place in the codebase to look.

Some conditions are not answerable from one board — “which events have already fired”, “how many consecutive days we have held the ridge”, “how many units we have lost”. MissionProgress is that tally: a small integer dictionary with exactly one writer (the same seam that fires events), saved in the campaign profile beside the embedded battle. It is mission bookkeeping, not board state, which is why SaveCodec does not grow a field for it and a skirmish save is untouched.

D3 · Events fire before the verdict, and a boundary settles whole

Precedence in Battle.conclude_command becomes: fallen-army banner → due events → CampaignSession.decide. Every event due at a boundary applies before any verdict is taken, because a relief column that arrives on the turn the deadline expires must be on the board the deadline is judged against, and an event that ends the mission is a MissionRuntime reason like any other. Within a boundary, events fire in the mission's authored order and each is a separate command, so a replay can show them landing one at a time.

MissionRuntime's own precedence is untouched — losing still outranks winning throughout. Events are a step in front of that class, never a case inside it.

D4 · The board can name a unit — the one core/ waiver taken in CD1

Unit.tag: StringName, empty by default, authored as an optional fifth column on a map's [units] row and carried in save v9. Without it, “destroy Draeg's siege gun”, “the marshal must survive” and “get the courier to the pass” are all unsayable — which is exactly the rejected-verb list the scenarios plan's §5 wrote off.

It is inert data: nothing in core/rules/, nothing in ai/ and nothing in the damage chart reads a tag, so a tagged unit fights, moves, is priced and is planned against identically to an untagged one. The merge bar is the usual one and it is what stands in for the waiver: a fixed-seed byte-diff of make commander-balance and make difficulty-check with no accepted departure. Read it the way the AI Arena's AR1 cache is read — a recorded, justified departure, not a rule violation.

Rejected alternative: identify a unit by the cell it started on. A unit that moves stops being identifiable, and a protect objective matters precisely when the unit is moving.

D5 · Consequences are a flag ledger, and a flag is written by an event

CampaignState.flags: Dictionary[StringName, int], carried by CampaignSaveCodec VERSION 2. A flag is set only by a SetFlag effect or by mission completion (stars, bonus, verdict). Integers rather than booleans, so a ledger can count — “cities saved: 4”, “marshals still standing: 3” — without a second kind of fact.

A flag reaches the board only by choosing which authored content is used — a variant briefing line, a conditional starting unit, whether a mission opens — never by tuning a number. Nothing in core/ or ai/ reads one. That boundary is what keeps a campaign from becoming a second, invisible balance surface: a player who saved every city plays a different authored mission, not the same mission against a weaker AI.

D6 · The carried army carries condition, never quantity

A board authors carry slots — a marked [units] row. A mission's carried roster fills them in order, and the map's own authored unit stands in any slot the roster cannot fill (short roster, first mission of a block, a mission that declares carry_in = false because the player has changed commander or faction).

So every board always fields exactly the army it was balanced for. What carries forward is HP, tag and identity. This is the single decision that makes carry-over shippable across 108 boards nobody is going to re-balance, and it is also the better game: a veteran tank at 40 HP is a unit you have a history with and a decision to make about, not an arithmetic disadvantage.

Rejected alternative: survivors appended to the map's army. It is the only version of carry-over that changes force size, and it invalidates the authoring of all 108 boards at once — in both directions, since a player who lost nothing snowballs and a player who lost badly death-spirals.

The floor is explicit and authored: a mission declares the HP a carried unit is refit to at minimum (carry_floor_hp), so “the campaign is unwinnable because mission 6 went badly” is a number an author sets rather than an emergent property. R1 owns the risk.

D7 · One source for what comes next; branching narrows, it never forks

CampaignDefinition.missions stays the only order — that class's own comment already states why (“two sources for what comes after this is how a campaign ends up with a mission nothing reaches”), and this plan does not spend it. Branching is MissionDefinition.unlock_requires: a ledger/star condition saying whether a mission opens at all. The campaign advances to the next mission in the list whose condition holds.

That buys optional missions (open one, skip it) and alternate routes (two adjacent missions, at most one of which opens) while making it structurally impossible to author an unreachable mission. is_complete counts only missions that could have opened, so a route not taken does not read as a campaign not finished.

D8 · Mission presence on the board is presentation, and it is the event system's mouth

An objective panel and an event speech overlay under scenes/ui/, built from UiTheme / UiKit with no hardcoded colour or size (the HUD plan's rule), reading CampaignSession and nothing else. The speech overlay reuses MissionSpeech, so a line spoken mid-battle is drawn by the same drawer as the briefing's.

It obeys the boundary conventions already in force: an event's speech is an awaited blocking beat any press skips (TransitionInput, ux-recovery D3), no interactive menu sits under it, and it is suppressed while animator.capturing and under Instant so make smoke stays byte-stable outside the frames the panel is deliberately in.

D9 · Every mission carries a beat, and the content gate proves it

make campaigns (tools/check_campaigns.gd) is the authoring authority and it grows to hold the new content: every mission names at least one event; every trigger and effect resolves; every tag an objective, trigger or effect names exists on that mission's board; every flag read is written by some earlier mission on every route that reaches the reader; every carry slot is a legal cell for what can land in it; and every mission is reachable. That last check is what keeps D7 honest, and the flag-reachability check is what stops a branch from producing a briefing line that refers to something that never happened.

06

The retrofit

108 missions is the bulk of this plan. The authoring rule is what keeps it from becoming 108 gimmicks.

The authoring rule

A mission's event should be the thing its briefing already promised. The dialogue on all 108 missions is already written and it already says what the fight is about — “Orlov and I split the attack across two passes”, “her column is advancing in the open, in daylight”. An event that performs that sentence is the mission finally doing what it said; an event that contradicts it is a gimmick. Most events should therefore be small: a line spoken at the moment it becomes true, plus one board fact. A minority — the act openers, the block finales, the turning points — carry the big ones: the relief column, the defection, the revealed objective.

The corollary is that the retrofit is mostly reading the dialogue that is already there and asking what would have to happen on the board for it to be true. That is a much cheaper pass than inventing 108 new beats, and a much better one.

Per mission, what the pass does
  1. Re-read the briefing and name the fight it describes.
  2. Replace the primary objective if “take their HQ” is not that fight.
  3. Tag whatever the objective or the story names on the board.
  4. Author one event: the promised beat, its trigger, its line, its board fact.
  5. Wire the ledger — what this mission writes, what it reads.
  6. Mark carry slots on the board (typically the player's core 2–4 units).
  7. Re-check par day and the deadline against the new shape.
Per campaign, the shape to hold
  • The ledger has a spine. Each of the six wars gets a named handful of facts (5–8) that its 18 missions write and read — not a fact per mission, which is a ledger nobody can follow.
  • Blocks close. Every block's last mission writes a fact its interlude reads, so the three-mission rhythm the block_titles already declare becomes something the player is told about.
  • Two optional missions per campaign, no more. Enough that a route is a route; few enough that the reachability and flag checks stay legible.
  • Carry resets at block boundaries where the player changes commander — which is most of them, and is why D6's short-roster fallback is the common case rather than the edge.

07

Risk register

R1 · The carried army death-spirals

A player who loses badly in mission 6 carries a broken army into 7, loses worse, and the campaign becomes unwinnable without a replay. Guard: D6's floor (carry_floor_hp) is authored per mission and the roster only ever replaces, so the worst case is the board's own army at the floor — never fewer units. Blocks reset the roster. CD5's fixture campaign tests three consecutive bad missions.

R2 · Event soup

Every mission having a beat becomes a twist per mission, which is a campaign with no baseline and therefore no surprises. Guard: §6's authoring rule — the event performs what the briefing already promised, and most are a line plus one small board fact. The big beats are budgeted per block, not per mission.

R3 · Replay format churn

FORMAT → 3 refuses every recording on disk, which is by design (replay D3) but is a cost paid once. Guard: the bump happens in CD3 and nowhere else in this plan; CD1's tag rides in the save envelope, not the line format, and every later milestone adds effects rather than line kinds.

R4 · A trigger reads something not on the board

The one way to break determinism here: a trigger that consults the clock, a setting, or the presentation layer would replay differently. Guard: D2 is the rule and tools/check_scripts.sh can enforce the import boundary the way it already enforces the single live broker; test_replay_fidelity.gd is the proof.

R5 · Two save formats move in different milestones

Board save v9 lands in CD1; campaign profile v2 in CD4; the profile embeds the board save. A v1 profile holding a v8 battle must still resume. Guard: the two codecs are already independent by design and each validates its own shape; CD4's gate names this case explicitly.

R6 · Authoring volume

108 missions × four kinds of change is the majority of the plan's hours. Guard: CD7 is six independently shippable PRs in a deliberate order (§6), so value lands continuously and a regression is attributable to one war. The engine milestones are complete and gated before any of it starts.

R7 · The planner is surprised by an effect

An effect that removes, spawns or defects units mid-turn can leave AIPlanCache holding a plan for a unit that no longer exists or no longer belongs to that army. Guard: the cache already drops on a board diff rather than on the returned command, which is the correct existing behaviour — CD3 owes it a test per effect kind rather than new code. A reinforcement wave the planner did not foresee is fine and intended: it replans every command.

R8 · The ledger becomes a difficulty surface

The tempting next step after flags is “a player who is struggling gets a weaker enemy”. Guard: D5 forbids it — a flag chooses authored content, never a number — and the difficulty plan's lock already forbids a campaign shipping its own AI values. A mission names a shipped tier and nothing else.

08

Gates & supersessions

The bar every milestone clears
  • Both balance reports byte-identical across CD1–CD6 with no accepted departure — make commander-balance and make difficulty-check. Nothing in this plan may change how a shot resolves or how the planner scores, and the tag waiver (D4) is what this bar stands in for.
  • make verify green — check, lint, format-check, GUT.
  • make campaigns green.
  • make smoke byte-stable outside the frames the objective panel and the event overlay are deliberately in; those get their own scenarios and a recorded SMOKE_HASHES manifest.
  • test_replay_fidelity.gd reproduces a campaign mission with events, end to end, from CD3 onward.
What this plan supersedes
  • campaign-scenarios-plan.html §5 — the expressibility ruling that evacuate, escort, keep alive, reinforcements and changes sides “are presumed to need engine work until proven otherwise”. This plan is that engine work; §5's table is answered line by line in §4 above. Its underlying rule survives intact and is worth restating: a tactic being shipped is not a mission being expressible.
  • campaign-mode-plan.html's out-of-scope list — “mid-mission reinforcements, scripted event triggers, escort units, branching missions, a persistent army”. All five are in scope here, under D1–D7. That plan's architecture (MissionRuntime, the objective Resources, the campaign save, hub → briefing → battle → debrief) is not superseded and is what this builds on.
  • The campaign entry in CLAUDE.md gains this plan's decisions; its existing D1–D5 stand unchanged, and D2's “no evacuate/escort/named-unit objective exists on purpose” is the one clause this retires.

What this plan deliberately does not do

  • No new commanders, factions, units, terrain or art rows. The roster of 22 and the four liveries are the cast; this is entirely mission and campaign work.
  • No animated cutscenes. Events speak through MissionSpeech and the existing banner conventions — the same text-and-portrait vocabulary the briefings already use.
  • No campaign-specific AI. A mission names a shipped difficulty tier and a shipped commander doctrine; the difficulty plan's lock holds (a tier is an AIProfile and a label, and the AI never cheats).
  • No per-mission balance tuning of the roster or the damage chart. Balance lives in data/ and is the whole game's, not a mission's.

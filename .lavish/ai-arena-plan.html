The AI Arena

Build the best computer opponent this planner can carry, by making it play itself thousands of times and keeping what wins. Three things stand between here and there, and only one of them is the tournament: a match is too slow, the harness cannot seat an arbitrary candidate, and — the finding that reorders this whole plan — the planner is missing the parameters worth searching. The dials it has are largely a choice between two kinds of bad play, which is why the shipped ladder is inverted over a horizon that resolves.

01

Where it stands, measured

Every number below was taken on 2026-08-02 in this worktree, or is quoted from a committed document. Nothing here is an estimate.

1a — What a match costs

2.2 s

per match, ironworks, no CO, Normal v Normal, 20-day cap — the horizon difficulty-check uses.

5.1 s

per match on the same board played to a natural end. A 120-day cap costs the same as a 60-day one: every seed routed by day 41.

~8 ms

per planned command. A match is 563–731 commands over 33–41 days. This is the number the arena lives or dies by.

~54 s

core-seconds per match across the Grand Atlas — 20,280 matches, 304.7 core-hours, 250-day cap, heavy boards included.

The cost is superlinear in army size, and it is one line of code.

AIController.plan_next_command returns one command. AIUnitActionPlanner.plan_next gets there by scoring every ready unit — each one a fresh MovementResolver.reachable flood fill plus a sweep over reachable cells × visible enemies — then throwing all but the winner away. The scene and the harness then call it again, and it rescores every survivor from scratch.

With N units acting, that is N + (N−1) + … + 1 full plans per turn where N plus a handful of deltas would do. At the 16–19 units these matches end with, it is roughly 130–190 flood fills per turn to issue 16–19 commands. The committed turn-time reading in docs/difficulty_check.md (33 ms Normal, 63 ms with a live threat dial) is the same cost seen from the scene side.

It is not wasted by accident — re-planning after every command is load-bearing, and data/ai/hard.tres says so in its own comment: it is why a wounded target's kill is visible to the next attacker without a focus-fire dial. So the fix cannot be "plan the whole turn at once". It has to be a cache that is exactly equivalent. AR1.

1b — What the harness cannot do

BalanceSideSpec is <commander>:<tier>, and it validates both halves against the databases on purpose. A side can therefore be exactly one of three parameter vectors — easy.tres, default.tres, hard.tres. There is no grammar anywhere for "play this vector", which is the entire input a tournament needs.

What is already right, and must not be rebuilt: BalanceMatchEngine is the one match loop (balance plan D1), it demands an independent AIController per side, it plays paired seeds from both seats, and it is Node-free so GUT drives it. The arena is a third preset over it, not a second engine.

1c — What the planner cannot express at any dial setting

This is the finding that reorders the plan. A search over the shipped dials is a search over a policy with these holes in it, and the holes are wider than the dials.

GapEvidence in the treeWhy it bounds the search
Positioning is blind to cover _advance_value is −distance − threat − cohesion + doctrine. Grepping ai/ for defence stars returns one comment and no code. Terrain defence reaches the planner only through forecast_at inside ThreatMap — which Normal never builds (threat_aversion 0.0) and Difficult weights at 0.1. The default AI parks a tank on plains beside empty woods and cannot notice. Sable Wren's stand_value proves the seam exists and is unused by the neutral planner.
Four commands are never issued JoinCommand, SupplyCommand, LoadCommand, DropCommand: zero references in ai/. Two 4-HP infantry stay two 4-HP infantry forever. The APC is unbuildable dead weight. Ferrying is the standing naval R1 and is why most four-seat boards carry no water.
Value ignores condition _attack_score, _threat_penalty and _consider_withdraw all price a unit at type.cost. A 10-HP Md Tank is priced like a fresh one, as target and as asset. Every risk-and-reward dial is being asked to compensate for a valuation that is wrong by up to 90%.
Retreat is priced by damage alone _better_refuge: safety, then repair, then walk length. withdraw_weight is pinned at 0.0 by a defect, not by balance — at 0.05 it walks artillery out of its own firing ring (test_indirect_unit_backs_off_into_firing_range). One of nineteen dials is switched off because the behaviour behind it is mispriced. A search cannot fix that; it will simply rediscover 0.
Greedy, single-unit, no ordering plan_next takes the globally best single action. focus_fire_bonus is the only nod to coordination and has measured negative three times, most recently with cohesion live. No blocking a choke, no "this unit goes first so that one can pass", no trading a shot to open a lane. Structural, and the largest of the gaps.

And the ceiling is already documented.

The Grand Atlas — 20,280 matches at a 250-day cap — found the tier ladder inverted: Easy > Difficult > Normal, with Normal taking only 15.6% from Easy. Easy is the timid profile: kill_bonus 1.0, counter_weight 1.0, the highest threat aversion of the three, no md tank. It wins the long game by declining the fights the other two take.

Read that as a statement about the parameter space, because that is what it is: the shipped dials mostly trade one class of bad play for another, and the difference between the extremes of the space is worth less than the difference between playing and not playing. An optimiser turned loose on those nineteen numbers will find a good local optimum of a weak policy. That is worth having — it is AR5 — but it is not the deliverable, and pretending otherwise is how this plan would waste 300 core-hours. The deliverable is AR6.

03

Decisions

Locked unless a later plan supersedes them in writing.

D1 — The arena is an instrument; the game never learns it exists

Nothing under core/ gains a field, a hook or a branch for this plan. Nothing under ai/ gains an "arena mode": a candidate is an AIProfile and the planner cannot tell whether it is being measured or played. The tournament driver lives in tools/ and reads BalanceMatchEngine.Outcome, which already carries every fact fitness needs.

This is balance plan D2 applied to a new instrument, and it exists for the same reason: the moment the sim can see the measurement, the measured game stops being the shipped one.

D2 — A performance change is proven byte-identical, never argued

AR1 makes the planner faster without making it different, and the only acceptable evidence is the bar this repo already uses: a fixed-seed byte-diff of make commander-balance and make difficulty-check across the change, both reports identical, no accepted departure. A faster planner that plays a different game is a different AI and has to be measured as one — which would silently invalidate the difficulty ladder, the Atlas, and every commander number in docs/commander_balance.md in one commit.

The byte-diff is necessary and not sufficient, because both gates seat commanders and short horizons. AR1 adds a differential test as well: the cached and uncached planners run the same seeded matches command for command and must agree on every one. That test stays in the suite afterwards, which is what keeps the cache honest as new dials are added to it.

D3 — A candidate is an AIProfile and nothing else

The search space is exactly the exported fields of ai_profile.gd. No arena-only code paths, no feature flags the driver can toggle, no "experimental planner" fork. A capability the search is allowed to explore is an @export that ships at its inert default and is written into all three tier files in the same commit — which test_every_tier_explicitly_writes_every_profile_field already enforces, and which is the whole reason that test exists.

The consequence is the useful part: anything the arena discovers is shippable by editing one .tres. A champion that needed a code branch would be a research result nobody can play.

D4 — Fitness is a margin, not a win

Twenty-odd continuous dials scored by a boolean outcome is a step function: almost every mutation changes nothing measurable, and the search spends its compute on noise. Fitness is therefore a graded score per match, built only from fields Outcome already carries — the winner, the day it ended, and the property / surviving-unit / army-value margins the tiebreak already reads.

A decisive win outranks any scored one, so the ordering the game cares about is never inverted by the gradient underneath it; within each class the margin ranks, and an earlier decisive win outranks a later one. The exact weights are AR4's deliverable and live in docs/ai_arena.md with the reasoning, because a fitness function nobody can read is a leaderboard nobody can trust.

D5 — Both seatings, always; and nobody plays themselves

Measured today: a mirror on clash gave the first seat +100 pp. run_balance_sim already plays non-mirror pairings from both seats on paired seeds, so both sides meet identical luck; the arena inherits that and counts both, so the seat cancels rather than being reported.

And a pairing of two identical vectors is skipped, not played: its outcome is the seat talking, and feeding that into a leaderboard credits whichever twin drew seat one. The existing mirror single-seating shortcut is a bias measurement and stays what it is — it must never become the arena's scoring path.

D6 — The horizon is the one that resolves

docs/difficulty_check.md at a 20-day cap and the Grand Atlas at 250 disagree about which tier is stronger, and the document itself concedes the point: a 20-day reading "measured who was ahead early". Measured today on ironworks, every seed routs by day 41 and a 120-day cap costs the same wall clock as a 60-day one — so on land boards the resolving horizon is nearly free.

The arena's default cap is therefore 100 days, and an unresolved match is recorded as unresolved — never folded into fitness by the score tiebreak. Two candidates that cannot beat each other have told us that, and scoring the stalemate on property count rewards whichever one hoards better. command_cap stalls stay what they already are in this toolchain: a hard invariant failure of the run, not a draw.

D7 — The opponent is the archive, not the champion

The obvious loop — promote the winner, make everyone fight it — is the one that cycles. This game is visibly intransitive already: Easy beats Difficult beats Normal beats Easy is a measured fact in the Atlas, not a hypothesis. A ladder that only ever plays the current leader will happily walk a circle and report progress every lap.

So every generation is scored against three things: a fixed anchor set (the three shipped tiers, unchanged for the life of the run, which is the only measurement comparable across generations), a sample of the archive of past champions, and its own generation. Progress is the anchor score. Everything else is selection pressure.

D8 — The arena recommends; a human ships

No milestone here writes a searched number into data/ai/*.tres automatically, and no make target does either. This is the rule the Atlas already set for its own leaderboard — review trigger, never auto-nerf — and it holds harder here, because the arena optimises for winning a headless match against a machine, and the product is a game a person plays. AR7 is a written recommendation with the evidence attached; the edit that follows is a normal reviewed change under BL2's ownership of tier numbers.

D9 — The build list is a permutation and gets its own search

build_priority is an ordered Array[StringName], not a float, and the three tiers differ in it more than in anything else they carry. Mutating it means swaps and insertions over a discrete space, with membership itself in play — Easy fields no md tank at all, and that is a real strategic choice rather than a weight.

It is therefore searched in a separate block from the continuous dials, alternating with them rather than jointly: a permutation move and a Gaussian step do not belong in the same proposal distribution, and mixing them makes every result unattributable. Same for air_answer_ids, on the same grounds.

04

Milestones

Seven, each independently mergeable, each ending with make verify green. AR1–AR2 buy the compute, AR3–AR4 build the instrument, AR5 calibrates it, AR6 is the actual work, AR7 is the verdict.

AR1 — Make a match cheap shipped

Removes the N² re-plan. No behaviour change, and the merge bar is that claim.

  • The cache. AIUnitActionPlanner keeps each ready unit's UnitPlan between commands of the same turn, in scan order, and rescores only the units whose plan could have changed.
  • The invalidation, which is the whole milestone. A unit's plan is a function of its own reach and of what stands inside reach-plus-range. So after a command, rescore any unit whose move_budget + max_range + 1 Manhattan envelope contains a cell the command touched — the mover's origin and destination, the target cell, a captured cell, a built cell — and any unit whose candidate set included a unit that died. That bound is a deliberate over-approximation: it may rescore a unit whose plan did not change, and it can never skip one whose plan did. Cheap to state, cheap to check, and exact in the direction that matters.
  • The claim exception, and the envelope is not sufficient without it. AE2 added _assign_capture_claims, which settles a claim for every capturer at once as a global nearest-pair assignment the first time any one of them asks. That makes a capturer's goal a function of where every other capturer is — which an envelope centred on the unit itself cannot see, so a capturer walking clean across the map changes a second capturer's claim without ever entering its envelope. So the cache carries one cross-unit rule: any command that moves, removes, or is issued by a capture-capable unit invalidates every capture-capable unit's cached plan. Capturers are a small fraction of an army, so it costs little. Today it is latent rather than live — capture_claim_depth ships at 0 on all three tiers and the claim returns early — which is precisely the danger: the byte-diff bar would pass without the rule, and AR5 is what turns the dial on. A cache that is exact only while a dial is zero is the silent divergence R3 describes, so the differential suite must include a run with the dial live.
  • The threat map cache in AIPlanningContext is untouched and keeps its own signature key. Two caches, two invalidations, no shared state — and now one cross-unit rule belonging to neither.
  • Everything else in the turn stays as it is. Production is planned once per command and is cheap; the acted unit leaves ready_units by the existing path.

Tests — a differential suite (D2) running the cached and uncached planners over N seeded matches on three boards, asserting command-for-command identity, including one run with capture_claim_depth above zero, since no shipped tier reaches that path; plus targeted fixtures for each invalidation trigger, each of which fails if that trigger is dropped — one of them moving a capturer outside another capturer's envelope and proving the second one is recomputed.

Exit — both committed reports byte-identical; the differential suite green; and a recorded before/after wall clock on ironworks --seeds=4 --days=120, whose 21.4 s reading was taken at b1893f6 and should be re-taken on current main before any speedup is quoted — AE1–AE3 ship inert, so the path should be identical, but "should" is not a measurement. Target ≥ 2.5×; below 1.5× the milestone is reconsidered rather than merged, since the cache costs real complexity in the planner and has to earn it.

AR2 — Make the machine busy shipped

Eight cores, one of them working. Nothing in core/, ai/ or the engine changes.

  • A worker-pool driver in tools/, modelled on the Atlas's grand_sweep_driver.py and resumable the same way — a shard whose output file exists is skipped, so a killed run costs the shard in flight and nothing else. That property was worth more than any other during the Atlas and is non-negotiable here.
  • Sharding is by pairing × seed, one process per shard batch, because that is the unit of work that shares an engine boot (~0.6 s, amortised over a batch) and produces an independently readable result.
  • One spike, timeboxed: in-process threading over WorkerThreadPool. The sim is pure RefCounted over read-only UnitDB / DamageChart / TerrainDB, so N matches in N threads is plausible and would erase the boot cost entirely. It is a spike: if determinism per match does not hold bit for bit against the single-threaded run, it is abandoned without argument and the process pool ships. Threads are an optimisation; reproducibility is the product.

Exit — a documented throughput figure in matches/minute on this machine, and a resumed run proven to skip completed shards. On the measurements above, AR1 × AR2 should put a 100-day ironworks match under 1 s of wall clock each at 6 workers.

AR3 — Seat an arbitrary candidate shipped

The missing input. A new preset over the one match loop, per balance D1.

  • tools/run_ai_arena.gd, a sibling of run_balance_sim.gd, taking a profile per side: --red-profile=<path.tres> and --blue-profile=, plus --pairings=<file.json> so one process plays a whole shard.
  • BalanceSideSpec is not touched. Its grammar is shared with watch mode, and its own docstring says why a spec parsed two ways eventually disagrees. A profile path is a different flag, not a third field in that spec.
  • Output is JSON per shard — one record per match carrying the Outcome fields D4's fitness reads, and nothing else. No CSV, no timeline, no commands.jsonl: at arena volume the telemetry is the dominant write cost and answers a question nobody is asking. A single interesting pairing can always be re-run through make balance-sim with the full instruments on, which is the point of them being one engine.
  • Neutral commanders throughout (doctrine_weight is then inert), which is a scope choice and is recorded as R8.

Tests — GUT over the flag grammar and the profile loader on the same terms the launch layer is already tested on; a fixed-seed shard proven byte-identical to the same matches played through run_balance_sim, which is what keeps the two presets one engine rather than two.

AR4 — Decide what "better" means shipped

The scoring, the pools, and the anchors. Mostly a document; the code is a scorer.

  • D4's graded match score, written down with its weights and its reasoning in docs/ai_arena.md.
  • A training pool and a held-out validation pool, split by map and seed and fixed for the life of a run. Every reported result carries both numbers. A champion that leads on training and not on validation has overfitted the pool, which is R1 and is the failure this instrument is most likely to have.
  • The fixed anchor set: easy, default, hard — never re-tuned mid-run, because they are the only measurement comparable across generations (D7).
  • A boards list chosen for resolution, not variety: land boards that rout inside the cap, with the naval ones excluded while R1 of the naval plan stands (a fleet that cannot ferry cannot conclude, and the_straits at Easy is 100% command-cap stalls in the Atlas — that is a known instrument failure and must not be fed to a fitness function).

Exit — docs/ai_arena.md committed, and a scored run of the three shipped tiers against each other that reproduces the Atlas's inverted-ladder finding at the arena's own cap. That reproduction is the calibration of the fitness function itself: an arena that ranks the tiers in the box-art order is measuring the wrong horizon and is wrong before it has searched anything.

AR5 — Calibrate on the dials that exist proposed

The first real search. Its purpose is to test the instrument, not to ship a profile.

  • Search the nineteen live dials only — no new capabilities — with the build list frozen, so the continuous block is measured alone (D9).
  • The acceptance criterion is a known answer. The search must find something at least as strong as the best shipped tier under its own fitness, and its champion's dials must be legible: high threat aversion and low kill bonus, if the Atlas is right about timidity winning long games. A champion nobody can explain, or one that cannot beat easy.tres, means the fitness function or the pools are wrong and AR4 reopens.
  • Then the same search with the build list unfrozen, in the alternating block of D9, to size what the permutation is worth on its own.
  • No data/ edit. The output is a report and an archive of profiles under reports/.

Exit — a written result including the compute spent, the training/validation gap, and an explicit statement of how much the nineteen dials were worth end to end. That number is the argument for AR6, and if it turns out to be large this plan was wrong about the ceiling and should say so in writing.

AR6 — Widen the shelf shipped

The actual work. Each term is one @export, inert at its default, written into all three tiers, with the search re-run after it.

Each sub-milestone is separately mergeable and separately reverted, and carries the AI Judgement merge bar unchanged: both committed reports byte-identical, because an inert default must be genuinely inert. §5 lists the shelf with the reasoning; the order below is by measured gap over cost.

All four terms are in the tree, each at 0.0 on all three tiers — and none has been through the gate below, which is AR5's search and is not built. The shelf ships unmeasured on purpose: focus_fire_bonus is the precedent for a capability that waits at zero until something measures it, one edit away from being tried.

  1. AR6a — Cover. shipped A defence-stars term in _advance_value and in the attack destination penalty. The single clearest hole in §1c, and the cheapest to close: the number is already on TerrainType and CombatResolver already reads it. Shipped 2026-08-04 (COM-159) as cover_tiles, counted in tiles on both paths — the attack path converts through step_cost_penalty, so one dial says the same thing to the advance and to the shot. The double-pricing answer is per cell: a cell the counter or the threat map already prices scores no stars, because those numbers are CombatResolver's through the same terrain and stars on top would price one wood twice (Judgement R3). _cover_stars became CombatResolver.cover_stars so the planner asks the damage formula what cover a cell gives instead of reading defense_stars itself — the air rule with it.
  2. AR6b — Condition. shipped Value a unit at cost × hp/100 wherever it is currently valued at cost, behind a dial that interpolates between the two so zero is today's behaviour exactly. Shipped 2026-08-04 (COM-160) as condition_weight over five sites, not the three this plan named — _focus_bonus values the same enemy _attack_score does, and leaving it would have given one unit two prices inside one score. _unit_value is now the file's only unit valuation. Defined on 0–1 and clamped there in code as well as in the export: past 1 the interpolation prices a wounded unit below nothing and inverts every reader's sign.
  3. AR6c — Join. shipped JoinCommand as a scored candidate in _best_unit_plan, beside dive and withdraw — the precedent D4 of the Judgement plan already set for a non-attack action that may outbid a shot. Shipped 2026-08-04 (COM-161) as join_weight, denominated in value as a multiple of the HP that survives the merge, because that is the currency the candidates compete in. The overflow the cap destroys is charged separately from the dial, at face value: a loss the file can already price needs no dial, and without the split a high weight pours fresh units into wounded ones.
  4. AR6d — Refuge pricing. shipped Fix what pins withdraw_weight at zero: a refuge that gives up the unit's own firing ring is not a refuge. Adds a term to _better_refuge; the existing failing test is the acceptance criterion, and this one is worth doing even if the arena is never built.

AR6e is gone, and was right to go. This plan originally carried a fifth term — price a property by what it produces — which the ai-economy-plan's AE3 shipped on 2026-08-02, in two dials rather than one (production_capture_multiplier in value, capture_goal_value_tiles in tiles) for the same reason threat_aversion and advance_threat_tiles are two fields. Its defensive half — pricing the factory we hold the same way — is explicitly out of scope there, because AJ1's R3 already says three dials price one board and a fourth reading counts the same doctrine twice. Both new dials ship inert, so they arrive here as search dimensions rather than as behaviour, and they live in §5a's economy block.

The gate on each: a re-run search including the new term must beat the previous champion on the validation pool. A term that does not is kept in the tree at zero, exactly as focus_fire_bonus is — zeroing a smart that did not pay is the established remedy here, and it keeps the next search one edit away from re-testing it.

Deliberately not in AR6: transports and ferrying (naval R1 is a cross-turn planning problem, not a dial), and any form of coordinated multi-unit ordering. Both are named in §7 with why.

AR7 — The verdict proposed

No search. A report and a recommendation, per D8.

  • The champion against all three shipped tiers, on the validation pool, at the resolving horizon, both seatings, with the compute cost stated.
  • A read of what it does at a table. The champion plays a watched match (make balance-watch) and a human describes its play in prose. An opponent that wins by declining every fight for 90 days is a worse game than the one it replaced, and no fitness number will say so — this is the check that catches it, and it is the one step of this plan that cannot be automated.
  • A recommendation per tier: what to change, what it buys, and what it costs the DF4 ladder, handed to BL2 which owns those numbers.
  • Whether the arena becomes a standing instrument (a make target and a docs page) or a one-off study, decided on what AR5 and AR6 actually returned.

What the search may move today, and what AR6 would add. The right-hand column is the part that matters: a dial with no reasoning behind it is a dimension that costs compute and returns noise.

5b — The shelf AR6 adds

All of it is added, as of 2026-08-04: three new dials — cover_tiles, condition_weight, join_weight — at 0.0 on every tier, plus AR6d's refuge rank, which no shipped tier reaches while withdraw_weight is zero. So they are search dimensions exactly like §5a's economy block, and none of them is behaviour until AR5 prices one.

TermWhere it goesWhy it is worth a dimension
Cover value (AR6a) shipped _advance_value, and the destination penalty in _consider_attacks The planner had no term for terrain defence — the first thing a human player learns. Shipped as cover_tiles, in tiles on both paths. Couples to the threat dials by construction rather than by taste: the stars speak only where no forecast has already priced the fire arriving on that cell, so turning a threat dial on takes cover out of exactly those cells.
Condition weighting (AR6b) shipped _unit_value, the one valuation _attack_score, _focus_bonus, _threat_penalty, _consider_withdraw and _join_score all read Everything was priced at full cost regardless of HP, and every risk dial was compensating for a valuation off by up to 90%. Shipped as condition_weight, defined and clamped on 0–1. Search it with kill_bonus and never alone: that bonus is the other correction to this same valuation, so a search that fits it first fits it to a valuation this dial then changes.
Join (AR6c) shipped A candidate in _best_unit_plan The command existed, the rules existed, ai/ held not one reference. Shipped as join_weight. The premise here that two half-dead infantry are strictly worse than one healthy one did not survive the work: damage and capture both scale linearly with HP, so the pair deals the same and can chip two properties. A merge is a positional trade that also spends two units' turns — which is why it is a weighted candidate rather than a rule, and why the tuned value is expected well below 1.0.
Refuge quality (AR6d) shipped _better_refuge Unpinned a dial that was zero for a defect. Standalone value even with no arena.
Property class value shipped elsewhere _consider_captures, by ai-economy-plan AE3 Was AR6e. Shipped 2026-08-02 as production_capture_multiplier (value) + capture_goal_value_tiles (tiles). The mirror into _defend_bonus that this plan expected is deliberately absent — AE3's out-of-scope list rejects it on AJ1 R3 grounds. Do not re-file it.
06

Risks

Ranked by how likely they are to make the result wrong rather than late.

R1 — Overfitting the pool high

The sim is deterministic per seed, so a candidate can be tuned to specific games rather than to play. With a few hundred training matches and twenty dimensions this is not a risk, it is the default outcome. Mitigation: the held-out validation pool of AR4, split by map and seed, with both numbers on every reported result and the gap between them treated as the headline diagnostic rather than a footnote.

R2 — The optimiser finds an exploit, not a strategy high

Fitness maximisers find the cheapest path to the number. The known exploitable seams here: stalling past the cap while ahead on the tiebreak; the first-seat timing edge that save_up_turns buys; and a board whose stalemate the fitness reads as a narrow win. Mitigation: D6 refuses to score unresolved matches at all, D5 counts both seatings so a seat-dependent edge nets to zero, and cap stalls stay a hard invariant failure of the run. Each of the three is already an instrument the toolchain has.

R3 — AR1's cache is subtly inexact high

A missed invalidation is a different AI that still passes most tests, and it would invalidate the ladder, the Atlas and the commander numbers without announcing itself. Mitigation: D2's byte-diff of both committed reports, plus a differential suite that stays in the tree afterwards, plus the over-approximating envelope bound which fails toward doing extra work rather than skipping it. If the envelope cannot be stated simply, the milestone is wrong and should be dropped in favour of AR2's parallelism alone.

R4 — A stronger opponent is a worse game medium

The Atlas already shows what winning looks like in this sim: the timid profile wins long games. An arena champion may well be a turtle that refuses engagements for ninety days and wins on properties. That is a correct answer to the fitness function and a bad opponent for a person. Mitigation: AR7's watched match and written read, D8's human-ships rule, and the fact that difficulty tiers are a product decision BL2 owns. Worth stating plainly: this plan can produce a result that should not ship, and that is a success of the process.

R5 — Dimensionality outruns compute medium

Fifteen dials become twenty with AR6, and the sample cost per evaluation is high because a single match is a noisy read of a matchup. Mitigation: block-wise search (the four blocks of §5a, plus D9's permutation block), never a single joint optimisation; add an AR6 term only when the previous one has been measured; and the compute budget stated per milestone up front. The Atlas's 305 core-hours is the reference for what "too much" looks like — it is a verdict instrument, and an iteration loop that costs the same is not an iteration loop.

R6 — Intransitivity reported as progress medium

Measured, not hypothetical: Easy > Difficult > Normal > Easy in the Atlas. Mitigation: D7 — progress is the score against the fixed anchor set, and the archive is sampled as an opponent so a champion that only beats its immediate predecessor is caught by an ancestor.

R7 — The permutation block dominates low

build_priority may turn out to be worth more than all nineteen floats — the tiers differ in it most, and "what you build" plausibly beats "how you weigh a trade" in a game decided by army composition. Not a problem, but it would change what the plan is for. Mitigation: AR5 measures the two blocks separately and in that order, specifically so the answer is attributable.

R8 — Commander-free training may not transfer low

Training with neutral doctrine keeps the space clean (doctrine_weight inert, no 18 × 18 matrix), but the shipped game is played with commanders whose passives change the forecasts the planner scores through. Mitigation: AR7 spot-checks the champion under two or three commanders on both sides before recommending anything. If it does not transfer, that is a finding worth having and the arena grows a commander axis in a follow-up — not mid-run.

07

Out of scope, and why

Named so a later plan can pick them up on purpose rather than by drift.

Not doingWhy
Search, lookahead, or a learned evaluator The whole toolchain rests on the planner being cheap, deterministic and RNG-free — that is what makes replays verify, the Atlas reproducible and the ladder measurable. A minimax or a neural evaluator is a different project with a different set of guarantees, and it should be proposed as one rather than arrived at through this plan.
Transports and ferrying Naval R1. The planner cannot plan load-move-unload across turns, and no dial reaches that: it needs cross-turn intent, which AIPlanningContext deliberately has nowhere to keep (rebuilt every command; only the threat map survives, on purpose). Real work, its own plan.
Multi-unit coordination and turn ordering The largest gap in §1c and genuinely structural: it changes plan_next from "best single action" to a joint decision, which is where determinism and scan-order tie-breaking get hard. Deliberately left after AR6 so the cheap wins are banked first and there is a fast instrument to measure it with.
Commander doctrine tuning Owned by commander-doctrine-ai-plan and measured by make commander-balance. Neutral throughout here (R8).
Shipping a tier D8. The arena recommends; BL2 owns tier numbers and the ladder they are measured by.
Naval and four-seat boards in the pool Naval R1 makes some of them unable to conclude (the_straits at Easy is 100% command-cap stalls). A board the instrument cannot resolve is not a fitness signal. Land duels only, for this plan.

If only two milestones are ever built

AR1 and AR6d. The first makes every existing instrument in this repo — the ladder, the commander matrix, the Atlas, the scene's own turn pause — cheaper by the same factor, and it is provably behaviour-neutral. The second fixes a dial that is switched off because of a bug rather than a measurement. Both are worth doing whether or not a single tournament is ever run.

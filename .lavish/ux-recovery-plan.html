Grid Commander · unified UX recovery plan · 2026-07-24

Two playtests, one plan.
The tactics are readable — the first five minutes are not.

On the same day, two independent playtests hit the shipped game from different angles: a keyboard-first first-contact playthrough (menu → day two, capture, build) and a blind full-match audit (fresh user://, Day 1 to defeat, then fog, subs, hot-seat and resume passes with findings traced to file and line). They converge on the same verdict: the core loop communicates well, but the UI hides the next action, moves navigation off-screen as content grows, answers invalid input with silence, and — the audit's discovery — paints the wrong faction colours in every commander-match cut-in. This document merges both issue registers into one plan of record and supersedes each on overlapping items.

U-11 High VERIFIED FC H-04
NP B11 · A6		 Transition boundaries mishandle input in both directions. The day banner plays during the ANIMATING state and drops every press — the game elsewhere trains "any key skips", so queued commands simply vanish (whole sequences were lost twice in the audit). Meanwhile menus can open underneath banners and collide, and the outcome panel appears with Rematch already focused, so one buffered press from a mashed cut-in silently reloads the entire match. One convention everywhere (see D3): a press during a banner skips its blocking beat; no interactive menu may open beneath a banner or cut-in; the outcome panel swallows input for ~0.5s and takes no default focus, so the first press only highlights.
U-12 High FC H-05
NP B8		 There is no way to leave a battle. The map menu offers Commanders / Speed / End Turn / Save / Cancel — no route to the main menu, no concede. Until the match ends the only exits are winning, losing, or killing the application. Add "Save & Main Menu" and a confirmed "Main Menu without saving" to the map menu, preserving the existing single-slot save model.
U-13 High FC H-06
NP B4		 The damage forecast speaks percentages while every HP display speaks tenths: "Atk 28% / Counter 30%" against a unit whose panel says "HP 8/10" reads like hit chance and forces unit conversion mid-fight. The preview never says HP, range, or that luck can move the result. Lead with HP: "Deal 5 HP · take 2–3 HP", defender "10 → 4–5 · luck included." Keep percentages as secondary detail if desired. The forecast pipeline already carries both HP snapshots — this is a formatting change, not a new computation (the cut-in's "replay, never decide" rule applies).
U-14 High VERIFIED FC H-08
NP B9 · B6		 The command-power surface fails all three jobs at once: the HUD chip's name, meter, READY state and FIRE action are nearly illegible over the board; the FIRE button is mouse-only (commander_hud_chip.gd:93, focus_mode = NONE) with nothing connecting a keyboard player to the map-menu route that exists; the ACTIVE label truncates ("ACTIVE — Coordina…"); and no player surface ever says what the charge numbers count. Opaque faction-dark backing with high-contrast status text and a distinct ready/focused treatment; a key hint on the chip pointing at the map-menu power entry; untruncated state copy; one line on the commander sheet — "charges as your armies take and deal damage."
U-15 Medium FC M-01
NP B5		 Match setup hides its decision-critical context in hover tooltips: map size/pitch, AI fairness, speed semantics, fog and animation behavior are mouse-hover-only, so keyboard players never see them. The map picker is twelve bare names — "First Steps" sounds like the tutorial and sits ninth — and the popup already touches the screen edge; the next map added will clip. Echo the tooltip line (size · properties · description) as a static caption under the dropdown; badge or order the beginner map first; one-line option help for each row; disable Difficulty when 2 Player is chosen.
U-16 Medium FC M-02
NP B3		 Blind buys: the build menu shows artwork, name, cost and affordability but no role or stats. Movement, range, and matchup strength exist only as data — no surface exposes the damage chart — so first purchases are name-recognition guesswork. A compact detail strip on the focused row: role, movement, range, strongest targets, and "acts next day." A fuller unit sheet on a secondary key can come later.
U-17 Medium VERIFIED FC M-03 A freshly bought unit is badged "Waited" (terrain_panel.gd:90). It did not wait; it was built exhausted. The status misstates what happened and misses the chance to teach production timing. "Built · ready next day." Reserve "Waited" for a unit that committed Wait.
U-18 Medium FC M-04
NP B7		 Advanced actions are guess-and-check: Dive, Supply, Join, Drop, capture interruption and "any key skips" have no explanation at the point of use. The Speed row cycles Normal→Quick→Instant blind, closing the menu per press — nothing marks it as a cycler, and reaching Instant means reopening the menu twice. A two-line focused-action explainer in the action menu; an "Any key: skip" chip during cut-ins; make Speed a real submenu (or at least mark the cycle and keep the menu open).
U-19 Medium FC M-05 Continue has no metadata and Save overwrites the sole slot anonymously — players cannot judge whether Continue is relevant or whether Save replaced the intended match. Label Continue with day and map; the Saved banner says "Saved Day N · Map."
U-20 Medium FC M-06 Battle chrome omits ownership and outcome context: no You/CPU markers, no expected income, and the victory panel never says why the match ended. Small You/CPU badges on the day chrome, "+2000 next turn" on the funds readout, and "HQ captured / army routed" on the outcome panel.
U-21 Medium NP B10 The CPU defaults to a mirror of your commander: side 2's picker opens on whatever side 1 just locked, so the path of least resistance is Alina vs Alina in matching factions. The mirror fallback handles it visually, but a first game reads better against contrast. Default the CPU to the first commander of a different faction. Costs nothing; the fallback stays for deliberate mirrors.
U-22 Medium NP A7 Board art bleeds through the HUD info panels: tall buildings and units ghost through the text rows ("Fuel 99/99" printed over a castle). Reads as a z-order glitch even though it's alpha. Raise the panel style's background alpha or add a solid underlay behind the text rows — without losing the corner-flip and cursor-dodge behaviors (see protect list).
U-23 Polish NP C1 Cut-in fade-in ghosts the board through the letterbox: HP digits and chip fragments float in the sky until the fade completes — reads as a glitch rather than a wipe. Opaque the letterbox bands from frame one; fade the diorama, not the chrome.
U-24 Polish NP C2 Iron Dominion's board sprites read as silhouettes — unit types are hard to tell apart at a glance, worse under fog dimming. More value separation in the iron ramp or a stronger rim light, keeping the faction moody. Touches the tint recipe — respect the atlas-row art contract (rows 0–2 byte-stable).
U-25 Polish NP C3 The movement-range tint nearly disappears on water (blue over blue) — selecting a sub shows barely any range at all. An outline pass or brighter overlay on sea tiles. Coordinate with the range-preview branch, which owns the overlay surfaces this touches.
U-26 Polish NP C5 Hot-seat without fog swaps turns with no beat — the camera snaps to the other army with only the small day banner as punctuation (the handoff screen deliberately gates fogged matches only). 03

Locked decisions

Where the two documents agreed, the agreement is locked; where they differed, the difference is resolved here — once.

D1 · Presentation-only, no exceptions

Every change in this plan lives in scenes/, presentation resources, and device-local settings. Nothing under core/ or ai/ learns about onboarding, banners, contrast, menu layout, or hints. Tutorial-dismissal state lives in user://settings.cfg beside game speed — it is a device preference, never match state, never saved.

D2 · Stable chrome, scrollable content

Titles, side chips, primary actions and control hints occupy reserved space on every screen; variable copy scrolls inside bounded bodies. Content length may never move navigation, and a save's presence may never change the layout budget. Every dynamic-copy surface gets an explicit height budget at 640×360 logical.

D3 · One transition convention: any press skips the beat

The sources pulled in opposite directions — "hold input until the banner ends" versus "let a press skip it." Resolved in favor of the game's own established convention (cut-ins already skip on any key): a press during a banner's blocking beat skips the beat, it is never silently swallowed. The complementary rule: no interactive menu opens beneath a banner or cut-in, and the outcome panel briefly swallows buffered input and takes no default focus.

D4 · Forecasts speak HP first

Damage communication leads with the unit the rest of the UI uses — HP out of 10 — with percentages demoted to secondary detail. The forecast displays only numbers the pipeline already carries (both HP snapshots exist); no surface recomputes combat. This is the battle cut-in's "replays, never decides" rule extended to text.

D5 · Identity is resolved once, upstream

The U-06 fix follows the faction-identity plan's letter: the cut-in director resolves the atlas row from view.identity exactly where it already resolves accent colours, and hands rows to staging. No cut-in surface may accept a raw team int where a row is meant. The regression gate is a commander-match smoke variant, because the no-CO fallback provably hides this class of bug.

D6 · Teach by doing, retire by success

Progressive, dismissible hints triggered by real scene events — no separate tutorial ruleset, no blocking slideshow, no tutorial map. The mission strip observes the same commands and events the scene already animates; each hint retires permanently after the player performs the action once.

D7 · The driven playtest harness becomes the acceptance vehicle

The audit was produced by a temporary in-engine input driver (tools/playtest_driver.gd, currently uncommitted in the small-map-camera worktree — inert unless AWPLAYTEST_DIR is set). Adopt it: the acceptance matrix in section 05 is captured by driving the real UI with real input events, the same way both audits were performed, rather than by hand each release.

D8 · Read-only previews stay read-only

Everything that shows the player more (enemy reach, fire rings, forecasts, build stats, threat context) is a pure read of the existing single authorities — AttackRange, MovementResolver, the forecast pipeline, Vision via view.can_see_unit. If an overlay or panel needs the sim to mutate, it is wrong — the range-preview plan's D1/D5 applied to every new surface here.

04

Four shippable slices

Each slice is independently shippable and playable. The order is the audit's priority order folded into the first-contact plan's delivery shape: verified micro-fixes and blockers first, then the first five minutes, then state clarity, then depth.

UX0

Ship blockers · U-01 — U-07

Make every route fit and every promise true

Open with the three verified micro-fixes — cut-in atlas rows (U-06), the commander-select ui_accept handler (U-03), the ActionMenu reset_size() (U-07) — then the layout blockers: fixed chrome and bounded scrolling on the main menu, commander selection and commander reference; remove the working-title and comparison-brand copy.

  • Primary actions stay visible with and without a save.
  • Keyboard alone can browse, confirm, start and back out at 640×360.
  • Cut-in colours match the board for every faction pairing; commander-match smoke variant added.
  • No menu ever shows a ghost panel; all commander copy readable without moving Confirm or Close.
  • No player-visible working-title or comparison-brand copy anywhere.
UX1

First five minutes · U-08 — U-10, U-13

Teach through successful actions

The mission strip and key legend, contextual acknowledgements for every rejected confirm, the end-turn ready-unit guard, and the HP-first forecast. The tutorial owns no rules — it observes the same commands and events the scene already handles (D6).

  • Select → move → action menu → capture → end turn is taught in place, then retires.
  • Every rejected confirm explains why in one line.
  • End Turn names the ready units before committing; silent when none remain.
  • Combat predicts HP dealt and returned, luck acknowledged.
UX2

State clarity & exits · U-11, U-12, U-14, U-22

Give each mode one visual owner

The D3 transition convention end to end — banners skip on press, menus never open beneath them, the outcome panel can't be mashed into a rematch — plus the deliberate exit path to the main menu, the commander-HUD legibility and keyboard-power work, and opaque info panels.

  • No press is ever silently swallowed at a state boundary, and none over-lands.
  • A match can be left — saved or abandoned — without killing the app.
  • READY, ACTIVE, FIRE and the charge meter legible over the brightest and darkest shipped maps.
  • The keyboard path to firing a power is discoverable from the chip itself.
UX3

Depth without a manual · U-15 — U-21, U-23 — U-26

Explain decisions at the point of choice

Promote tooltips into persistent captions, build-menu stats, honest unit-status copy, save/income/ outcome context, the contrasting CPU default, and the polish set (cut-in fade, iron ramp, sea overlay, hot-seat beat).

  • Map choice shows size, properties, domains and opening style without a mouse.
  • Build rows explain role, reach and "acts next day" before the purchase.
  • Continue, income, speed, cut-in skip and victory cause are explicit.
  • Polish items land opportunistically as their surfaces are touched.
05

Acceptance gates

Three gates, in increasing order of honesty: a capture matrix (driven via the D7 harness), named regression scenarios each pinned to the finding it locks down, and a blind five-minute human test.

Visual & interaction matrix (driven captures)

  • Main menu with no save and with Continue present.
  • Every commander, both side slots, at the canonical viewport.
  • Commander reference with the longest doctrine/power pair on both sides.
  • Turn banner plus attempted map/build menu — the menu waits or is never obscured.
  • Base, airport and port build lists at minimum and maximum funds.
  • Commander HUD charging, ready, active, AI-controlled and mirror-faction states.
  • Combat and capture cut-ins for every faction pairing, commanders on.
  • Mouse, keyboard and controller routes: start, cancel, save, leave to menu, rematch.

Blind five-minute test (a human, no hints from us)

  • Choose a sensible first map without hovering.
  • Start a one-player match keyboard-only and identify who is "you."
  • Move one unit and explain why a cell is reachable.
  • Inspect an enemy's reach before attacking it.
  • Capture a property and state what remains.
  • Build Infantry and predict when it can act.
  • End the turn without accidentally abandoning ready units.
  • Leave to the main menu without closing the app.

06

What already works — protect it

The audit's do-not-regress list, promoted to a gate: a UX slice that breaks any of these fails review even if its own acceptance passes.

The commander HUD chip fades when the cursor walks under it; the info panel flips corners to dodge the cursor. Never "fix" these into static opacity — U-22's opacity work must keep both behaviors.
Capture cut-in copy is exemplary — "OCCUPYING — 4/20 LEFT", "CAPTURED!", the mash chips splitting the points — and the board echoes it on hover.
The targeting flow shows attack and counter before committing; the target's panel appears while aiming. U-13 reformats the numbers, it does not restructure the flow.
The movement overlay and yellow path read clearly on land; damaged units grow HP digits; acted units desaturate; panel states are named.
Faction fallbacks hold in play: no-CO matches read "First/Second Army" in classic colours; mirror matches stay distinct; the winner line speaks faction.
Speed survives relaunch as a device preference; Save toasts; Continue restores the exact board; zoom is floor-capped on small maps; the AI's turn presents through the same cut-ins as the player's.
07

Risks

R1 · Tutorial fatigue

Captures far outnumber first launches; hints that outstay their welcome become the new friction. D6 is the mitigation: event-triggered, dismissible, permanently retired on success — and the blind five-minute test is run by someone who has played before, to catch hints that nag veterans.

R2 · Responsive regression

Larger windows are not evidence the shipped minimum works. Fix and gate at 640×360 logical first; every dynamic-copy surface gets an explicit height budget, and the capture matrix runs at the canonical viewport only.

R3 · Information overload

Keep primary rows short; role and consequence live in a focused detail area, not expanded on every row at once. The map caption and build strip are one line each.

R4 · Cross-branch collisions

Three worktrees currently touch the same presentation surfaces: range-preview (overlays, must merge before U-09/U-25), small-map-camera (zoom + the uncommitted playtest driver D7 adopts), and uncommitted promo-recording edits in battle.gd/battle_scenario_driver.gd. Land UX slices on their own branches, merge range-preview first, and adopt the playtest driver via a deliberate commit — never as a ride-along.

Locked direction

Stable chrome + scrollable content, one transition convention, contextual onboarding, HP-first forecasts, reasoned feedback, identity resolved upstream — none of it requires a gameplay, save-format or AI change, and all of it is gated by captures a driven harness can reproduce.

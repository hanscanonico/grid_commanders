Grid Commanders · design of record · shipped

Six New Commanders

The shipped twelve cover combat, movement, terrain, vision and logistics. They cover nothing else: no general in the roster touches money, and none touches the air and sea domains the naval plan added. This plan takes the roster from twelve to eighteen along three axes — price, domain and plunder — plus one power shape the game has never had, and it is honest about which of the six are free and which three buy new rules seams.

1

Three empty axes, six generals, two new seams

Read the twelve by what they are about and the gaps are obvious. Ward is adjacency, Orlov is the finisher, Rook is mobility, Holt is logistics, Quill is luck, Voss is the counterattack, Rowan is terrain, Flux is vision, Sol is indirect fire, Wren is concealment, Reed is infantry, Draeg is armour. Twelve doctrines, and every one of them is a statement about a fight that is already happening on the ground. Nobody plays the economy, and the air and sea domains the naval plan shipped have no general at all.

The six below fill those three, and Iris Colt adds the one thing no shipped power does — a Command Power that is not "a hook returns a bigger number". Three of the six need no change to core/ whatsoever — they are a subclass and a .tres, because Engagement already hands a hook both units. The other three buy two seams between them, and one of those also touches the planner. That distinction drives the milestone order: the seams land first and alone, provably behaviour-neutral, before any general is hung off them.

Six leaves the faction grid uneven, on purpose

The shipped roster is exactly 4 factions × 3. This plan drafted eight — two per faction, a clean 4 × 5 — and review cut two of them (the anti-air general and the day-decay general; both are recorded in section 7). What ships is Meridian 5, Iron 5, Aurora 4, Verdant 4.

That is a deliberate acceptance, not an oversight. Faction is flavour — CommanderType.faction is documented as "the rules never read it" — so nothing in the game breaks; the select panel simply shows two shorter columns. The two open slots are Aurora's and Verdant's, and the two ideas parked in section 7 (an income general and a comeback general) are exactly the candidates that would fill them and square the grid at 4 × 5 later. The allocation of what does ship is not arbitrary: Iron gets the elite army and the mercenary, Meridian gets the fleet and the coordination power, Aurora gets the air war, Verdant gets the swarm.

2

Decisions

Seven locks. Each is a thing that can only be decided once, because every general afterwards is built on top of it.

D1 — Price is a hook. Value is not. They are different numbers that happen to start equal.

UnitType.cost is read raw in five places today: BuildCommand.validate and .apply, ai_production_planner.gd:108 and :131, and battle_menus.gd:110. There is no single authority the way MovementResolver.step_cost is the one reader of terrain cost, so a discount added to any one of them would let the build menu, the AI's budget and the rules disagree about what a tank costs. NC2 adds that authority — one static UnitPricing.cost_for(state, team, unit_type) over a CommanderType.build_cost_pct hook — and all five readers go through it. The hook is a percentage, in the repair_cost_pct tradition already on CommanderType: 100 is the standard price, 90 is Calder, 120 is Vale. Integer percentage points like every other hook, so no doctrine can drift a price into a fraction.

But UnitType.cost is also the value currency for two systems that have nothing to do with buying: the Command Power meter (CombatResolver.bank_losses banks cost × hp_lost / 100) and the AI's target scoring (ai_unit_action_planner.gd:96). Those two keep reading the base cost, forever. Routing them through the pricing authority would mean a discount commander silently charges its opponent's meter more slowly and makes its own army read as low-value targets to the planner — two balance changes nobody asked for, hidden inside an economy doctrine. A tank is worth 7 000 to kill no matter what its owner paid.

The authority carries two invariants of its own, in the step_cost tradition: a price never falls below MIN_COST = 500, and a doctrine can never make a unit free. And it rounds down to the nearest 100, once, inside the authority — so a discounted price is a number a player can read off a menu and add up in their head, and every one of the five readers rounds it identically because none of them does the rounding.

D2 — A domain doctrine never reaches outside its domain, so a land-only board makes it exactly the neutral commander.

Measured over the shipped roster: 9 of 24 boards have an airport, 5 of 24 have a port. A general whose upside is domain-gated and whose downside falls on land units is strictly worse than picking nobody on 15 boards, and worse than that, a new player cannot see it coming from the select screen. That is a trap, not a trade-off.

Perrin Ash and Halden Marr therefore carry no malus at all: every clause of both doctrines names air units, ships, landers, or a shore tile. On boot_camp neither general has anything to apply and both play as the neutral commander, bit for bit — which is what NC4's acceptance test asserts. The alternative shape considered and rejected — Ash buffs air and debuffs ships — leaves six boards (airport, no port) where she is pure upside at zero cost anyway, so it bought nothing except a trap on the other fifteen.

The cost of no malus is that where their domain does exist, both are upside-only, and their bonuses are correspondingly small — +10%, the smallest combat numbers in the roster. A commander whose only lever is a bonus has to be tuned by that bonus alone, which is what R6 watches: combined and channel are the only guard rails these two have.

D3 — The bounty is stolen, not minted, and it comes out of the one exchange funnel.

CombatResolver.bank_losses is already the single place an exchange's value is computed, and it already receives dealer_team. Direct attacks, counterattacks and drowned cargo all route through it. Ferrow's bounty is that same computation spent on funds instead of on the meter: one hook, one call site.

It hooks there because of what that funnel excludes. TurnRules._lost_to_empty_tank deliberately never calls it — "running yourself dry is not an exchange" — so an enemy bomber falling out of the sky pays Ferrow nothing, which is the correct rule and costs nothing to get.

Stolen, not minted. Money that appears from nowhere stacks a compounding economy bonus onto a general who is, by definition, already winning the fight: money buys units, units get kills, kills buy money. Taking it from the victim's treasury is the same number with twice the swing, is self-limiting the moment the loser is broke, gives the losing player a real counter-play (spend down to nothing so there is nothing to take), and reads instantly as predation rather than as a bookkeeping bonus. Both teams are already threaded through bank_losses, so it costs nothing extra to implement. Clamped at the victim's balance; funds never go negative.

D4 — "Play again" is a saved refresh, not a turn.

An actual extra turn is a change to turn order, and turn order is load-bearing for four other things: EndTurnCommand.apply advances the seat and the day unconditionally, TurnRules.begin_turn runs income, upkeep, resupply and repair, Duration.ROUND powers expire on exactly two lines that both assume one turn per seat, and with three or four seats "again" is ambiguous — insert, or skip the next army? Then saves and replays. It is a new system, and core/commander_type.gd says why that is the wrong shape: "A Command Power is not a separate system; it is 'this hook returns a bigger number while the power runs'."

Iris Colt's Second Wind is on_power_activated setting acted = false. No turn order and no extra income, upkeep, resupply, repair, day or duration tick. It refreshes only units that have already acted this turn and did not attack. That scoping is load-bearing: a fresh build is also marked acted = true, and must never become a buy-and-fire unit.

The proposed shortcut — infer eligibility from movement and add no state — did not survive implementation review. A mid-turn save retained acted but could not tell whether that action attacked, so loading could change which units Second Wind refreshes. The shipped rule is additive and exact: Unit.refreshable is set by a committed non-attack action, cleared by an actual attack and at turn start, and remains false on a build. Save format v8 carries it; older saves default false rather than grant an action that cannot be proven. This supersedes the paragraph's original “no new state, no save format change” claim while preserving the decision that Second Wind is a refresh rather than an extra turn.

D5 — When the AI asks for its power is a commander fact, not a difficulty weight.

Before this plan, AIController.plan_next_command asked for the power first, before any unit acts — the file says so: "Command Powers stay here because they precede every ordinary action." Every existing doctrine wants that, because every existing power is "hooks return bigger numbers for the rest of this turn." A refresh power is the exact inverse: fired before anything has moved, it refreshes twelve already-fresh units and does nothing at all. The AI would burn a full meter for zero value, every turn, forever — the banked-meter failure the doctrine toolkit exists to avoid, in its most embarrassing form.

So CommanderType gains @export var power_timing: Timing — BEFORE_ACTIONS (default) or AFTER_ACTIONS — and plan_next_command asks at the matching point. It is data on the general, like power_duration beside it, not an AIProfile weight: the difficulty plan's lock says a tier may only change which profile the planner weighs moves with, and a general's sense of timing is a match fact, not a difficulty smart. Every shipped commander keeps the default, so make difficulty-check stays byte-stable.

D6 — Behaviour in the subclass, numbers on the .tres, integer percentage points.

The repo rule, restated because six new generals at once is where it would erode. Every number in section 3 is an @export on the general's resource so the balance matrix can move it without a code change, every hook returns integer percentage points so no doctrine can drift the golden damage values, and no hook touches a Node. Calder's 90 and Vale's 120 are exported numbers on two .tres files sharing one subclass shape; Ferrow's 10% bounty is exported beside them. Nothing in section 3 is a literal in a .gd.

D7 — Two new balance fixtures ship before the generals that need them.

The three scenarios run tiny economies: clash gives each army 3 properties, or 3 000 a turn. A price doctrine measured there is measured where it does almost nothing, while on heartland (60 properties, ~15 000 a turn per army) it compounds all match. A bounty doctrine has the opposite problem: killing a full-HP Tank pays Ferrow 700, which is a quarter of a turn's income on clash and a twentieth on heartland — a five-fold swing in strength that has nothing to do with the number on the card. And no fixture lets a fleet meet a fleet, so Marr would be measured on three boards where two thirds of his doctrine does not exist.

NC1 adds holdings (high property count) and channel (connected water, land armies still meet on day one) to SCENARIO_NAMES. Instruments before subjects.

The six, one by one

Every number below began as a starting value for the balance matrix to move. The cards now record the shipped figures after NC7's full pass. Power costs are on the original roster's scale (10 000–14 000) except where the text says otherwise and says why.

Ines Calder

Verdant League Price · cheap and many
Doctrine
Every unit costs 10% less to build. All units −10% attack.
Power — Weight of Numbers · 12 000 · owner's turn
All units +25% attack this turn. (The hook returns +15 while active; her standing −10 is folded in, so the card can promise a number the player can read off the damage preview.)
What −10% across the board actually buys her
Infantry 1 000 → 900, Tank 7 000 → 6 300, Battleship 28 000 → 25 200. The discount reaches every tier — she does not have to swarm, she can simply out-build. That is a wider identity than a foot-only discount, and it is also the harder one to balance, because money compounds and stats do not: her −10% attack is a fixed tax per exchange while the discount grows with every turn of income she survives. Advance Wars' Colin ran −20% / −10% and was strong for exactly this reason. The proposed 20% discount measured 73.6% on holdings; moving that one export to 10% brought the same 18-commander slice to 56.9% and the final full matrix to 49.3%. The attack malus stayed put because it is what makes her fights legible.

Perrin Ash

Aurora Compact Domain · air
Doctrine
Air units +10% attack. No drawback.
Power — Air Superiority · 9 000 · through the opponent's next turn
Air units +10% attack until her next turn — +20% in total while it runs.
The cleanest doctrine in the roster, and the quietest
Nothing outside the air domain, in either direction (D2), so on the 15 boards with no airport she is bit-identical to the neutral commander. Where there is an airport she is a straight upgrade to an expensive, fragile domain — which is a real effect, since a Bomber costs 22 000 and a Fighter 20 000, and a tenth more damage on a unit that expensive is a tenth more of the most valuable shot on the board.
The power cost came down with the power
9 000, below the roster's floor of 10 000, because +10% on one domain through one round is the smallest Command Power in the game and pricing it beside Rhea Sol's 14 000 Grid Saturation would mean a meter she fills once a match for almost nothing. The ROUND duration stays: air is the domain that has to fly home, so a power that expired at end of turn would be spent on the approach.
She has no answer general
The drafted counterpart — an anti-air denial general — was cut in review (section 7). So the air domain now has a general who is for it and none who is against it. That is survivable because the units already answer her (Anti-Air, Missiles, Fighters are in every roster and the AI's build list has an air-answer tier that outranks any doctrine bias), but it is worth knowing that the rock-paper-scissors here is unit-level, not commander-level.

Halden Marr

Meridian Coalition Domain · sea and shore
Doctrine
Ships and landers +10% attack and +1 movement. Any of his units standing on a shoal, reef or port gets +1 defence star. No drawback.
Power — Make for the Shore · 12 000 · owner's turn
Ships and landers +2 movement; every unit on a shoal, reef or port +1 further defence star and +10% attack this turn.
His biggest lever is the movement, not the damage
+10% attack on a domain is small; +1 movement on every hull is not. A Cruiser at 7 and a Lander at 7 change which shore is reachable this turn and which is not, and that is what a naval doctrine is actually about. Read the card top to bottom and the damage line is flavour on top of a mobility doctrine — which is also why he survives having no drawback: the movement is the thing to tune, and it moves in whole tiles.
Shore, not deep water — and why that widens him by nine boards
"Ships hit harder" alone would be dead on 19 of 24 boards. The shore clause reaches any board with a coastline, which is most of the ones with water at all, and it makes his identity legible without a fleet: his infantry hold a beach, his landers arrive faster. It also sidesteps the naval plan's standing R1 — the AI cannot plan a ferry and never builds transports — because none of his doctrine depends on a ferry existing. shoal, reef and port are all real terrain resources under data/terrain/; the hook asks TerrainType by id, never by a hardcoded list of map characters.
Quotes
"The coast is a road. Learn to read it." · "Every shore is somebody's front door."

Dane Ferrow

Iron Dominion Economy · plunder
Doctrine
Destroying a unit takes 10% of its build cost from its owner's treasury and gives it to yours (clamped at what they have). All units −10% defence.
Power — Collect · 10 000 · owner's turn
Bounties this turn take 20% in total instead of 10%. Nothing else: no attack bonus, no movement.
One rate, stated twice
The doctrine and the power are the same number at two settings — the standing 10% and the fired 20% — so the card is one sentence a player can hold in their head, and the hook is one exported integer with a doubling gate on _is_active. It is priced at 10 000, the roster floor, beside Gideon Holt's Open the Depots: both are pure-utility powers that put nothing on the board and change no damage number. The turn it fires is a turn Ferrow goes looking for the most expensive thing he can kill, which is the decision the whole general exists to create.
Self-limiting by construction
10% of a 16 000 Md Tank is 1 600 — 3 200 with Collect up; 10% of an Infantry is 100. The bounty rewards killing expensive things, which is already the hard and interesting decision, and pays almost nothing for farming foot units. It pays on counterattacks too — the funnel is shared — which suits a general whose own defence is worse. And because it is stolen (D3), the swing is double the number on the card and it dries up entirely against an opponent who spends their income the turn they get it.
The AI plays this one better than you would expect
ai_unit_action_planner.gd:96 already multiplies an attack's value by profile.kill_bonus when the forecast kills, so the planner already prefers securing the killing blow rather than spreading damage. The bounty amplifies a preference it has. What it cannot do is want kills more than another general does — kill_bonus is an AIProfile weight written per difficulty tier, and the one per-commander planner dial is doctrine_weight. So he will be mildly underplayed by the computer. That is an accepted gap, not a defect: three shipped generals advise the planner nothing at all.

"Every wreck is a receipt." · "Your budget, my payroll."

Iris Colt

Meridian Coalition Tempo · act again
Doctrine
All units −10% attack and −10% defence. The standing tax on the strongest power in the game.
Power — Second Wind · 22 000 · owner's turn · fires AFTER_ACTIONS
Every unit that has already acted this turn and did not attack may act again.
Why 22 000 when the roster tops out at 14 000
The meter fills at 100% of value lost plus 50% of value dealt, so a cost is a statement about how much fighting buys the effect. A second movement phase for an entire army — repositioning out of a threat range, closing a gap, finishing a capture the opponent thought they had a turn to stop — is the largest single swing any power in this game can produce, and it should take most of a match to earn. If the matrix says 22 000 is still cheap, raise it before touching the effect: the effect is the identity.
Two things the scoping buys
"Did not attack" makes it a reposition power rather than a double alpha strike, which is both easier to balance and the more interesting decision. And it excludes units bought this turn through the saved Unit.refreshable eligibility (D4), so the game's oldest rule — a new build waits a turn — survives a power and a mid-turn save.

What this does to the balance gate

The matrix is O(N²), and this is a 3.75× bill

Today12 × 12 × 3 scenarios × 4 seeds = 1 728
After NC1 (5 scenarios)12 × 12 × 5 × 4 = 2 880
After NC718 × 18 × 5 × 4 = 6 480 matches

A 3.75× release task. Two thirds of that is the scenario list, not the generals: the two fixtures alone take the current roster from 1 728 to 2 880. That is real, and it is the price of measuring economy and naval doctrines at all — but the runner already has the answer for day-to-day work: --commanders=, --scenarios= and --seeds= focused mode is the iteration loop, and the full batch stays out of make verify and make test where it already is. Measure and record the new wall-clock in NC7 rather than discovering it during a release.

If it proves intolerable, the lever is the scenario list, not the pairings: a doctrine that cannot exist on a board (Marr on clash) is 288 matches of measuring nothing, and two of the six generals are in that position on three of the five boards. Per-general scenario subsets would cut the batch by roughly a fifth — but that is a runner change with its own byte-stability bar, and it is out of scope here.

The matrix will misread four of these six

docs/commander_balance.md already warns about exactly this: "The AI is rule-based and can underuse terrain, timing, or a specialised economy effect. A commander outside the preferred win band is a review trigger, not an automatic numeric nerf." This plan adds three specialised economy effects, so that paragraph stops being a caveat and becomes the operating instruction.

  • Calder and Vale will under-read on the small fixtures and are tuned on holdings.
  • Ferrow will over-read on the small fixtures, for the same reason inverted, and is tuned on holdings too.
  • Colt depends on the planner using a second movement phase well, which is exactly the kind of thing a greedy planner does adequately and a human does brilliantly. Expect the human deck to rate her above her win rate.

None of these get a numeric nerf off the leaderboard alone. All four get a human deck row. Ash and Marr are the two the matrix reads honestly — provided they are read on combined and channel and nowhere else.

The evidence this plan is built on

Measured on the shipped repository, not assumed: 9 of 24 boards carry an airport, 5 of 24 carry a port (hence D2). Income is 1 000 per property; clash seats 3 properties per army, heartland about 15 — a five-fold swing in what a bounty or a discount is worth (hence D7). The meter banks 100% of value lost and 50% of value dealt, which is how Colt's 22 000 was sized. Portraits are generated from a FACES table rather than drawn, so six new generals put no artist on the critical path — the per-general cost is a subclass, a .tres, a table row, two quotes and a test.

6

Risk register

R1 · high — The economy snowball

Money buys units, units get kills, kills buy money. Calder and Ferrow are both on that loop, and Calder's discount is proportional, so unlike an absolute rebate it cheapens the expensive units that end games as much as the cheap ones that start them — 6 300 Tanks and 25 200 Battleships compound faster than 900 Infantry. This is the largest single balance risk in the plan. Mitigation: Ferrow's bounty is stolen rather than minted (D3), so it is self-limiting against a poor opponent and gives the loser real counter-play; both generals are tuned on holdings, the board where the loop is fastest; and the discount rate is one exported integer, so a nerf is a data change and a re-run rather than a redesign. Tripwire: if either exceeds the win band on holdings while sitting inside it everywhere else, that is the snowball — and the lever is the price percentage, not the attack malus.

R2 · high — Second Wind is the strongest effect in the game

A whole army moving twice can end a match outright, and a power that ends matches makes every other decision in the game smaller. Mitigation: the "did not attack" scoping (D4) makes it a reposition rather than a second alpha strike; 22 000 is well above the roster ceiling; the standing −10/−10 taxes every turn she has not fired it. Tripwire: if the human deck reports "the match was decided by Second Wind" more than it reports "Second Wind saved my line", the effect narrows further — refresh foot units only — before the cost moves again.

R3 · medium — A fixture that measures the clock instead of the doctrines

The combined history is the warning: its first version separated the armies with water, day-capped in 430 of 432 matches, and produced a twenty-point first-side bias out of the tiebreak alone. channel is the same trap by construction — it exists to put water in the middle. Mitigation: a land bridge, and the NC1 acceptance bar is a measured day-cap rate, not a visual inspection.

R4 · medium — The pricing seam leaks into value

Five call sites move to the authority and two deliberately do not. The failure is silent: route bank_losses through it by accident and Calder's opponents charge their meters more slowly forever, discovered as "the balance numbers moved and nobody knows why". Mitigation: D1 written into the comment at both non-routed call sites, and an NC2 test that asserts the meter banks base cost while a discount is active.

R5 · medium — Six doctrines is six more chances for a rule seam to split

The standing lesson from the hook layers: a rule that lives in both the resolver and a command's validate() is a rule that unit tests miss and an AI-vs-AI soak catches. Pricing is exactly that shape — it lives in validate, in apply, in the menu and in the planner's budget. Mitigation: one authority (D1) and a soak in NC3, not just unit tests.

R6 · medium — Ash and Marr are upside-only

Review removed both drawbacks (D2), so on a board with an airport or a port each is a free bonus over the neutral commander with nothing paid for it. A commander with no cost cannot be balanced by moving what they give up; the only dial is the bonus itself, which is why both sit at +10% — the smallest combat numbers in the roster. Mitigation: they are measured only on the boards where their domain exists, so a misread cannot hide behind fifteen neutral results. Tripwire: either above the win band on combined or channel means the bonus drops to +5% or acquires a drawback — and if it acquires one, D2 says it must live inside the same domain.

R7 · low — "One city buys one infantry" stops being true

Every price today is a multiple of the 1 000 income, which makes the tutorial boards teach a clean lesson. Calder's Infantry at 900 and Vale's at 1 200 break the correspondence. Accepted: leftover funds simply bank, the menu shows the real price, the authority rounds to 100 so no price is ever an awkward number, and the two price generals are not the ones a first-time player meets — the tutorial board leads with the neutral commander.

R8 · low — Naval R1 still stands

The AI cannot plan a ferry and never builds transports. Marr is designed around it rather than against it — nothing in his doctrine needs a lander to reach anywhere — and channel satisfies R1 by shape, the way atoll does: one body of water touching both ports.

Deliberately out of scope

  • Yuna Sorel, an anti-air denial general (Aurora Compact) — Anti-Air, Missiles and Fighters buffed, the whole doctrine priced in cash through NC2's seam. Drafted as the rock to Perrin Ash's scissors and cut in review. The consequence is recorded on Ash's card: the air domain has a general for it and none against it, so that particular rock-paper-scissors stays unit-level. She would fit Aurora's open slot if the roster ever goes to 4 × 5.
  • Bram Osei, a day-decay general (Verdant League) — +30% attack on day 1 falling to nothing by day 11, the whole strategy stated as "win by day 8 or lose". Drafted, free to build (the hook reads GameState.day) and cut in review. With him goes the only doctrine in either roster that plays differently late than early, and the only one the 20-day balance cap would not have been inert for. Verdant's open slot.
  • A literal extra turn. Considered and rejected in D4, with the reasons. If it is ever revisited it is a turn-order plan, not a commander plan, and it starts with saves and replays rather than with a doctrine.
  • An income commander. "+X per property" needs a seam in TurnRules.begin_turn, and it would sit on the same axis Calder and Ferrow already occupy from two more interesting angles. The seam is cheap; the roster slot is not. A future plan that takes the roster to 4 × 6 can have it.
  • A comeback commander whose bonus scales with how far behind they are. Genuinely free to build and a real gap in the roster's emotional range — but it is the doctrine most likely to feel arbitrary in the moment ("why did that hurt so much?") and it needs its own legibility work on the HUD. Named here so it is not lost, and left for the 4 × 6 plan.
  • Per-commander AIProfiles. Rejected once already by the doctrine-AI plan: a tier × commander matrix forks every future weight eighteen ways. Ferrow's mild underplay (section 3) is the accepted cost of that decision, not a reason to reopen it.
  • New units. Nothing here adds a unit type, a weapon or a damage-chart row. Six doctrines over the existing roster is already the largest single change to the balance surface since the naval domains.
  • Campaign integration. Which of the eighteen appear in the Ember Line, and as whom, is the campaign story's decision and not this plan's.
  • Telemetry. The balance simulator observes and never instruments (balance plan D2); nothing here adds a hook for it, the bounty included.

Commander Doctrine AI

Each general's army, when a computer plays it, should play like that general: Sable Wren's line lives in the woods, Viktor Draeg fields armour, Tomas Reed floods the map with infantry, Gideon Holt rotates worn units home because his repairs are cheap. This plan closes the gap between the doctrines the sim already enforces and the choices the planner makes.

1

Half of this already works, for free — the plan is the other half

The planner never re-derives a rule: it scores attacks with CombatResolver.forecast, walks moves with MovementResolver.reachable, and both call every CommanderType hook unconditionally. So every combat and movement passive already steers the AI without a line of AI code: Cass Orlov's finisher bonus raises the forecast on wounded targets and the kill multiplier compounds it; Rhea Sol's indirect bonus makes her artillery outscore her tanks; Nia Rowan's terrain discount makes the flood fill reach farther through woods, so her infantry already route through them; Mara Voss's initiate penalty already discounts the attacks she shouldn't open. Power timing is also solved: wants_power lives on each doctrine (commanders plan, C4) and five generals already override it.

What the resolvers cannot carry into the planner is everything that is not a forecast of a single exchange:

Gap 1 — Ground. The advance path is terrain-blind.

_advance_value is distance to a goal minus threat, nothing else. No unit — anyone's — prefers to end a quiet move on defensive terrain, so Wren's woods bonus, Voss's hold-the-line and Ward's mixed-line adjacency never shape a turn that doesn't fire a shot.

Gap 2 — Production. One global build list for twelve doctrines.

AIProfile.build_priority is the same for every general. Draeg (armour +15%) buys the same roster as Reed (foot +15%, vehicles −10%), so both waste their bonus half the time and pay their malus the other half.

Gap 3 — Power setup. Nobody stages their own power.

Wren's wants_power correctly refuses to fire Vanish with nobody in cover — but the planner never puts anyone in cover, so on open ground she can sit on a full meter all match. The gate and the planner disagree about whose job the precondition is. Rook is the mirror image: he never overrode wants_power, so Rapid Redeployment (−20% attack, the repositioning power) fires on the default offensive read — exactly the turn it penalises.

Gap 4 — Economics. Retreat is priced for the neutral commander.

AIProfile.retreat_hp = 45 for everyone. Holt repairs at 80% and tops the army up when the depots open; his units should rotate home earlier because the round trip costs him less. He has no combat hook at all, so the forecasts give his AI nothing.

2

Decisions

D1 — Advice lives on CommanderType, beside wants_power

The AI-advisory hooks join the doctrine subclass, with their numbers @export on the general's .tres — exactly the pattern wants_power set and defended: "a general's sense of timing lives beside the rest of its doctrine instead of in the planner." A general's sense of ground and production belongs in the same file, where the doctrine it expresses is three lines up. The base class returns zero from every advisory hook, so the neutral commander — and any general with nothing to say — advises nothing and the planner is bit-for-bit what it was.

Rejected: a mirror tree of adviser classes under ai/commanders/. Twelve more files, each doctrine split across two trees that can drift apart — the exact bug class the hook-layer split has bitten this repo with before. The hooks are advisory reads of sim state; they don't make core/ depend on the planner.

D2 — One planner dial: AIProfile.doctrine_weight

How strongly advice re-ranks candidates is a planner weight, so it lives on AIProfile like every other one. 0.0 skips the hooks entirely — the threat_aversion pattern: at zero the code that reads them never runs and planning is byte-identical to today. The shipped default is on at every tier: a commander's personality is a match fact the player chose, not a difficulty smart — Easy's Sable Wren still loves woods; the tiers may retune the strength but never gate the behaviour. This stays inside the difficulty lock ("difficulty may only change which AIProfile the planner weighs moves with"): it is one more field on the same resource. Per AIProfile's own header rule, the field is written into every profile under data/ai/, and test_ai_profile.gd holds them to it.

D3 — Exactly three hooks, each mapped to one planner seam

Hook (on CommanderType)CurrencyPlanner seam
stand_value(state, unit, cell) -> int Tiles, like advance_threat_tiles — how many tiles of progress standing here is worth giving up. _advance_value adds doctrine_weight × stand_value. The advance path only: attack destinations already price doctrine through the forecast's counter and terrain (R1 below).
build_bias(state, team, unit_type) -> int Places on the build list, like duplicate_priority_cost — negative pulls a unit up, positive pushes it down. _build_rank's priority tier, after the reactive/static rank and duplicate cost. The air-answer and capture-shortage tiers outrank it untouched: no doctrine talks the AI out of answering bombers.
retreat_hp_delta(state, unit) -> int Displayed-HP points added to profile.retreat_hp. _advance_goal's repair gate.

Power staging is not a fourth hook. stand_value receives the state, and the meter is sim state (commander_state(team).charge vs power_cost) — so Wren's implementation simply values woods more as Vanish approaches READY. The precondition and the gate that checks it end up reading the same doctrine file.

Rejected: a per-commander build_priority list override. Twelve hand-ordered lists to keep in sync with the roster and with S3's reactive re-rank; a rank delta composes with both and with the duplicate tax instead of replacing them.

D4 — Advice never cheats, never rolls, never double-counts

Three standing rules for every hook implementation, enforced by review and by the seam tests: (1) it reads only what the planner already reads — terrain, its own units, its own meter; anything of the enemy's goes through the same Vision.is_hidden_from discipline _can_strike uses. (2) It is pure, integer-valued and RNG-free, so planning stays deterministic and scan-order ties still break as they do today. (3) It never reads the damage chart — expected damage is the forecast's job, and a hook that re-derived it would count the same doctrine twice (once in the forecast, once in the advice).

D5 — The merge bar, restated for a change that is supposed to move balance

Advice changes AI decisions by design, so the byte-diff bar splits: make difficulty-check stays byte-stable (its matches seat no commanders; neutral advice is structurally zero, and that identity is the proof the seam is inert when unused) — while make commander-balance is expected to move and is regenerated and read, per docs/commander_balance.md, with the July 2026 grand sweep as the baseline. That sweep is also the motivation: its Gideon and Sable findings are partly artefacts of an AI that doesn't play either doctrine — a commander the planner can't express is measured weaker than the commander is.

4

Milestones

CA1 — The seam shipped

The three advisory hooks on CommanderType (neutral returns zero), doctrine_weight on AIProfile and written into every data/ai/*.tres, and the three planner read-sites (_advance_value, _build_rank, _advance_goal). No commander implements anything yet.

Gate: with doctrine_weight = 0 or a neutral commander, planning is byte-identical — proven by make difficulty-check staying byte-stable and a seam GUT test that plans the same fixed-seed turn with the field at 0 and with a no-advice commander. test_ai_profile.gd extended for the new field.

CA2 — The ground doctrines shipped

stand_value for Wren, Voss and Ward, meter-aware for Wren; the Rook wants_power correction. Each ships with a GUT test asserting the observable choice on a fixed board: Wren's tank ends an equal-distance advance in woods rather than plains, and stages into cover on a near-full meter so a subsequent turn can fire Vanish (the stall, as a regression test); Voss holds the mountain when reachable; Ward's quiet move ends beside a different-class friendly; Rook banks his meter on a turn he could attack and spends it on a turn he can only gain ground.

Gate: the four tests, plus make verify; difficulty-check still byte-stable.

CA3 — The economy doctrines shipped

build_bias for Draeg, Reed, Sol, Rook and Flux; retreat_hp_delta for Holt. Tests assert rank order out of _build_rank per commander (Draeg's base ranks a tank over the infantry the default list ties it with; Reed the inverse; Sol's base pulls artillery ahead) and that the air-answer and capture-shortage tiers still win over any bias; Holt's 60 HP tank seeks repair where the neutral commander's advances.

Gate: the tests, make verify, and a played sanity check of one Draeg-vs-Reed watch match: the two rosters visibly diverge.

CA4 — The proof, and the tune shipped

Regenerate make commander-balance and read it against the July 2026 grand-sweep baseline per docs/commander_balance.md — the question is whether the doctrine-played commanders' win rates move toward each other, with Wren and Holt (the sweep's named outliers) the ones to watch. Tune the advisory @exports and doctrine_weight on that evidence; every tuning change is a .tres edit. Update docs/commander_balance.md with the new baseline and CLAUDE.md's plan index with this plan's entry.

Gate: the report regenerated and its findings written down; no commander's advice made the AI worse with that commander than the neutral planner was (the sweep answers this per pairing); an AI-vs-AI soak with commanders seated completes on the shipped boards.

5

Risk register

R1 — Double counting a doctrine

The forecasts already carry every combat passive; advice that re-prices one (Wren valuing woods because of the ambush damage, say) counts it twice and over-rotates the AI. Mitigation is D4's third rule — hooks never read the damage chart — and the review question for every implementation: "is this value visible to a forecast?" If yes, the hook stays silent about it. Lyra is the accepted inverse case: luck is visible to no forecast, and stays unpriced.

R2 — Advice that fights the planner

A tile-denominated bias too large stops armies advancing at all (the advance_threat_tiles lesson: below ~1.6 it is inert, far above it the unit cowers). Starting values sit near the working threat-dial numbers, and CA4's soak is the check that every army still closes, captures and wins. If a doctrine AI visibly stalls, the fix is the .tres number, not code.

R3 — The balance reports move and the bar blurs

Every commander-seated report changes by design, so "the diff is expected" could hide a real regression. D5 keeps one hard identity — difficulty-check byte-stable, since it seats no commanders — and makes the commander-balance regeneration a read deliverable with named questions, not a rubber stamp. Both-sides regeneration per the recorded merge-bar procedure (reports/ is gitignored).

R4 — Determinism and scan order

Advice joins score comparisons that break ties by scan order with strict operators. Integer-valued hooks scaled by one float keep the arithmetic stable; the CA1 seam test runs a fixed-seed turn twice and byte-compares the command log. No hook may allocate per-call state or read the RNG.

R5 — A hook that peeks

Advice reading enemy positions without the Vision.is_hidden_from filter would let a doctrine plan around units its side cannot see — a cheat, and a violation of the difficulty plan's lock in spirit. Voss's "while an opponent can strike" gate reuses _opponents_can_strike, which already carries the discipline; any new enemy-reading helper goes through the same toolkit.

R6 — Tier inheritance trap

A new AIProfile field left out of a shipped tier's .tres silently inherits the code default — the exact trap the class header documents. doctrine_weight is written into every profile under data/ai/ in CA1 and test_ai_profile.gd holds them to it, as it does for every other field.

6

Deliberately out of scope

  • Multi-turn intent. Rook's "true" redeploy — planning a repositioning turn and cashing it in later — needs plan-ahead the greedy planner doesn't have. His fix here is the corrected trigger and build bias; a lookahead planner is its own future plan.
  • Luck in forecasts. Pricing Lyra's floor into scoring means pricing it into CombatResolver.forecast, which is also the player's damage preview. Not for this plan.
  • Transports. Still unbuilt, still naval plan R1. No doctrine bias may pull a transport into the build (the hooks bias ranks; transports have no rank to bias).
  • Per-commander AIProfiles. The tier × commander matrix as twelve more .tres files would fork every future weight twelve ways. One dial plus per-doctrine numbers on the commander's own resource covers it.
  • Telemetry. The balance simulator observes and never instruments (balance plan D2); nothing here adds a hook for it.

Grid Commanders · design of record · proposed · AE1–AE4 · 2026-08-02

AI Economy

A player wins a match on Difficult and cannot say what they did. They were not out-fought — the computer attacked all match, and on day 12 an infantry of its was standing on their headquarters. They were out-earned: 43 properties to 21, four idle factory turns in the opening, and half the board the computer never sent a single unit toward. This plan gives the planner the thing that no combat weight reaches — a model of the map as an economy: enough infantry to race for it, goals that fan out across it, and a price on the ground that builds tanks.

01

What you can see at the table

One match, read out of its own recording. Nothing here is an impression: every number below comes from a replay the game wrote by itself and from the offline analyser that re-issues it, and every cause is a line of shipped code.

“I have the feeling the AI just let me take the whole map.”

The feeling is right about the result and wrong about the cause. The computer was not passive — it opened an attack on day 2, fought a running battle down the western corridor for fourteen days, and on day 12 began capturing the player's home headquarters and was killed before the second turn of it. It lost because it never bought, and never sent, anything that could take ground.

Marchlands, Difficult, fog off. Player seat 1 (Dane Ferrow), computer seat 4 (Alina Ward), seats 2 and 3 closed. Player won on day 21. Recording: user://replays/2026-08-02T16-58-22.jsonl. Reproduce with make replay-report REPLAY=<file> ARGS="--team=4".

Observation 1 — it opened with three idle factory turns, on purpose

Playerd1 infantry · d2 APC · d3 infantry · d4 tank
Computerd1 bank 4 000 · d2 bank 8 000 · d3 bank 12 000 · d4 Md Tank

Cause. The infantry target is exactly the starting roster, so the urgent tier is empty on turn one of every board.

  • capture_unit_target = 3 (ai_profile.gd:64, and 3 in every tier .tres). Every shipped board deals each army 2 infantry + 1 mech, and those are the only two units with can_capture = true. So wants.short_of_capture_units is false before the first command of the match.
  • With the urgent tier empty, _build_rank (ai_production_planner.gd:147) falls through to the priority list, whose head on Difficult is md_tank at 16 000.
  • _worth_waiting_for (:119) then asks one question and only one: could I afford something better within save_up_turns? Yes — so bank. It never asks what an idle factory costs while the map is being taken.
  • The banking rule's own guard is best_rank < wants.first_priority_rank(), which is to say “never bank when something urgent is wanted.” The guard works perfectly. It is simply never true, because the target is met at boot.

The analyser names it four times without knowing why: hoarding on days 1, 2, 3 and 9 — “ended the turn on 14 900 funds with an idle factory that builds from 1 000.”

The call is not wrong everywhere — it is wrong here

Saving three turns for a Md Tank is a fine trade on a cramped board with little to take, where the match really is decided by which side fields the stronger few units. What makes it the wrong call on Marchlands is the twenty neutral properties sitting on it: every turn spent banking is a turn the other side converts into permanent income, and that compounds while a one-off 16 000 purchase does not.

So the fix is not “bank less”. It is “bank less when there is a map to take” — which is exactly what D5's property-scaled roster buys, and why D6 leaves the banking branch alone. The one case that argues the other way — banking for a decisive unit because there is a knockout available now — is a capability the planner does not have and this plan does not build; it is recorded in R2 and §6.

Observation 2 — its capture units converge, so half the board is never anyone's goal

All ten properties the computer captured lie between x=1 and x=15 on a 22-wide board. It never sent a unit to (18,14) — a factory sixteen tiles down its own side of the ridge, on the same lateral road as its headquarters. The player took it on days 12–13 and built out of it on days 14, 15, 16 and 20.

seats 2 and 3 closed → their corners are neutral ground x= 0 . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . . 18 19 21 y=1 [1]HQ [1]B · · · · · · · · · [ ]B [ ]Q ← player took both, d7–d14 y=14 [4]HQ [4]B · · · · · · · · · [ ]B [ ]Q ← player took both, d12–d15 ↑ computer's own half — it never sent a unit past x=15

Cause. The capture goal is nearest-only, per unit, with nothing claiming a target.

  • _advance_goal (ai_unit_action_planner.gd:468–478) collects every unowned property and returns _nearest(unit.cell, capturable) — plain Manhattan distance (:530).
  • Every capture-capable unit runs that independently and nothing marks a property as already someone's. Three infantry standing near each other pick the same tile and walk it together.
  • There is nowhere for a claim to live today, and that is by design: AIPlanningContext.begin ends with goals.clear() (ai_planning_context.gd:61) — the goal cache is one command deep, and the threat map is the one fact deliberately allowed to survive between decisions.
  • The consequence compounds with distance: on a board dense in properties there is always a nearer tile than the far corner, so the far corner is never chosen by anybody, ever, at any point in the match.

The analyser sees the near half of this as six missed_capture findings — a unit that could have started a capture and walked past. It cannot see the far half at all, because a property nobody ever approached produces no finding.

Observation 3 — a factory is priced as a field

The two corners the player took were not cities. They were two headquarters and two factories belonging to the closed seats — under the open-seats decision D1 a closed seat's ground opens to neutral, so it is there to be taken by whoever wants it. The player gained two extra production lines out of them and used both.

Cause. Nothing in ai/ knows a property builds anything.

  • _consider_captures (:193) scores capture_score, times hq_capture_multiplier for an HQ, plus the progress bonus. A base, an airport and a port score exactly as a plains city.
  • TerrainType.builds is read by BuildCommand, the build menu and the production planner's facility scan — and by nothing on the capture path. The authority for “this ground makes units” exists and the capture score has never asked it.
  • The goal distance does not ask either, so a factory can never outbid a nearer city even in principle.

What those three add up to

The computer built eight more units across days 13–20 and still shrank: it was replacing losses back to a roster of three capturers plus whatever the priority list bought, out of an income that never grew. From day 16 it is three units inside its own corner. That is not a side declining to take the map. It is a side that has nothing left to take it with.

The one sentence

The planner buys and moves for the fight in front of it, and has no model of the map as an economy. Every property judgement it owns is either a constant that happens to equal the starting roster, a nearest-neighbour walk, or a flat price — so the side that simply keeps taking ground wins on income long before the fighting resolves anything.

02

The boundary with the balance retune — read this before writing code

This plan is the third to touch ai/ in a fortnight. The other two drew their boundaries in writing and this one inherits both.

What this plan owns

  • Planner capabilities about property and production: how many capture units an army wants, which property a unit walks to, and what a property is worth when it is taken.
  • New @exports on AIProfile, each written into every tier at its inert value.
  • A measured band per dial, handed to BL2 in docs/difficulty_check.md.

What it does not own

  • Any shipped number in data/ai/. Those are BL2's, by the AI Judgement plan's D2, and this plan repeats that boundary rather than reopening it.
  • The difficulty ladder itself. Difficulty may only change which AIProfile the planner weighs moves with — the AI never cheats at any tier, and nothing here gives it income, vision, damage or luck it did not earn.
  • Anything under core/. See D7.

Why now, and the honest cost

The DF4 ladder gate fails knowingly: 68.3% Normal-over-Easy and 53.3% Difficult-over-Normal against a required 70%, measured 2026-08-01 at 15 seeds with the Judgement dials live. Its remedy is named — BL2 must close the gap by making Difficult better rather than Normal worse — and BL2's problem is that it has run out of numbers that make Difficult better. These are three capabilities that could, and none of them is available as a weight today.

The cost is stated rather than hidden. These capabilities apply to every tier, so switching them on for Difficult alone is a tier split — and §4c of docs/difficulty_check.md records four tier splits tried at 15 seeds and four failures, because the gate measures the difference and improving the middle rung closes it from the other side. This plan therefore ships the capabilities inert and hands BL2 the bands; it does not promise the gate. R5 carries that risk in full.

03

Decisions

Locked unless a later plan supersedes them in writing.

D1 — Every capability ships inert, and inert skips the code

The same contract as the AI Judgement plan's D1 and the difficulty plan's S1–S3. Each dial ships at the value that reproduces today's planner — 0.0 for an additive or a rate, 1.0 for a multiplier, 0 for a count — and at that value the new code is not executed, not merely neutral. A per-capability suite builds an explicitly zeroed profile and proves the branch is skipped.

The merge bar for every milestone is a fixed-seed byte-diff of both balance reports, with no accepted departure — make commander-balance and make difficulty-check. reports/ is gitignored, so generate both sides. And test_capability_defaults_plan_exactly_like_the_shipped_profile (tests/unit/test_ai_smarts.gd:480) keeps passing unchanged: an install with no profile file plays the same game, which means ai/ai_profile.gd's class defaults and data/ai/default.tres move together or not at all.

D2 — This plan owns capabilities; BL2 owns the tier numbers

No AE milestone edits a shipped value in data/ai/, and AE4's deliverable is a probe band in docs/difficulty_check.md, never a tier value. Inherited verbatim from the AI Judgement plan's D2, for the same reason: two plans editing the same numbers means neither can be measured.

One consequence worth naming. The Judgement plan's D1 was superseded on 2026-08-01 when its dials went live ahead of BL2. That is allowed to happen here too — but it is BL2's decision to make, recorded there, not a milestone of this plan quietly taking it.

D3 — A capture goal is claimed by derivation, never by stored state

A property is not this unit's goal when capture_claim_depth or more other capture-capable friendlies are strictly closer to it. That is computed from the board at the moment the goal is asked for — pure, integer, RNG-free, and a function of nothing but the current state. The nearest unit still takes the nearest property; the second one is pushed to the next.

The rejected alternative is a claims registry that survives the turn. AIPlanningContext.begin clears goals on every command by design, and the threat map is the one fact deliberately allowed to persist — it earns that by being expensive and by carrying an invalidation signature. A second cross-decision cache is exactly the cross-turn state the AI Judgement plan's D5 rejected a formation manager for, and the reasoning is unchanged: the planner has nowhere to keep it, and anything it did keep would have to be invalidated against a board that moves after every single command.

Ties break by scan order, which is the repo's standing determinism rule — a claim that swapped between two equidistant units on successive replans would leave both walking and neither arriving. R6 is the pin.

D4 — A property is priced by what it produces, in the currency captures are already in

capture_score stays the unit of account. A production property multiplies it by production_capture_multiplier, asked of TerrainType.builds being non-empty — never a terrain id checked in three places, which is the same rule that already governs BuildCommand, the build menu and the AI's own facility scan. An HQ keeps hq_capture_multiplier, asked as it is today.

One judgement, two readings. The same multiplier feeds the goal-side detour through capture_goal_value_tiles, so “worth taking” and “worth walking to” cannot disagree — the shape AI Judgement's D3 established when it priced denial off the capture arithmetic read backwards. A unit that walks to a factory because it is valuable must then score it as valuable when it arrives, or it turns around.

The two are in different units — value and tiles — because the two paths are. _consider_captures competes in value against _attack_score; _advance_value counts in tiles. That is the same split threat_aversion and advance_threat_tiles already live on, and for the same reason.

D5 — The capture roster scales with the unowned board, floored at today's number

capture_unit_target becomes the floor rather than the answer, and stays at 3 in every tier, so an install with the new rate at zero is byte-identical. The target is max(capture_unit_target, ceil(capture_units_per_property × unowned)), where unowned counts property cells this team's side does not hold — through GameState.allied, so an ally's ground is not a target, exactly as _advance_goal already reads it.

It counts what is left to take, not the size of the board, and that is what makes it self-damping. The target falls as the map is taken: an army that has captured everything wants no more infantry and the priority list resumes on its own. A flat count scaled to board area would keep buying infantry after the race was over, which is R1 with no brake on it.

D6 — Banking becomes board-dependent, and it happens without touching the banking rule

The opening hoard looks like a banking bug and is not one. _worth_waiting_for already refuses to bank whenever something urgent is wanted (best_rank < wants.first_priority_rank()), and infantry are urgent whenever the roster is short. D5 makes the roster short on day 1 of a property-rich board, and the existing guard then fires by itself — no new branch, no new dial, and no second opinion about when to spend.

The consequence is the point of this decision, not a side effect: the same code then banks on a small board and spends on a big one. A property count is the board asking whether there is a race on, and routing the answer through the roster target means the AI reads it without anything in ai/ having an opinion about map size. Twenty neutral properties makes infantry urgent and the Md Tank waits; four makes the target unreachable-by-choice, the tier stays empty, and the Md Tank is bought exactly as it is today.

The other reason to bank — a decisive unit bought because a knockout is available this turn — is a judgement about the fight rather than the board, and the planner has no way to form it. It is not built here. R2 carries what its absence costs once D5 is live.

save_up_turns is explicitly left alone. Its own comment records the measurement behind it — first-side bias of +5.6 pp at no banking, +14.9 at two turns, +20.2 at three — and banking is what lets the AI reach the expensive half of the roster at all. Re-tuning it to treat a symptom would trade first-move fairness for something D5 gets for free.

If AE4 measures idle factory turns surviving AE1–AE3, that is a new observation and earns its own milestone. It does not earn one in advance. Section 6 carries it as the one named conditional follow-up.

D7 — Nothing under core/ is touched, and no new telemetry

Every read this plan needs goes through an authority that already exists: MapData.property_cells, GameState.properties_of, GameState.allied, GameState.home_hq, TerrainType.builds, UnitPricing.cost_for. No rule changes, no signal is added, and the balance simulator gains no hook — the balance plan's D2 holds: the telemetry observes, it never instruments the sim.

The evidence this plan is built on required no instrumentation either. The recorder observes at the two brokers that already existed, and the analyser asks the rules rather than the planner — which is why a finding about day 4 of one match is still readable after the planner has moved.

04

Milestones

Four, each independently mergeable, each ending with make verify green and both balance reports byte-identical. Four new @exports in total, and one existing one re-read as a floor.

AE1 — Buy enough infantry to race shipped

Answers observation 1 — and, by D6, the opening hoard along with it. One dial: capture_units_per_property, default 0.0. First, because it is the cheapest change and the one the evidence points at hardest.

  • The dial on AIProfile, written into all three tiers at zero.
  • The target computed in AIProductionPlanner.plan and passed to BuildWants.from_facts, which already takes it as a parameter — the value object's factory deliberately accepts no callback, and this change keeps it that way.
  • The unowned count from context.state through D5's reading. AIPlanningContext gains nothing: this is one scan in the production planner, not a per-decision fact the unit planner shares.

Tests — a fixture on a property-rich board where the starting roster of three leaves the target unmet, proving the day-1 build is infantry and not a bank; the same fixture at 0.0 proving it still banks; and a fixture proving the target falls as properties are captured, which is D5's self-damping claim on a board.

And the mirror of that, which is the milestone's real bar — a property-poor board at the same non-zero rate, proving the AI still banks and still fields its Md Tank. D6's board-dependence is a claim about two boards and only one of them is the bug; a rate that buys the property race by deleting the expensive half of the roster everywhere has failed. R2 is what that fixture is guarding.

Exit — both reports byte-identical; the new tests fail before the change and pass after.

AE2 — Spread the capture goals shipped

Answers observation 2. One dial: capture_claim_depth, default 0 (off). 1 is classic claiming — the nearest unit takes it; higher values let that many units converge on one contested property.

  • The claim filter inside the capture clause of _advance_goal (:468–478), per D3 — derived, never stored, ties by scan order.
  • At depth 0 the filter is not built and the clause is the shipped _nearest call, unchanged.
  • Explicitly not a new fill, a new path or a new geometry: it filters the candidate list the existing walk already collects, and MovementResolver is untouched.

Tests — three infantry and three properties in a line: at depth 1 each takes a different one, at 0 all three converge as today. A determinism fixture with two exactly-equidistant units, asserting the same assignment across repeated planning. And test_replay_fidelity.gd unchanged and green, which is the standing pin on scan order.

Exit — both reports byte-identical; the convergence fixture reverses at depth 1.

AE3 — Price what a property produces proposed

Answers observation 3. Two dials, one judgement per D4: production_capture_multiplier (default 1.0, the action score) and capture_goal_value_tiles (default 0.0, the goal-side detour).

  • The multiplier applied in _consider_captures (:206–211) beside the HQ multiplier, on the same line of arithmetic, so the two compose rather than compete — a captured enemy headquarters is already a production property and should read as both.
  • The goal-side reading converts the same multiplier into tiles of allowed detour inside the capture clause, composing with AE2's claim filter rather than replacing it.
  • Both ask TerrainType.builds, once, in one helper. No terrain id appears anywhere in ai/ as a result of this milestone.

Tests — a factory and a city equidistant from one infantry: it takes the city at the inert values and the factory above them. A factory one tile further than a city, proving the detour dial is what moves it and that the two readings agree. And the inert pin at 1.0/0.0.

Exit — both reports byte-identical; the factory fixture reverses.

AE4 — Characterise the dials, hand BL2 the bands proposed

Adds no code, by design — the AI Judgement plan's AJ4 is the precedent and the reason: a measurement belongs on the milestone that changes nothing, or every review round invalidates the number taken before it.

  • At 15 seeds minimum, not 16 matches. §4b of docs/difficulty_check.md was measured at n=16 and §4c re-measured it at 15 seeds and found the direction consistently opposite on the largest effect it had reported. Treat anything smaller as a hint and label it one.
  • The three axes probed together, not alone: AE1's rate against AE2's depth is the pairing that matters, since more infantry with no spreading is a bigger clump and spreading with no infantry is a thinner line. §4c's cohesion result is the standing warning — its two axes had to be probed together or the conclusion inverted.
  • Reported as a band per dial, plus the property-race counters the balance report already carries (blue_props / red_props in matches.csv), which is the outcome this plan is actually aiming at — the win rate is downstream of it.
  • The turn-time table re-measured. §4's is stale by its own admission: it predates the Judgement dials going live, and Normal now runs the cohesion term on every advance, so no current reading of the shipped tiers exists.
  • Two negative results re-checked against the new board state, because both were argued to have failed for want of the conditions this plan creates: focus_fire_bonus (refuted three times) and withdraw_weight (negative at every value, and blamed on the refuge's price).

Exit — a new section in docs/difficulty_check.md carrying the bands, the n, and the explicit statement of what was not measured. No tier value changes.

05

Risk register

Ordered by what they would cost if ignored. Every mitigation names where it lands.

R1 — The infantry tide high

The capture roster is an urgent tier: it outranks the entire priority list until it is met. Set the rate too high on Heartland (48 cities) and the AI buys infantry and nothing else while a Md Tank walks through them. This is the single most likely way to make the AI worse.

Mitigation. D5's self-damping — the target counts what is left to take, so it falls as the map fills and releases the priority list on its own. The floor stays at today's 3. AE4 probes the rate from below and reports the value at which the roster stops answering the enemy's, using the balance report's existing per-side unit counts.

R2 — The finisher is never bought high

R1's failure mode read from the other end, and the more insidious of the two. The capture roster is urgent, so while it is unmet nothing else can be bought at all — and D5 makes it unmet for longer on exactly the big, property-rich boards where the match runs long enough for a Md Tank to matter. A rate set to win the property race can quietly delete the expensive half of the roster from every board worth playing, and the symptom is the opposite of the one this plan started from: an AI with plenty of ground, plenty of income, and nothing that can break a defended line.

The exception the planner cannot see makes it worse. Banking three turns for a decisive unit is the right call when there is a knockout available — an enemy army it can break, or a headquarters within reach of the thing it is saving for. Nothing in ai/ can form that judgement: production reads the roster and the board, never the state of the fight, and hq_capture_multiplier prices taking a headquarters only once a unit is already standing beside it.

Mitigation. AE1's property-poor fixture is the direct guard, and AE4 reports Md Tanks built per match per board size as a first-class result rather than a footnote — if the rate that wins the property race is the rate that ends heavy production, that is the plan failing and it should be visible in the same table. The knockout capability itself is §6's second named follow-up, deliberately deferred: it is a model of the fight's future, which is the AI Judgement plan's territory rather than this one's, and building it speculatively before AE4 says whether R2 bites is scope creep with no measurement behind it.

R3 — Spreading is thinning high

AE2 sends capture units to ground they may not be able to hold, one at a time, into fire. This is AI Judgement's R1 from the opposite side — that plan's cohesion term exists to stop the army arriving piecemeal, and this one deliberately scatters part of it.

The two do not collide by accident today, and it is worth knowing why: AJ3's D5 exempts every errand from the cohesion penalty, capture goals included, because the column's pull would otherwise cancel the errand outright. So spreading is untaxed by construction — which means nothing currently limits it either.

Mitigation. capture_claim_depth is an integer with a meaning, not a switch: depth 2 lets pairs travel. AE4 probes depth against cohesion_tiles as a pair. And R3 inherits the honest limit AJ4 recorded on R1 — both sides in an AI-vs-AI sweep are the same planner, so the instrument is structurally blind to whether a thin line is punished. This one needs a human playtest, and saying so is not a deferral of the risk but its accurate location.

R4 — Three plans, one planner, one fortnight high

The doctrine hooks shipped 2026-07-31, the Judgement dials went live 2026-08-01, and this plan opens 2026-08-02. Every band in docs/difficulty_check.md was measured on a planner that has since moved, and AJ3 has already recorded what that costs: a measurement taken inside a milestone whose code was still changing had to be moved out of it entirely.

Mitigation. AE4 adds no code, which is the whole point of it being a milestone. Nothing measured before AE3 merges is quoted as current. And the byte-diff bar at every step means the planner this plan measures is the planner it shipped.

R5 — The ladder does not close medium

These capabilities help every tier that switches them on. If BL2 gives them to Normal as well — and §4c's reasoning says it should, because withholding basic competence to protect a spread is a worse game — Difficult's margin over Normal may not move at all. Four tier splits have already been tried and all four failed.

Mitigation. This plan does not promise the gate and D2 keeps it out of the tier files. What it promises is a better opponent at whatever tier is switched on, which is measurable on its own terms through the property-race counters rather than the ladder. If the gate stays failed, it stays failed knowingly, which is the state §4c already put it in.

R6 — Determinism and scan order medium

AE2's claim is the first thing in the planner whose answer for one unit depends on where the other units are standing. A tie broken inconsistently makes two units swap goals on successive replans and neither arrives; a tie broken by anything but scan order breaks same-seed reproducibility, which is the property saves, replays and the whole balance lab rest on.

Mitigation. Strict comparison and scan-order ties, the rule the planners already keep. test_replay_fidelity.gd — a seeded headless match, recorded and re-issued, reproducing every checkpoint — is the merge bar, and an oscillation fixture in AE2 is the direct pin. The replay analyser's own oscillation detector is the field check: if AE2 makes units walk in circles, a recorded match will say so by name.

R7 — Cost medium

AE2's derivation is O(capture units × properties) per goal, per command. On Heartland with a dozen infantry and 48 properties that is real work, on the path the planner runs most often. AJ4 already measured a live threat-map dial roughly doubling Normal's per-turn planning time (33 → 63 ms), so this planner is not fast.

Mitigation. The candidate list is already collected by the shipped clause; the filter reuses it rather than re-scanning the map. At depth 0 nothing is built at all. AE4 re-measures the turn clock on the shipped tiers, which is overdue independently of this plan.

R8 — Tier inheritance low

A profile .tres that omits a new @export inherits the class default, so a class default and data/ai/default.tres that disagree make an install with no profile file play a different game.

Mitigation. Every dial written into all three tier files at its inert value in the same commit that adds it, and test_capability_defaults_plan_exactly_like_the_shipped_profile as the pin — the same discipline AJ followed when its class defaults moved with the shipped profile.

06

Deliberately out of scope

Scope creep is this repo's named top risk, and this plan touches the file three other plans have edited in the last fortnight.

Not built here

  • Any change to save_up_turns or the banking branch. D6 — the roster floor reaches it, and the dial carries a measurement that would be traded away.
  • Any tier value. D2, BL2's.
  • Transports and ferries. The planner cannot plan a load-move-unload across turns; that is naval R1 and it stays open. It is also why the ferry-only prizes on Atoll and Confluence are ground the AI is expected to leave alone — a property it cannot reach is not a goal this plan can give it.
  • An income model. INCOME_PER_PROPERTY is flat, so a city is a city; the only property distinction worth pricing is what it builds, which is D4.
  • A defensive reading of any of this — denying an enemy a factory the way AJ1 denies a capture. AJ1's R3 is the reason: three dials already price one threat map, and a fourth reading of the same board is how a plan counts the same doctrine twice.
  • New telemetry, and any board edit. D7.

Two conditional follow-ups, both about banking

1 · A banking guard that sees an idle factory. Earned only if AE4 measures idle factory turns surviving AE1–AE3 — that is, on a board where the roster target is already met and the front is contested anyway. If it is earned, the shape is a floor in _worth_waiting_for, not a new heuristic and not a retune of save_up_turns. Written down rather than built because the evidence points at the roster, and one observation should buy one fix.

2 · Banking because there is a knockout available. The exception that argues for hoarding even on a property-rich board: save for the decisive unit when the fight is nearly won and one more Md Tank ends it. This is the honest counterweight to D6 and it is not a production change — production would only be reading an answer someone else computed. Forming it means modelling what the board looks like two turns out, which is squarely the AI Judgement plan's territory (“what the enemy will do next turn”), and that plan closed with its own withdrawal dial still measuring negative. Building a knockout detector on top of that, speculatively, before AE4 says whether R2 bites, would be the second unmeasured model of the future in a planner that has not yet paid for the first.

If AE1 closes the hoard and R2 does not bite, neither of these is ever needed and the plan will say so.

Where this plan is recorded

On merge of AE1 this plan joins the “Designs of record” index in CLAUDE.md, between the AI Judgement plan and the balance retune, with its decisions summarised and every supersession recorded there rather than edited into this file. .lavish/ is gitignored — the plan needs git add -f or it stays untracked.

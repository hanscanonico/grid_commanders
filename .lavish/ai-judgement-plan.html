Grid Commanders · design of record · draft 1 · 2026-07-31

AI Judgement

Three things a player notices inside the first hour: the computer ignores the infantry about to take its headquarters, it throws units into fights it cannot survive, and it arrives one unit at a time. They look like three bugs. They are one shape — the planner scores each unit alone, one command at a time, with no model of what happens next. This plan gives it the three things a .tres number cannot: sight of what the enemy is achieving, a price on its own survival, and a reason to travel together.

01

What you can see at the table

Three observations from play, each with its cause in code. None of them needs new telemetry to see; all three are readable in one 352-line file.

“When the HQ is attacked the AI does not really care and still attacks other units even if it is going to lose because of this.”

Cause 1. The planner prices a target by what it is, never by what it is doing.

  • _attack_score (ai_unit_action_planner.gd:92) is target cost × damage fraction, kill-boosted, minus the discounted counter. Nothing else.
  • state.capture_progress is read in exactly one place (:174) — the AI's own captures. Progress standing on the AI's ground is never looked at.
  • profile.hq_capture_multiplier (ai_profile.gd:30) exists only for the offence. There is no defensive counterpart anywhere in ai/.
  • The insult on top: HQ terrain carries the board's best defence stars, so the forecast damage against a unit standing there is low — which makes the infantry about to win the match score worse than the same infantry in a field. The planner is actively steered away from the one unit that matters.

Losing the home HQ is elimination (four-players D3). No score the planner computes can see it coming.

“The AI seems a little bit suicidal — it attacks relentlessly but sometimes it makes no sense. It should think: this Md Tank can attack me next turn, better to step back because it will one-shot me.”

Cause 2. Two halves — a dial at zero, and a structural gap no dial reaches.

  • The dial. data/ai/default.tres ships threat_aversion 0.0 and advance_threat_tiles 0.0. At zero the ThreatMap is never even built (:72). Easy runs 0.3 / 3.0 and Difficult 0.1 / 2.0 — the tier most people play is the only one with none. The only risk Normal prices is the target's own counter-attack, discounted to 0.6.
  • The structure. Retreat lives only in the _advance_command fallback (:39–42), reached only when nothing clears min_useful_score. So retreat_hp = 45 does not mean “at 45 HP pull back” — it means “at 45 HP, if you had nothing to do anyway, walk toward a repair property.” A 15 HP tank with any half-decent shot takes it and dies.
  • And the map cannot tell one-shot from chipped. incoming_damage (threat_map.gd:77) sums expected damage and caps it at the unit's HP, so “an Md Tank deletes me” and “three units chip me for the same total” price identically. On the advance path the penalty is denominated in tiles while the unit's worth is in funds, so a 20,000 bomber flinches exactly as hard as a 1,000 infantry.

“The AI never groups units, it attacks one by one.”

Cause 3. One capability disabled on purpose; the other has never existed.

  • Targeting. _focus_bonus (:119) is built and ships at 0.0 in all three tiers. The ladder is why (difficulty_check.md:345): 0.2 / 0.5 / 1.0 → 43.3 / 41.7 / 43.3 %, all under the 50 % baseline. Its stated reason: re-planning after every command already exposes a wounded target's finishing shot for free, and biasing the first shot drags it off its own best trade. So the pile-on you occasionally see is incidental — it happens only when a second attacker was already in range. Nothing ever moves a unit into range because a target was softened.
  • Approach. There is no cohesion code at all. _advance_goal (:285) computes each unit's goal in complete isolation — nearest visible enemy, or nearest capturable property — and _advance_command minimises distance to it. Nothing in the file ever reads where the other friendlies are.
  • Consequences: a tank (6 move), an artillery and a mech (2 move) get the same goal and travel at their own speeds, so the tank arrives three turns early, alone, and dies. Nothing waits — no staging cell, no line. And _nearest gives each unit its own closest enemy, so flanks chase different targets and the army fans out instead of concentrating.
  • plan_next_command (ai_controller.gd:33) is greedy per command: best single action across all ready units, apply, re-plan from scratch. There is no joint plan for a turn anywhere in the architecture. “Attacks one by one” is literally what it does.

The one sentence

The planner has no model of the board's future: not what the enemy is achieving (the HQ), not what the enemy will do next turn (the Md Tank), and not what its own other units are doing (the trickle). Three symptoms, one absence.

02

The boundary with the balance retune — read this before writing code

.lavish/balance-retune-plan.html already owns half of observation 2, and it owns it well. Its atlas — 20,280 matches, 305 core-hours — found the same defect from the other end and named it: “Normal is the only tier with zero self-preservation.” Its BL2 is the ladder repair. This plan must not be a second design of record for that work.

What BL2's dials can buy

  • Normal flinching from a kill zone at all — threat_aversion 0.0 → 0.05 / 0.1, inside the band the ladder measured as a real gain.
  • Normal giving up ground to dodge a lethal shot while walking — advance_threat_tiles 0.0 → 1.6 / 2.0.
  • Sharper finishing and trade weights — kill_bonus, counter_weight.
  • An earlier walk home for the wounded — retreat_hp.

That is a real fix for the character of observation 2, and the atlas says it is worth a great deal: Normal beats Easy only 15.6 % over a long horizon today.

What no number in data/ai/ can buy

  • Defence of ground. There is no term to weight. Observation 1 is untouched by every dial that exists.
  • Retreat outbidding a shot. retreat_hp only steers where an idle unit walks; it can never reach a unit that has an attack available, because that unit never reaches the fallback.
  • Arriving together. There is no cohesion term for a weight to scale. Observation 3 is untouched.
  • Value-denominated caution. advance_threat_tiles is in tiles by construction; “this shot costs me 20,000 funds” cannot be said in that currency.

The interleave — and it is available right now

The balance retune is Draft 1 with zero shipped milestones: BL1 has not run. Its central structural claim is that every commander number is measured on the Normal planner, so BL2 rights the ladder and then freezes Normal for BL3–BL5. If this plan's capabilities land after that freeze, they unfreeze Normal and every roster number BL3–BL5 measured is stale the moment they land — the exact failure the retune's “wrong order” panel warns about, arriving from a direction it did not anticipate.

Landing them before BL2 costs nothing, because every capability here ships at 0.0 (D1). Both committed reports stay byte-identical, Normal does not move, BL1's baseline stays valid — and BL2 simply arrives to a wider set of dials than it expected, with recommended bands already measured for them.

The honest cost, stated plainly

The repo's working rule is “ship something playable each milestone”. AJ1–AJ4 are not playable. An inert capability changes nothing a player can feel; the moment the computer starts defending its headquarters is BL2, not AJ1. That is a deliberate trade and it buys one thing: not invalidating a 305 core-hour measurement and the entire roster tune that stands on it. If that trade is unacceptable, the alternative is discussed and rejected in D2 — read it before overriding this.

03

Decisions

Locked unless a later plan supersedes them in writing.

D1 — Every capability ships at 0.0, and 0.0 skips the code

Exactly the pattern the difficulty plan established for S1–S3: at zero, the code that reads the weight never runs. So the shipped AI is byte-identical to today's, on the same RNG stream, and test_capability_defaults_plan_exactly_like_the_shipped_profile (tests/unit/test_ai_smarts.gd:480) keeps passing unchanged. That test is this plan's merge bar, not its casualty — BL2 is where it moves, as the retune's own D2 already records.

Merge bar for AJ1–AJ3: a fixed-seed byte-diff of make commander-balance and make difficulty-check on both sides of every pull request, both reports identical, no exceptions. This plan claims no accepted departure — unlike four-players FP3, which had a structural one. If a report moves, a default is not actually zero.

D2 — This plan owns capabilities; BL2 owns Normal's numbers

No AJ milestone edits a shipped value in data/ai/*.tres. New fields are written into all three tiers at zero (R7), and that is the only edit those files take. AJ4's deliverable is a recommended band per dial, in the §6 probe-table shape docs/difficulty_check.md already uses — so BL2 reads it the way it already reads the threat_aversion table.

The rejected alternative, named: lighting up hard.tres early, so Difficult gets the behaviour immediately while Normal stays frozen. It is tempting — the roster tune is measured on Normal, so Difficult looks free. It is not: BL2's exit criterion is Difficult-over-Normal ≥ 70 %, so moving Difficult moves the gate BL2 is trying to hit, and BL2 would be tuning one rung against a moving other. One tier at a time, in BL2. If the reviewer wants the behaviour in the game sooner, the right lever is to run BL1+BL2 sooner, not to split the ladder.

D3 — Denial is priced at the price of capture, read backwards

One price list, both directions. _consider_attacks gains a bonus for shooting a capture-capable enemy standing on ground our side owns, built from the same three numbers _consider_captures already uses — capture_score, capture_progress_bonus, hq_capture_multiplier — scaled by one new dial, defend_weight. At 1.0, denying a capture is worth exactly what making one is worth.

That symmetry is not a coincidence to be tuned away later: they are the same event seen from two sides, and _best_unit_plan already compares capture scores against attack scores directly, so the two are in the same currency by construction. A separate defensive price list would be a second opinion about what a property is worth.

Three details that are decisions, not implementation:

  • Only capture-capable enemies count. A tank parked on our city takes nothing from us; the danger it poses is the threat map's job, and paying twice for it is R3.
  • The ground is our side's, asked of GameState.allied — an ally's city is the side's city (four-players D2), and it is never re-derived as owner == unit.team.
  • The HQ multiplier applies only to a home HQ, asked of GameState.home_hq (four-players D3, open-seats D3) and never terrain_at(cell).id == &"hq". A survivor standing on an HQ they conquered cannot be beheaded through it, so defending it is worth a city and no more — and a vacant seat's HQ has nobody to fell.

D4 — Withdrawal is a scored candidate, not a fallback — and it is denominated in value

_consider_withdraw joins _consider_attacks, _consider_captures and _consider_dive inside _best_unit_plan, competing on one score. _consider_dive is the precedent and the proof it fits: a non-attack action that already outbids a shot when the board says it should.

Its score is withdraw_weight × unit.type.cost × damage_avoided / 100 — value, the same currency _attack_score is in, which is precisely what advance_threat_tiles could not be. This is the whole of the “that Md Tank one-shots me, step back” case: an Md Tank facing certain death puts its own 16,000 on the table against a marginal trade and wins the argument. It is also what finally makes a wounded unit's survival competitive with its last shot.

It reads the same ThreatMap the other two dials read, the same way — incoming_damage at a candidate cell. No second opinion about danger, and no new geometry: the destination is chosen from the MovementResolver.reachable the planner already built.

D5 — Cohesion is a term on the advance path, not a formation manager

No groups, no leaders, no squad objects, no roles. _advance_value gains one term: −cohesion_tiles × max(0, distance_to_nearest_friendly − cohesion_radius), in tiles, which is what that function is already denominated in. A unit inside the radius pays nothing; beyond it, it is pulled back toward its army.

The “wait for the slow ones” behaviour is emergent and deliberately not coded: the goal term still pulls forward, so the equilibrium is a clump that advances at the speed of its rear. There is no waiting state to enter, leave, or get stuck in.

The rejected alternative: an explicit formation manager with assigned leaders and shared objectives. It needs cross-turn state the planner has nowhere to keep — AIPlanningContext is rebuilt every command and only the threat map deliberately survives — and it would be a second opinion about where a unit should stand, competing with the goal it already has.

D6 — Nothing under core/ is touched, and no new telemetry

Every read goes through an authority that already exists: AttackRange (who may shoot what, how far, with which weapon), MovementResolver (reach and budget), CombatResolver.forecast_at (damage — luck-free, draws no RNG, and a pure read that never stands a unit somewhere to ask), Vision.is_hidden_from (via the existing visible_enemies scan, so the AI's sight rule is unchanged), GameState.allied (hostility), GameState.home_hq (the elimination stake) and GameState.capture_progress (already read from the other side).

Same rule as the balance plan's D2 and the doctrine plan's D4: the planner observes, it never instruments. Every new term is pure, RNG-free, and iterates collections AIPlanningContext already snapshots in scan order — so determinism, replay and the save format are all untouched.

04

Milestones

Four, each independently mergeable, each ending with make verify green and both committed reports byte-identical. Four new @exports in total.

AJ1 — Defend what is being taken shipped

Answers observation 1. One dial: defend_weight, default 0.0.

  • defend_weight on AIProfile, written into all three tiers at zero.
  • _defend_bonus(state, unit, enemy) in the unit planner, added inside _consider_attacks beside _focus_bonus — the same seam, so the destination penalty and the forecast are computed exactly once as they are today.
  • Built from D3's mirrored price list, with the three qualifications D3 locks.
  • The advance path: only the home HQ diverts. If a capture-capable hostile stands on a home HQ belonging to our side, that cell becomes the advance goal — placed ahead of the capture and nearest-enemy clauses in _advance_goal. Ordinary cities are defended opportunistically, on the attack path only, because a city is a setback and the headquarters is the match. R2 is why this line is drawn here rather than wider.

Tests — an AI turn where the only sane move is shooting the capturer off the home HQ; a second where a juicier kill elsewhere must lose the argument; and the inert-at-zero pin, which is the same fixture with the dial off.

Exit — both reports byte-identical; new tests fail before the change and pass after.

AJ2 — Self-preservation that can outbid a shot shipped

Answers the structural half of observation 2 — the half BL2's dials cannot reach. One dial: withdraw_weight, default 0.0.

  • _consider_withdraw in _best_unit_plan, per D4. Builds the threat map on its own when > 0, exactly as threat_aversion does.
  • The destination is the reachable cell of least incoming_damage, tie-broken by step cost and then scan order. No new pathing, no new geometry, no new fill.
  • A withdrawal that also lands on a property servicing the unit's domain scores higher — which is what finally makes retreat_hp mean what its own comment says it means, instead of only steering an idle walk.

Tests — the user's case as a fixture: a wounded Md Tank inside a lethal threat ring with a marginal attack available. It must step back at withdraw_weight > 0 and must still take the shot at 0.0. Plus a fixture proving a healthy unit does not flinch from a survivable shot, which is the R3 boundary.

Exit — both reports byte-identical; the “attacks and dies” fixture reverses. The sweep's wall clock moves to AJ4 (R6): at withdraw_weight 0.0 the threat map is never built on this path, so a timing taken here would measure a code path that does not execute, and the measurement belongs where the dials are exercised live.

AJ3 — Fight as an army shipped

Answers observation 3. Two dials — cohesion_tiles (default 0.0) and cohesion_radius (default 2) — and the goal exemptions that keep them off every errand.

  • One term in _advance_value, per D5, over context.friendly_units — already built, already scan-ordered.
  • The goal exemptions, which are the other half of the milestone: AdvanceGoal.keeps_formation defaults to false and only the ordinary advance on the enemy turns it on, so every errand _advance_goal computes first — refit, repair, a property to capture, and AJ1's besieged home HQ — goes untaxed by construction rather than by a list of exceptions. Both terms count in tiles and the column's pull is the stronger, so charging an errand cancels it outright.

Tests — cohesion is pure arithmetic over a unit list, so a fixture with one fast and one slow unit pins that the fast one holds short of the front at cohesion_tiles > 0 and runs ahead at zero, and a six-day fixture pins that the column advances rather than stalls (R1). One fixture per exempt errand pins that it still reaches its objective with the dial live.

Exit — both reports byte-identical. The focus_fire_bonus re-test with cohesion live was attempted here and moves to AJ4: every measurement taken agreed on the sign, but each review round changed the planner it had been measured on, so no number stayed current inside a milestone whose code was still moving.

AJ4 — Characterise the dials, hand BL2 the bands

No code. The deliverable is a table.

  • One axis per probe, COM-120 style, on the focused presets the docs already define — make difficulty-check on boards that resolve, and make commander-balance BAL="--commanders=… --seeds=…". Never the atlas: it cost 305 core-hours and is a verdict instrument, not an iteration loop.
  • Because every shipped tier is at zero, each probe runs against a hand-built profile rather than a committed one — so there are no rejected values to restore, because nothing was ever written to data/.
  • The one deliberately two-dimensional probe is (cohesion_tiles, cohesion_radius), for the reason R1 gives.
  • Deliverable: a §6-shaped probe table per dial — the band that helps, the cliff that hurts — plus the R3 cross-term note, committed to docs/difficulty_check.md.
  • And the sweep's wall clock, deferred here from AJ2 (R6): recorded with the dials that build the threat map live against the same sweep with them at zero, which is the only comparison in which the cost is real.
  • And the focus_fire_bonus re-test with cohesion live, deferred here from AJ3. The standing negative result was measured on a planner whose units arrive one at a time — focus fire had nothing to focus. If it measures positive now, it is a recommended band for BL2; if negative again, it stays at zero and the plan records the second refutation, which stops the next person re-deriving it. Either outcome is a deliverable.
  • Why both measurements landed here rather than in the milestones that named them: a measurement belongs on frozen code. AJ2's wall clock had no live dial to time and AJ3's focus-fire table went stale at every review round that touched the planner — AJ4 is the milestone that adds none, so it is the only one whose numbers stay true after it ships.
  • Explicitly not a deliverable: a shipped tier value. That is BL2 (D2).

Exit — the table is committed and all three data/ai/*.tres are untouched but for the four new fields at their defaults.

05

Risk register

Ordered by how much they would cost if ignored. Every mitigation is either a design line already drawn above or a probe AJ4 is required to run.

R1 — The clump is artillery bait, and Command Power bait high

Cohesion concentrates the army, and concentration is exactly what an artillery shot and every area Command Power are priced against. A tighter army that dies to one power is worse than a scattered one.

Mitigation: cohesion_radius is a dial rather than a constant, and AJ4 measures the pair together rather than one at a time — the plan's only deliberately two-dimensional probe. If the band is empty, cohesion ships at zero and AJ3's value is the refutation — the focus-fire re-measurement it was first paired with is AJ4's own deliverable now.

R2 — The army marches home high

A defence goal any unit may adopt empties the front the moment one infantry steps on a city. The failure mode is a computer that shuttles its whole army between two properties while the player takes the rest of the board.

Mitigation is the design, not a dial: only the home HQ diverts the advance path, and ordinary properties are defended only by units that already had a shot available anyway. If even that empties the front, the diversion is narrowed by reachability — never widened to more property kinds.

R3 — Three dials, one fear, counted three times high

threat_aversion (attacks, in value), advance_threat_tiles (advances, in tiles) and the new withdraw_weight (its own candidate, in value) all read one ThreatMap. Turned on together they can price the same Md Tank three times and reproduce the R2 coward from a new direction — the 0.5 row that lost 88 % of its games. This is the single most likely way this plan makes the AI worse.

Mitigation: AJ4 probes withdraw_weight twice — with the other two at zero, and at the values BL2 is likely to ship — and reports both. The probe table states in writing that these three are one axis with three heads, so BL2 never tunes one of them alone.

R4 — The ladder measures the whole thing negative medium

Entirely possible. Caution has already measured negative once past its band, and focus fire measured negative in three shapes. A plan that adds judgement is not entitled to assume judgement wins.

Mitigation is the plan's own posture: every capability ships at zero, so a refuted dial costs nothing to leave in place, and zeroing a misbehaving smart is the established remedy — focus_fire_bonus is the precedent, kept rather than deleted precisely so the ladder can re-test it in one edit. A plan that ends with four zeros and four probe tables has still bought something real: the next person does not spend the compute again.

R5 — Determinism and scan order medium

Every new term iterates a collection already snapshotted in scan order and every comparison stays strict (>), so ties still break by scan order. The genuinely new surface is that _consider_withdraw puts a new kind of command into the competition, so a tie can newly land between two different command types — decided by the fixed order of the _consider_* calls in _best_unit_plan. That order is part of the contract and gets a test.

R6 — Cost medium

The threat map is one flood fill per visible enemy. withdraw_weight > 0 makes it build on turns where nothing attacks — which is exactly when it is cheapest to skip today. On the largest boards (Confluence, 32×24) that is not free.

Mitigation: the existing per-turn cache is keyed on the day and the enemy set and already survives across commands, so the cost is once per turn rather than once per decision. AJ4 records the sweep's wall clock on both sides of the change — not AJ2, whose dials ship at zero and so never build the map at all.

R7 — Tier inheritance low

A profile that omits a new @export inherits the class default, so adding one field silently retunes exactly the tiers that left it out.

Mitigation: write all four fields into all three .tres files explicitly, held by tests/unit/test_ai_profile.gd, which exists for this and already holds every shipped tier to it.

06

Deliberately out of scope

Scope creep is this repo's named top risk, and “make the AI smarter” is the most open-ended brief on the board. These are the things this plan is not.

Not in scopeWhy
Turn-level search plan_next_command stays greedy and one command at a time. Joint optimisation over a whole turn is a different program with a different cost, and every observation here is answerable without it.
Multi-turn lookahead No horizon beyond the one turn the threat map already models. “What will they do in three turns” is not a question this architecture asks.
Transports and ferrying Naval R1 stands: the planner still cannot plan a load–move–unload across turns, and nothing here changes it. Ferry-only ground on Atoll and Confluence stays deliberately unclaimed.
Any combat number CombatResolver and the damage chart are not opened. These are judgement weights; they change how the planner ranks its own candidates and nothing else (difficulty D2).
Per-tier capability gating Every dial exists at every tier; only its value differs. A tier may retune a strength, never gate a behaviour.
The AI's sight rule Unchanged — omniscient except doctrine-hidden, through Vision.is_hidden_from. Widening it needs its own plan decision, and defending a headquarters does not require one.
Commander doctrine advice The three CommanderType hooks stay as the doctrine plan shipped them. A doctrine that wants to defend harder is a follow-up, not this plan — and it would double-count against defend_weight if written carelessly (doctrine D4).
Shipping a tier value BL2. See D2 — and note the alternative was considered and rejected there, not overlooked.

Where this plan is recorded

On merge of AJ1, this plan joins the “Designs of record” index in CLAUDE.md with its scope and its surviving invariants, and .lavish/balance-retune-plan.html gains a one-line pointer at BL2 noting that the dial set it tunes was widened here. .lavish/ is gitignored, so this file needs git add -f or it stays untracked.

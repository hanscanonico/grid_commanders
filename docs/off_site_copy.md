## itch.io page description

> **Grid Commanders** is a turn-based tactics game on a square grid — free, in your browser, no
> download and no account.
>
> Every unit spends a movement budget the terrain charges it for: roads are cheap, woods are slow,
> a mountain takes foot soldiers only, and whatever you end your turn standing on decides how much
> of an incoming shot you soak. The roster runs across land, air and sea in a rock-paper-scissors
> shape, so nothing wins on its own — anti-air eats helicopters, fighters eat anything that flies,
> a submarine hides until a cruiser hunts it down.
>
> Money comes from the map. Infantry walk into towns, factories and airports and take a few turns
> to raise the flag; everything you hold pays you every morning, and that income buys the next
> tank. Commanders sit over all of it, each bending the rules a little and charging a Command Power
> that fires once.
>
> - **Campaign** — authored missions with objectives, briefings and a story between them
> - **Skirmish** — any board, four difficulty tiers, fog of war optional
> - **Up to four armies** — free-for-all, 2v2, 3v1 or a duel, any seat human or computer
> - **A map editor** — paint a board and play it
> - **Touch support** — the whole game works with a finger
>
> The computer opponent plays by your rules: it issues the same commands from the same rulebook,
> and it is never handed extra money or a look through your fog.
>
> It is inspired by Advance Wars, and it is its own game — original art, original units, original
> commanders. Built in Godot; the source is on GitHub.
>
> **Play in the browser:** https://gridcommanders.com/
>
> Not affiliated with, or endorsed by, Nintendo or Intelligent Systems.

### For a Godot community

> **Grid Commanders — an Advance Wars-style tactics game in Godot 4.7, now playable on the web**
>
> The whole simulation is Node-free typed GDScript: the game state, the rules and the AI live in
> plain classes, every action is a command object that validates then applies and emits typed
> events, and the scenes only render and animate them. Combat luck runs off a seeded RNG, so the
> same seed and the same commands replay byte for byte — which is what makes the replay viewer and
> the headless balance simulator possible at all.
>
> The web build exports with threads off, so it needs no cross-origin-isolation headers and any
> static host serves it. Unit and terrain art comes out of a deterministic voxel pipeline rather
> than a drawing program.
>
> Play: https://gridcommanders.com/ — source: https://github.com/hanscanonico/grid_commanders
>
> Glad to talk about the sim/presentation split or the export if either is useful to anyone.
